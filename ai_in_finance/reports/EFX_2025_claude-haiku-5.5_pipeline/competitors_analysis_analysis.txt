# Equifax (EFX) Against TransUnion, Paychex, and Verisk: Fiscal 2022 to 2025

Equifax grew fastest in its 2023 to 2025 period of the four companies only in the sense that it accelerated from 2.8% in 2023 to 7.9% in 2024 and 6.9% in 2025. Its profitability did not keep pace. Its EBITDA margin was 30.2% in 2025, against 32.8% in 2022, and in 2025 TransUnion (TRU) overtook it at 31.8%. Equifax's gross margin of 56.4% in 2025 was the lowest of the four companies, and its ROIC of 8.5% trailed Verisk Analytics, Inc. (VRSK) and Paychex, Inc. (PAYX) by a wide margin while leading TransUnion. The company's EV/EBITDA multiple of 16.92 in 2025 sits between TransUnion's 14.31 and Verisk's 20.18, and well below Paychex's 24.22. The sections below take each metric in turn, starting with the weaker results.

## Revenue and Revenue Growth

Equifax revenue rose from 5,122 in 2022 to 5,265 in 2023, 5,681 in 2024, and 6,074 in 2025. Growth was 2.8% in 2023, 7.9% in 2024, and 6.9% in 2025. The 2023 slowdown was the weak point of the period, and the 2024 and 2025 rates were the strongest of any year in the table for Equifax. Cumulatively, revenue rose 18.6% from 2022 to 2025 (illustrative: 6,074 divided by 5,122, minus one).

Against peers, Equifax was the largest company by reported revenue in 2025, with 6,074 against Paychex's 5,572, TransUnion's 4,576, and Verisk's 3,073. Growth, however, lagged TransUnion in both years. TransUnion grew 9.2% in 2024 and 9.4% in 2025, while Equifax grew 7.9% and 6.9%. Equifax was ahead of Paychex, which grew 5.4% in 2024 and 5.6% in 2025 on the fiscal-year labels used here, and roughly level with Verisk, which grew 7.5% and 6.6%. Paychex's fiscal 2026 figure of 6,512 is a 16.9% increase over 2025. The resource does not identify the source of that jump, so it should not be read as a continuation of the prior 5% to 6% trend without the underlying filing.

The revenue picture is therefore one of acceleration after a soft 2023, with Equifax growing at a mid-to-high single-digit rate that is below TransUnion's pace in the last two years. The growth rate in 2025 slipped 1.0 point from 2024, a small deceleration, not a break in trend.

## Gross Margin

Equifax gross margin was 57.5% in 2022, 55.7% in 2023, 55.7% in 2024, and 56.4% in 2025. The 1.8 point decline in 2023 is the main negative in the series. Gross profit actually fell from 2,945 to 2,930 that year, even though revenue rose by 143. In other words, cost of revenue grew faster than sales in 2023. The resource does not state the cause of that increase in costs. Gross margin was flat in 2024 and recovered 0.7 point in 2025, which still left it 1.1 points below 2022.

Peers show higher and, in most cases, rising gross margins. TransUnion was at 62.7% in 2022 and fell to 60.4%, 60.0%, and 59.1% in the following years, so it too declined, though from a higher base. Verisk rose from 67.0% in 2022 to 67.3%, 68.7%, and 69.9%. Paychex rose from 71.0% in 2023 to 72.0%, 72.4%, and 74.3% in fiscal 2026. In 2022 the gap between Equifax and TransUnion was 5.2 points; by 2025 it was 2.7 points, mainly because TransUnion declined. The gap between Equifax and Verisk widened from 9.5 points in 2022 to 13.5 points in 2025. Equifax has the lowest gross margin of the four companies in every year reported, and the gap to the asset-light peers has grown.

## EBITDA Margin

Equifax EBITDA margin was 32.8% in 2022 and then 30.0% in 2023, 30.3% in 2024, and 30.2% in 2025. EBITDA fell 6.1% in 2023, from 1,681 to 1,579, while revenue grew. From 2023 to 2025 the margin held in a narrow band near 30%, so the 2023 compression was not recovered. Illustratively, EBITDA grew 9.1% from 2022 to 2025 while revenue grew 18.6%, so EBITDA has grown at roughly half the rate of revenue over the period.

Equifax led TransUnion on this measure in 2022, 2023, and 2024. In 2025 the order reversed: TransUnion posted 31.8% against Equifax's 30.2%. TransUnion's margin was far lower in 2023, at 17.4%, and it recovered to 28.8% in 2024 and 31.8% in 2025. The resource does not explain the 2023 dip at TransUnion. The 2025 result is the first year in which Equifax is not the margin leader among the two credit-data companies.

Paychex and Verisk operate at much higher margins. Paychex was 45.2% in 2023, 46.1% in 2024, 44.7% in 2025, and 46.4% in fiscal 2026. Verisk was 69.7% in 2022, then 53.1%, 57.6%, and 54.3%. Equifax's 30.2% in 2025 is roughly 14.5 points below Paychex's 44.7% and 24 points below Verisk's 54.3%. The comparison with the asset-light peers shows that Equifax carries a structurally lower margin, and the comparison with TransUnion shows that its margin lead has disappeared.

## FCF Conversion

Equifax reported FCF of 133 in 2022, 516 in 2023, 813 in 2024, and 1,134 in 2025. Reported FCF conversion rose from 0.19 to 0.95, 1.35, and 1.72 over the same years. The improvement is real in the FCF figures themselves: FCF rose more than eight-fold from 2022 to 2025. The 2025 conversion figure of 1.72 is the highest of the four companies in that year, against TransUnion's 1.45 (2024: 1.82), Verisk's 1.31, and Paychex's 1.03.

The conversion figures need a caveat. The reported FCF Conversion values do not reconcile to FCF divided by EBITDA using the figures in the same table. Illustratively, Equifax's FCF divided by its EBITDA was 0.08 in 2022 and 0.62 in 2025, not 0.19 and 1.72. The resource does not define the metric. Computed on the simpler FCF-to-EBITDA basis (illustrative), the 2025 ranking changes: Equifax at 0.62 sits below Paychex at 0.69 and Verisk at 0.71, and above TransUnion at 0.46. The direction of travel for Equifax is unchanged under either basis, with a clear improvement from 2022 to 2025. The ranking against peers depends on the definition, and the reported 1.72 should be verified against the filing before it is used to claim cash-flow leadership.

## ROIC

Equifax ROIC was 8.6% in 2022, 7.2% in 2023, 7.9% in 2024, and 8.5% in 2025. The 2023 decline was reversed within two years, but the 2025 level is still slightly below the 2022 level. On this measure Equifax is clearly ahead of TransUnion, which posted 4.3%, 1.1%, 5.4%, and 6.7% over the same years. TransUnion's 2023 ROIC of 1.1% is the lowest figure in the group. Equifax has therefore earned more on invested capital than TransUnion in every year shown.

Against Verisk and Paychex, Equifax is well behind. Verisk's ROIC was 21.1% in 2022, 27.2% in 2023, and 33.6% in 2024, before falling to 20.7% in 2025. Paychex's was 36.8% in 2023, 37.2% in 2024, 19.1% in 2025, and 23.7% in fiscal 2026. Both peers have higher returns, and both also show declines in their most recent reported years. The resource does not explain either decline. The Paychex and Verisk drops in 2025 mean the gap to Equifax narrowed in that year, though Equifax still trails by a wide margin.

For Equifax, the recent trend is modest improvement: ROIC rose from 7.2% to 8.5% between 2023 and 2025 as cash generation improved. The level, however, remains in single digits, which indicates that the company generates a lower return on its capital base than the asset-light peers do.

## EV/EBITDA

Equifax EV/EBITDA was 17.43 in 2022, 22.79 in 2023, 21.18 in 2024, and 16.92 in 2025. The multiple peaked in 2023, the year EBITDA fell, which means the market was paying more for each dollar of lower earnings. As EBITDA stabilised and the market multiple fell, the multiple returned below its 2022 level by 2025.

Relative valuation changed across the years. In 2022, Equifax (17.43) traded below Verisk (17.88) and above TransUnion (14.26). In 2023, Equifax (22.79) traded below TransUnion (27.41) and Verisk (26.0) and above Paychex (16.57). In 2024, Equifax (21.18) traded above TransUnion (18.77) and Paychex (17.55) and below Verisk (25.09). In 2025, Equifax (16.92) traded above TransUnion (14.31), below Verisk (20.18), and well below Paychex (24.22). Illustratively, the 2025 multiple is about 18% above TransUnion's, while Equifax's margin is about 1.6 points below TransUnion's and its growth about 2.5 points below.

On the 2025 multiple alone, Equifax does not look expensive against the group. It is cheaper than Verisk and Paychex, and at a premium to TransUnion. The premium to TransUnion is harder to justify on the operating figures in this resource, since TransUnion has the higher 2025 EBITDA margin and faster revenue growth. Equifax's advantages are its higher ROIC and its stronger cash conversion in the FCF figures. Illustratively, the 2025 enterprise value implied by the reported multiple is about 31,000 (16.92 times 1,834 of EBITDA), which is consistent with the reported EV/Sales of 5.11 on 6,074 of revenue. A multiple of 16.92 is not an extreme level for this group, but it is not a discount to the group either.

## Conclusion

Equifax's financial position in this resource is stable but not strengthening on profitability. Revenue has grown at a mid-to-high single-digit rate since the 2023 slowdown, and free cash flow has risen sharply from a low 2022 base. Those are real improvements. Against them stand the 2023 gross profit decline, the EBITDA margin that has held near 30% since 2023 without recovering its 2022 level of 32.8%, a gross margin that is the lowest of the four companies, and an EBITDA margin that TransUnion has overtaken in 2025. ROIC has recovered to 8.5% but remains in single digits and below the asset-light peers.

The 2025 EV/EBITDA of 16.92 is moderate relative to the group. It is below Verisk and Paychex, whose higher margins and returns justify higher multiples, and above TransUnion, which has better 2025 margins and growth. On these metrics, the premium to TransUnion is the valuation point most in need of support. The cash-flow improvement is the strongest argument for it, but that argument depends on which FCF Conversion definition is used, so it should be checked against the filing.

The view in this analysis would be wrong if Equifax's EBITDA margin returned to the low 30s with revenue growth at or above 7% in the next reported year, and if the reported FCF Conversion figure reconciled to a definition showing Equifax ahead of Verisk and Paychex. It would also be wrong if the 2023 gross margin decline turned out to be temporary and gross margin moved back above 57% in subsequent years. A failure to close the EBITDA margin gap with TransUnion, or a return of ROIC below 8%, would support the view that the current multiple is not supported by the operating record in this resource.
