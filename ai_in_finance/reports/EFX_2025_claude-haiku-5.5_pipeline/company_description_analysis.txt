**1. Company overview and industry**

Founded in 1913 (predecessor dating to 1899), Equifax Inc. is a global data, analytics and technology company that provides information solutions to businesses, governments and consumers, and human resources business process outsourcing to employers. Revenue was $6,074.5 million in 2025, up 7% from $5,681.1 million in 2024. The company reports three segments: Workforce Solutions (43% of revenue), U.S. Information Solutions (USIS, 34%) and International (23%).

**2. Core strengths and key products**

The Work Number, the employment and income data platform, held about 209 million active and 813 million total employment records at December 31, 2025, drawn from payroll data of over 4 million organizations. Workforce Solutions earned a 44.2% operating margin in 2025, the highest of the three segments. Other products include credit reporting and scoring, identity and fraud management, and financial marketing services. The filing states that Equifax believes no competitor offers quite the same mix of products and services, but it does not quantify any advantage.

**3. End markets, customers and market position**

The U.S. generated 77% of 2025 revenue; the company operates in 20 countries outside the U.S. Clients span mortgage, financial services, employers, government, automotive and telecommunications. The largest client provides about 3% of revenue. The filing does not name customers or describe their credit quality, and it does not disclose market share. Competitors are described only by category, such as in-house HR teams, payroll processors and global consumer credit reporting companies.

**4. Industry trends, opportunities and challenges**

Management expects 2026 U.S. mortgage credit activity to be slightly below 2025. Mortgage refinancing is sensitive to interest rates. Regulation is tightening: the CFPB holds examination authority over credit reporting agencies, and Chile's Law No. 21.719 becomes enforceable in December 2026. Verification services face low barriers to entry, and cost of services rose $126.9 million in 2025, mainly from royalty, revenue share and purchased data costs.

**5. Strategic initiatives and response**

The company is migrating to a public cloud platform and is using AI and machine learning to develop products. It acquired a Workforce Solutions business in 2025 for $74.1 million net of cash acquired. The Board authorized up to $3 billion of share repurchases in April 2025; Equifax bought 4,006,173 shares for $927.4 million in 2025, leaving about $2.1 billion available. The quarterly dividend rose to $0.50 per share from the second quarter of 2025.

---

**Summary (under 130 words)**

Equifax grew revenue 7% in 2025 to $6,074.5 million and diluted EPS to $5.32, but operating margin slipped to 18.0% from 18.3%, mainly because royalty, revenue share and purchased data costs rose. Workforce Solutions, led by The Work Number, carries the highest margin, while Employer Services declined 2%. Management expects lower 2026 U.S. mortgage activity. The company responded with a $3 billion buyback authorization, $927.4 million of repurchases in 2025, a higher dividend and a $74.1 million acquisition. The filing gives no market share data. The Asia Pacific goodwill cushion is thin, so a decline in that unit's cash flows or a rise in the discount rate could trigger an impairment, which would prove the margin concern more serious than it looks.
