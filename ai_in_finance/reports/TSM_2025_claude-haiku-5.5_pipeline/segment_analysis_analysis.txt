# Segment Analysis: TSMC, Fiscal 2025 vs. 2024

**Scope note.** The resources do not report segment-level revenue, cost, or profit. The 10-K text describes two revenue groups: wafer fabrication (about 86% of 2025 net revenue) and the remainder from packaging and testing, mask making, design, and royalty income. The analysis below uses these two groups. Net profit is disclosed only on a consolidated basis: net income attributable to shareholders of the parent was NT$1,697,604 million in 2025, up 46.5% from NT$1,158,380 million in 2024. Income tax expense rose 39.6% to NT$346,530 million, mainly from higher taxable income. The effective tax rate was about 17.0% in 2025 and 17.7% in 2024 (illustrative: tax expense divided by pretax income).

## Wafer Fabrication (about 86% of 2025 net revenue)

The NT dollar strengthened from NT$32.13 to NT$31.11 per US dollar, which reduced reported revenue. Even so, net revenue rose 31.6% to NT$3,809,054 million, as wafer shipments grew to 15,022 thousand 12-inch equivalents (2024: 12,910 thousand) and 7-nanometer-and-below nodes reached 74% of wafer revenue (2024: 69%). Gross margin reached 59.9% (56.1%), but R&D rose 20.7% to NT$246,427 million for 10-, 14-, and 16-angstrom work.

## Packaging, Testing, Mask Making, Design, and Royalty (about 14% of 2025 net revenue)

These services make up the remaining 14% of 2025 net revenue (illustratively about NT$533 billion). The filing gives no separate revenue, cost, or profit for this group, so its growth and margin cannot be measured. Advanced packaging appears only as a capital expenditure purpose, alongside Fab 23 and Fab 24 expansion.

## Items the Resources Do Not Cover

- **Strategic partnerships:** The filing names no customer or technology partner. The only named counterparties are Citibank, N.A. (trustee for TSMC Arizona notes) and TSMC Arizona itself.
- **Market share:** No market share figure is given. Market position is described only as "a leader of the foundry segment."
- **Policy effects:** Carbon fees start in 2026 for 2025 emissions, which the filing says will raise manufacturing costs. The R.O.C. statutory corporate rate is 20%, and eligible R&D and equipment spending may earn investment tax credits of 25% and 5%. The filing does not quantify either effect.
