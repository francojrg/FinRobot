**Company overview and industry**

Founded in 1987, Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company Limited is a dedicated semiconductor foundry that manufactures chips to customers' proprietary designs. It also offers design, mask making, TSMC 3DFabric advanced stacking and packaging, and testing services. The company describes scale and capacity, particularly for advanced technologies, as a major competitive advantage. Annual capacity exceeded 17 million 12-inch equivalent wafers in 2025, and shipments rose to 15.0 million from 12.9 million in 2024.

**Core strengths and products**

Advanced nodes are the main differentiator. Technologies at 7-nanometer and below made up 74% of 2025 wafer revenue, up from 69% in 2024. High Performance Computing (HPC) became the largest platform at 58% of 2025 net revenue, up from 51%. The company also reports that its 2-nanometer technology entered volume production in 2025.

**Market position, end markets and customers**

Customers are described as many of the world's leading semiconductor companies, from fabless companies to integrated device manufacturers. The filing does not name them and gives no market share figure. Revenue is classified by customer headquarters: North America 75% of 2025 net revenue, Asia Pacific 9%, China 9%, Japan 4% and EMEA 3%.

**Industry trends, opportunities and challenges**

Demand is driven by AI, high performance computing and 5G. Challenges include a stronger NT dollar, which reduced reported revenue (rate moved from NT$32.13 to NT$31.11 per US$), carbon fees starting in 2026 for 2025 emissions, power and water supply concerns, and rising utility prices. Capital expenditure guidance for 2026 is US$52–56 billion.

**Strategic initiatives**

Initiatives include 16-angstrom risk production expected in 2026, and N3A automotive technology released at the end of 2025. TSMC Arizona entered high-volume production at the end of 2024, with construction of a second facility under way. Under the U.S. CHIPS Act, TSMC Arizona has a direct funding agreement of up to US$6.6 billion. JASM in Japan began volume production in December 2024, and its second site began construction in October 2025. ESMC in Dresden is 70% owned by the company, with Bosch, Infineon and NXP holding 30%.

**Summary**

Revenue rose 31.6% to NT$3,809,054 million in 2025, and net income attributable to shareholders rose 46.5% to NT$1,697,604 million. Gross margin improved to 59.9% from 56.1%, mainly from higher utilization and cost improvement, partly offset by currency. The costs are rising: R&D grew 20.7%, and 2026 capital expenditure guidance of US$52–56 billion compares with NT$1,272,411 million (US$40.9 billion) spent in 2025. Illustratively, 2025 operating cash flow less capital expenditure was about NT$1,002,565 million. The view weakens if HPC concentration or the 2026 capex plan proves hard to fund from operating cash flow and bonds.
