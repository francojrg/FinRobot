The resources describe one company, TSMC, so the business lines below are its six reported revenue platforms, ordered from weakest to strongest. Figures are in NT$ millions unless noted. Growth rates marked "illustrative" are my own calculations from the platform table, not figures the filing reports.

**Digital Consumer Electronics:** Revenue was essentially flat at NT$47,997 million in 2025, compared with NT$47,961 million in 2024, and held at 1% of net revenue in both years (NT$47,000 million in 2023). The filing names smart DTVs, set-top boxes, AI-embedded smart cameras and PMICs as the applications served, but it does not explain the lack of growth.

**Smartphone:** Revenue rose 11% to NT$1,110,816 million in 2025, an increase of NT$105,686 million from NT$1,005,130 million, but its share of net revenue fell to 29% from 35% in 2024 and 38% in 2023. The share decline reflects growth well below the company's 31.6% total, as High Performance Computing grew faster and became a larger part of revenue.

**Internet of Things:** Revenue rose about 15% (illustrative) to NT$191,047 million from NT$165,516 million, yet its share stayed at 5% of net revenue, down from 6% in 2024 and 8% in 2023. The filing cites the N6e and N12e ultra-low-power technologies as having entered volume production, but it does not quantify how much of the 2025 increase they produced.

**Others:** Revenue rose about 34% (illustrative) to NT$79,596 million from NT$59,487 million, recovering from the 2024 decline from NT$69,482 million in 2023, and the share was 2% in 2025. The filing does not describe the drivers of this category, which covers revenue outside the five named platforms.

**Automotive:** Revenue rose about 34% (illustrative) to NT$186,667 million from NT$139,323 million, with the share unchanged at 5%. The filing notes that the N3A 3-nanometer automotive technology was released to customers at the end of 2025 and that the 16-nanometer MRAM and 22ULL RRAM technologies passed Automotive Grade-1 requirements in 2025, but it does not attribute the revenue increase to these specifically.

**High Performance Computing:** Revenue rose 48% to NT$2,192,931 million in 2025, an increase of NT$716,040 million, and its share of net revenue climbed to 58% from 51% in 2024 and 43% in 2023. This platform accounted for roughly 78% of the NT$914,746 million total revenue increase (illustrative), and the filing names it the key growth driver, linking it to AI accelerators, server processors and advanced packaging such as CoWoS and SoIC. The 2024 growth rate was 58%, so the platform's growth rate slowed in 2025 even as its absolute increase was the largest of any platform.
