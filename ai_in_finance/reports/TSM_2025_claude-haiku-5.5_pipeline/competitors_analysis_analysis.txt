# Financial Metric Analysis: TSM Against UMC, ASX and GFS, Fiscal Years 2022 to 2025

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company Limited (TSM) grew revenue by 68.2% between fiscal 2022 and fiscal 2025, from 2,263,891 to 3,809,054, while its three peers, UMC (United Microelectronics Corporation), ASE Technology Holding Co., Ltd. (ASX) and GLOBALFOUNDRIES Inc. (GFS), all reported lower revenue in 2025 than in 2022. Its gross margin, EBITDA margin and ROIC were all at or near their highest levels in the period by 2025. The valuation has moved in the same direction: TSM's EV/EBITDA rose from 7.15 to 17.34, the highest multiple in the peer set in 2025. The resource provides figures and ratios but not the causes behind them, so the explanations below are limited to what the numbers show. The table does not state currency or units, so all amounts are used as given.

## Revenue and Revenue Growth

TSM's revenue weakened before it strengthened. Revenue fell 4.5% in fiscal 2023, from 2,263,891 to 2,161,736, and then rose 33.9% in fiscal 2024 to 2,894,308 and 31.6% in fiscal 2025 to 3,809,054. The 2025 figure is 76.2% above the 2023 trough, which is the more relevant comparison because the decline in 2023 is the starting point of the recovery.

The peers followed different paths. UMC's revenue fell 20.2% in 2023, the sharpest drop among the four companies, and then grew only 4.4% in 2024 and 2.3% in 2025. ASX fell 13.3% in 2023, grew 2.3% in 2024 and 8.4% in 2025. GFS fell 8.8% in 2023 and 8.7% in 2024, and was nearly flat at 0.6% growth in 2025. Over the full period, UMC's 2025 revenue was 14.8% below its 2022 level, ASX's was 3.8% below, and GFS's was 16.2% below. TSM is the only company in the group whose revenue in 2025 exceeded its 2022 level by a wide margin.

The growth gap is the largest single difference in the data. In 2025, TSM grew 31.6% while the next fastest peer, ASX, grew 8.4%. The table does not disclose the drivers of TSM's growth, so the analysis cannot attribute it to a product, customer or geography. What the data does show is that the growth was broad enough to lift absolute gross profit from 1,348,355 in 2022 to 2,281,294 in 2025, and EBITDA from 1,593,076 to 2,742,122.

## Gross Margin

TSM's gross margin fell from 59.6% in 2022 to 54.4% in 2023, the low point of the period, and then recovered to 56.1% in 2024 and 59.9% in 2025. The 2025 figure is the highest in the four-year series, 0.3 percentage points above 2022.

The peers' margins moved differently. UMC's margin declined in every year: 45.1%, 34.9%, 32.6% and 29.0%. Its 2025 margin is 30.9 percentage points below TSM's. ASX's margin fell to 15.8% in 2023 and recovered to 17.7% in 2025, still 42.2 points below TSM. GFS's margin was 27.6%, 28.4%, 24.5% and 24.9%, and sits 35.0 points below TSM in 2025.

TSM's margin in 2023 is the one year where it lagged its own history, and the decline was roughly 5.2 points. Each peer had a margin that was lower than TSM's in every year, so the gap has persisted across the cycle. The more notable pattern is that UMC, which started at a margin of 45.1% in 2022, lost 16.1 points by 2025, while TSM gained slightly over the same period.

## EBITDA Margin

TSM's EBITDA margin was 70.4% in 2022, 70.5% in 2023, 71.8% in 2024 and 72.0% in 2025. The series is stable, and the change in 2023 was small compared with the 5.2-point gross margin dip that year. This suggests that the 2023 gross margin decline was absorbed by costs below gross profit or by the difference between gross profit and EBITDA; the table does not provide the operating expense detail needed to confirm that.

In 2025, TSM's EBITDA margin of 72.0% compares with 45.8% for UMC, 33.5% for GFS and 19.6% for ASX. TSM's margin is 26.2 points above UMC, the next highest. Each peer's margin declined over the period. UMC lost 10.0 points between 2022 and 2025, GFS lost 6.5 points (after a low of 22.3% in 2024), and ASX lost 1.4 points. This divergence is the clearest evidence in the data that TSM's profitability is not a function of the cycle that affected its peers.

A cautionary point: the EBITDA margin for GFS was volatile, moving from 40.0% in 2022 to 22.3% in 2024 and back to 33.5% in 2025. Any single-year comparison with GFS should be read against that range.

## ROIC

TSM's ROIC was 26.7% in 2022, 19.8% in 2023, 22.2% in 2024 and 26.7% in 2025. The 2025 level equals the 2022 level, so the company has fully recovered from the 2023 trough over the period.

UMC is the only peer whose ROIC approached TSM's in the starting year, at 25.3% in 2022. Its ROIC then fell in every year to 15.3%, 11.7% and 9.9%. ASX moved from 14.2% to 9.2%, 7.6% and 7.9%. GFS went from 12.3% to 8.3%, then to negative 0.4% in 2024, and recovered to 7.2% in 2025.

The 2025 gap is large. TSM's 26.7% is roughly 2.7 times UMC's 9.9%, 3.4 times ASX's 7.9%, and 3.7 times GFS's 7.2%. The data shows that the gap widened over the period: in 2022 TSM led UMC by 1.4 points, and in 2025 it led by 16.8 points. The table does not show invested capital or the calculation method, so the reason behind this divergence cannot be determined from the resource. One consequence worth noting is that the ROIC and EBITDA margin trends point the same way for TSM, with both recovering after 2023, which suggests that the return on capital reflects operating performance rather than a change in the capital base alone. That is an inference from two series, not a finding from a capital schedule.

## FCF Conversion

TSM's FCF conversion was 0.52 in 2022, 0.34 in 2023, 0.74 in 2024 and 0.58 in 2025. The absolute free cash flow rose from 520,973 to 286,569 in 2023, then to 861,196 in 2024 and 992,378 in 2025. In 2025, free cash flow grew 15.2% over 2024, yet the conversion ratio fell from 0.74 to 0.58. This means the denominator in the ratio grew faster than free cash flow. The table does not define the denominator.

The resource's FCF conversion figures cannot be reproduced from the other data in the table. As an illustration, dividing TSM's free cash flow by its EBITDA gives 0.33 for 2022 and 0.36 for 2025, which does not match the reported 0.52 and 0.58. The same mismatch appears for UMC, where 2025 free cash flow of 49,131 against EBITDA of 108,870 gives about 0.45, versus the reported 1.22. Because the definition is not stated, the ratios can be compared across years for each company, but cross-company comparisons of this metric carry uncertainty.

On the reported figures, TSM's 2025 conversion of 0.58 trails UMC's 1.22 and GFS's 1.14, and sits above ASX's negative 0.59. ASX's free cash flow was negative in 2025 at negative 23,599, after positive figures in 2022 through 2024, so its conversion is the weakest in the group. UMC's conversion was negative in 2023 and near zero in 2024, so its 2025 figure reflects a recovery from a low base. TSM's free cash flow was positive in each year and rose in every year after 2023, which is the most consistent cash generation pattern in the group, even though its conversion ratio was not the highest in 2025.

## EV/EBITDA

The valuation trend is the most important point for the investment question. TSM's EV/EBITDA rose from 7.15 in 2022 to 10.53 in 2023, 15.62 in 2024 and 17.34 in 2025. The multiple more than doubled while the EBITDA margin rose by only 1.6 points, and EBITDA grew from 1,593,076 to 2,742,122, an increase of 72.1%. The multiple therefore expanded faster than the earnings base.

Year by year, TSM's position against the peers was as follows. In 2022, TSM at 7.15 sat above UMC (2.38) and ASX (3.89) but below GFS (9.25). In 2023, TSM at 10.53 ranked second behind GFS at 12.86, with UMC at 5.25 and ASX at 6.92 well below. In 2024, TSM at 15.62 was close to GFS at 15.86 and far above UMC at 4.65 and ASX at 7.83. In 2025, TSM at 17.34 was the highest multiple in the group. GFS had de-rated to 8.49, ASX was at 10.15 and UMC at 5.19.

Compared with the peer set's 2025 multiples, TSM's 17.34 is about twice the median of the three peers (8.49). As an illustrative calculation, a company valued at the peer median multiple would have an enterprise value about half of TSM's current level, all else equal. The peers' multiples are not evenly comparable: UMC and ASX have lower margins and lower ROIC, and GFS's 2025 EBITDA margin of 33.5% sits between them, so a lower multiple for these businesses is consistent with their weaker margin and return data. TSM's position is different on every operating metric in the table, so the premium is at least partly explained by the operating profile. The data alone cannot establish how much of the premium is warranted.

On price-to-earnings and price-to-book, TSM's 2025 figures (29.01 and 9.2) are also above the peers' (15.26 for UMC, 27.75 for ASX, 21.96 for GFS, and 3.25 and 1.63 on price-to-book for ASX and GFS, with UMC at 1.68). The premium is present across the valuation measures in the resource.

## Conclusion

TSM's financial health, as measured by these metrics, is strong and improving. Its 2023 weakness was real: revenue fell 4.5%, gross margin dropped to 54.4%, ROIC fell to 19.8% and FCF conversion dropped to 0.34. By 2025 each of those measures had recovered, and the company's revenue, gross margin, EBITDA margin and ROIC were all at or near their highest levels in the four-year series. Its peers did not show the same recovery. UMC's margins and ROIC declined in every year, ASX's free cash flow turned negative in 2025, and GFS's revenue fell in both 2023 and 2024 and was flat in 2025.

The valuation is harder to reconcile with this record. The operating data supports a premium to the peers, since TSM leads the group on revenue growth, gross margin, EBITDA margin and ROIC. The EV/EBITDA multiple, however, rose from 7.15 to 17.34 over the period, and the rise in the multiple was much larger than the change in the operating metrics. EBITDA grew 72.1% while the multiple grew 142%, and the EBITDA margin improved by 1.6 points. Most of the valuation change therefore reflects a higher price for each unit of the same earnings stream, not a large shift in profitability. The current multiple implicitly assumes that the growth of 31.6% in 2025 can be sustained or that the margin and return profile will persist at a level that the peers have not reached.

Several observable outcomes would challenge this view. If TSM's revenue growth falls well below the 2025 rate in the coming periods while the multiple remains above 15, the valuation would be harder to justify on the data in this resource. A gross margin that falls back toward the 2024 level of 56.1% would weaken the case that the 2025 margin is durable. An EBITDA margin that drops below 70% would also undermine the profitability argument, given that the margin has been above 70% in every year of the table. Finally, a ROIC that returns toward the 2023 level of 19.8% would suggest that the 2025 return was not a stable base. The resource does not include forward estimates, cost structure, capital expenditure or the drivers of the revenue growth, so these tests can be applied only as the company reports further results.
