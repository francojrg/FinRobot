# NVIDIA (NVDA) Financial Metrics Analysis Against AMD, Broadcom and Intel

NVIDIA's reported results across the four fiscal years in the data show revenue rising from 26,974 in 2023 to 215,938 in 2026, with gross margin and EBITDA margin both far above the three competitors. The most important concern in the data is that revenue growth slowed from 125.9% in 2024 to 65.5% in 2026 and gross margin fell from 75.0% in 2025 to 71.1% in 2026. The data sets are not fully aligned: NVIDIA's labels run from 2023 to 2026, while the competitors' run from 2022 to 2025, so the peer comparisons below cover 2023, 2024 and 2025 only. The fiscal year ends of the companies can differ, and I use the labels exactly as the data gives them. No competitor figures exist for NVIDIA's 2026 label.

## Revenue and Revenue Growth

NVIDIA's revenue was 26,974 in 2023, 60,922 in 2024, 130,497 in 2025 and 215,938 in 2026. Growth was 125.9% in 2024, 114.2% in 2025 and 65.5% in 2026. The percentage rate has decelerated in each year, but the absolute dollar increase has grown: revenue rose by about 33,948 in 2024, 69,575 in 2025 and 85,441 in 2026. The 2026 growth figure is the first clear sign of a slowdown in the data, and the resources do not explain its cause.

Against peers, NVIDIA's 2025 revenue of 130,497 compares with AMD's 34,639 (growth of 34.3%), Broadcom's 63,887 (growth of 23.9%) and Intel's 52,853 (growth of −0.5%). Illustratively, NVIDIA's 2025 revenue equals about 86% of the three competitors' combined 2025 revenue of 151,379. Broadcom's growth was 44.0% in 2024 and 23.9% in 2025, so its growth also decelerated. AMD accelerated from 13.7% in 2024 to 34.3% in 2025. Intel's revenue fell in each year from 63,054 in 2022 to 52,853 in 2025, with declines of 14.0% in 2023, 2.1% in 2024 and 0.5% in 2025.

## Gross Margin

NVIDIA's gross margin rose from 56.9% in 2023 to 72.7% in 2024 and 75.0% in 2025, then fell to 71.1% in 2026. The 2026 decline is the first reversal in the series and is the most significant negative change in the NVIDIA data. Gross profit still grew from 97,858 to 153,463 (about 56.8%), so the margin decline did not reduce profit dollars in 2026. The resources do not state why the margin declined.

In each overlapping year NVIDIA's gross margin was well above the peers. In 2023 it was 56.9% against Broadcom's 68.9%, AMD's 46.1% and Intel's 40.0%. Broadcom was the only peer above NVIDIA that year. In 2024 NVIDIA's 72.7% was above Broadcom's 63.0%, AMD's 49.4% and Intel's 32.7%. In 2025 NVIDIA's 75.0% was above Broadcom's 67.8%, AMD's 49.5% and Intel's 34.8%. So NVIDIA moved from a position below Broadcom to a position above it within two years. Broadcom's margin dipped to 63.0% in 2024 before recovering to 67.8%. AMD improved steadily, from 44.9% in 2022 to 49.5% in 2025. Intel's margin fell in every year, from 42.6% in 2022 to 32.7% in 2024, with a small recovery to 34.8% in 2025.

## EBITDA Margin

NVIDIA's EBITDA margin rose from 22.2% in 2023 to 58.4% in 2024, 66.0% in 2025 and 66.9% in 2026. The 2026 change was small, a rise of 0.9 percentage points, and it occurred while gross margin fell by 3.9 points. Illustratively, the gap between gross margin and EBITDA margin narrowed from about 9.0 points in 2025 (75.0% minus 66.0%) to about 4.2 points in 2026 (71.1% minus 66.9%). This means operating costs, net of the depreciation and amortization added back in EBITDA, fell as a share of revenue. The data does not show which costs drove this change.

Against peers, NVIDIA's 2023 EBITDA margin of 22.2% was below Broadcom's 57.4%, AMD's 17.9% was lower, and Intel's 20.7% was lower. NVIDIA was above AMD and Intel that year, and below Broadcom. In 2024 NVIDIA's 58.4% was above Broadcom's 46.3%, AMD's 20.0% and Intel's 2.3%. In 2025 NVIDIA's 66.0% was above Broadcom's 54.3%, AMD's 21.0% and Intel's 27.2%. The most recent comparison is therefore that NVIDIA's EBITDA margin is roughly 12 points above Broadcom's, and about three times AMD's or Intel's. Broadcom's margin also fell sharply in 2024 (from 57.4% to 46.3%) before recovering in 2025, so its margin is not steady either. Intel's margin fell to 2.3% in 2024 before rising to 27.2% in 2025.

## EV/EBITDA

NVIDIA's EV/EBITDA fell from 81.93 in 2023 to 42.71 in 2024, 34.14 in 2025 and 32.14 in 2026. The decline reflects EBITDA growing faster than the enterprise value implied by the multiple. Illustratively, the multiple applied to EBITDA gives an implied enterprise value of roughly 490,000 in 2023 (81.93 times 5,986), roughly 2,941,000 in 2025 (34.14 times 86,137) and roughly 4,646,000 in 2026 (32.14 times 144,552), in the table's units. Over 2023 to 2025, EBITDA rose about 14.4 times while the implied enterprise value rose about six times, which is why the multiple compressed. These are illustrative calculations, since the resources do not give enterprise value directly.

In 2023 NVIDIA's multiple of 81.93 was above AMD's 58.57, Broadcom's 18.17 and Intel's 22.65. In 2024 it was above Broadcom's 35.75 and Intel's 106.88 was higher, while AMD's 37.77 was close to NVIDIA's 42.71 and NVIDIA was above AMD. In 2025 NVIDIA's 34.14 was below AMD's 47.75 and Broadcom's 51.89, and above Intel's 15.09. Intel's 2024 multiple of 106.88 reflects an EBITDA of only 1,203 in that year; the high figure mainly reflects low denominator earnings, not a premium placed on Intel. On the 2025 price-to-sales measure, NVIDIA's EV/Sales of 22.53 is below Broadcom's 28.2, well above AMD's 10.03 and Intel's 4.1. In 2025 NVIDIA's P/E of 40.84 and P/B of 37.05 were below AMD's P/E of 80.82 and Broadcom's P/E of 77.49, but its P/B was above Broadcom's 21.56 and AMD's 5.54. The data does not say whether NVIDIA is overvalued or undervalued in absolute terms. Relative to these peers in 2025, its EV/EBITDA is in the middle of the group.

## FCF Conversion

NVIDIA's reported FCF conversion was 0.87 in 2023, 0.91 in 2024, 0.83 in 2025 and 0.81 in 2026. It stayed close to 1.0 across the period and fell modestly in the last two years. The stated figures are not reproducible from the table alone. Dividing free cash flow by EBITDA gives about 0.64 in 2023, 0.76 in 2024, 0.71 in 2025 and 0.67 in 2026. The resources do not define FCF conversion, so the reported figures are reported as given and the difference should be resolved with the source definition before comparing precisely.

Against peers, AMD's reported conversion rose from 2.36 in 2022 to 1.31 in 2023, 1.47 in 2024 and 1.55 in 2025. Broadcom's was 1.25 in 2023, 3.29 in 2024 and 1.16 in 2025. The 2024 figure for Broadcom is an outlier, as its free cash flow of 19,414 against EBITDA of 23,879 gives a ratio of about 0.81 on the table's figures. Intel's conversion was negative in 2022 (−1.17) and 2023 (−8.45), and not meaningful in 2024 and 2025, because free cash flow was negative in every year. On the reported measure NVIDIA's conversion is lower than AMD's and Broadcom's in most years, but it is far above Intel's. The reported figures for AMD and Broadcom sit above 1.0 while NVIDIA's sits below 1.0, so the comparison depends heavily on the definition.

NVIDIA's free cash flow rose from 3,808 in 2023 to 27,021 in 2024, 60,853 in 2025 and 96,676 in 2026. In the most recent overlapping year, NVIDIA's 2025 free cash flow of 60,853 was about 2.3 times Broadcom's 26,914, and about 9 times AMD's 6,735. Intel's free cash flow was −4,949 in 2025.

## ROIC

NVIDIA's ROIC was 10.0% in 2023, 56.9% in 2024, 83.3% in 2025 and 72.6% in 2026. The series rose sharply in the first two years and peaked in 2025. The 2026 value fell by 10.7 points, which is the first decline. The level is high by any comparison in the data and it indicates a very high return on the capital base in the resource, though the resources do not show the capital base itself.

Against peers in 2023, NVIDIA's ROIC of 10.0% was below Broadcom's 24.7% and above AMD's 0.8% and Intel's 0.8%. In 2024, NVIDIA's 56.9% was far above Broadcom's 6.4%, AMD's 2.8% and Intel's −5.1%. In 2025, NVIDIA's 83.3% compared with Broadcom's 13.2%, AMD's 4.8% and Intel's 1.2%. Broadcom's ROIC dropped from 24.7% in 2023 to 6.4% in 2024 before recovering to 13.2%, so it fell back sharply in the year NVIDIA's ROIC began to rise. The competitor figures remain low, which means NVIDIA's advantage in this metric is large in the data. The more important question for the decline in 2026 is whether the return on the growing capital base will continue to rise or stabilize at a lower level.

## Conclusion

NVIDIA's financial record in these resources is one of rapid expansion. Revenue increased more than eightfold from 2023 to 2026, gross margin reached 75.0% in 2025 before slipping to 71.1% in 2026, and EBITDA margin reached 66.9% in 2026. Free cash flow grew from 3,808 to 96,676 across the same period, and ROIC reached 83.3% in 2025. Against AMD, Broadcom and Intel in the overlapping years, NVIDIA led on gross margin in 2024 and 2025, on EBITDA margin in every year from 2024 onward, and on ROIC in 2024 and 2025. Its weaker points are a slowing growth rate, a decline in gross margin in 2026, and a reported FCF conversion below the figures of AMD and Broadcom. The data does not give the causes of the 2026 margin decline or the growth slowdown, so those causes cannot be assessed from these resources.

On valuation, the EV/EBITDA multiple fell from 81.93 in 2023 to 32.14 in 2026. This decline is consistent with earnings growing faster than the valuation applied to them. The current multiple of 32.14 is below the 2025 peer levels of AMD (47.75) and Broadcom (51.89), but the data does not establish whether it is a fair price. The financial record justifies a premium to Intel, whose EBITDA margin was 27.2% in 2025 and whose free cash flow was negative. Whether the record justifies the current level depends on whether revenue growth and margins hold up after 2026. A reversal of the margin trend or a continued fall in growth would be the clearest signal that the record is not sustaining the multiple. Specifically, the view would be wrong if gross margin continues to fall below 70% or if revenue growth falls toward the single digits while the EV/EBITDA multiple stays near 32. The view would also be contradicted if ROIC falls back sharply from its 2025 peak of 83.3% toward the low double digits seen at Broadcom.
