# Bloom Energy Corporation (BE): Financial Metrics Against FuelCell Energy, Plug Power and ERock, 2022 to 2025

BE's reported economics improved sharply over 2022 to 2025. Revenue reached 2,024 in 2025, up 37.3% from 1,474 in 2024, and gross margin rose from 12.4% in 2022 to 29.0% in 2025. EBITDA turned positive in 2024 (89, or 6.1% of revenue) but slipped to 20 in 2025 (1.0%). Its EV/EBITDA multiple of 1,240.45 in 2025 reflects that near-zero EBITDA and cannot be read as a valuation signal. The data set has three limits. The table does not state currency or units, so figures are used as given. The fiscal year ends of the four companies can differ and the data does not specify them. ERock has only two years of data, and FCF Conversion and EV/EBITDA are marked "n/m" for almost every company and year, which leaves the valuation comparison to EV/Sales and P/B.

## Revenue and Revenue Growth

BE's revenue rose from 1,199 in 2022 to 1,333 in 2023 (growth of 11.2%) and to 1,474 in 2024 (growth of 10.5%). Growth then accelerated to 37.3% in 2025. Over the four years, the company grew steadily, but 2022 to 2024 growth was modest in percentage terms. The 2025 step-up is the most significant change in the series.

Against peers, BE's growth was ahead of FuelCell Energy (FCEL) in 2023 and 2024, when FCEL's revenue fell 5.4% and 9.1%. Plug Power (PLUG) grew 27.1% in 2023 but then fell 29.5% in 2024, so BE's 10.5% was the more stable path in that year. In 2025, BE's 37.3% trailed FCEL's 41.0% and ERock's 42.5%, and it beat PLUG's 12.9%. In absolute terms BE is far larger than the others. Its 2025 revenue of 2,024 compares with 710 at PLUG, 183 at ERock and 158 at FCEL. Scale is a real difference, but the table does not show whether scale has brought any cost advantage beyond what the margin data shows.

## Gross Margin

BE's gross margin was 12.4% in 2022, 14.8% in 2023, 27.5% in 2024 and 29.0% in 2025. The 2024 jump of about 12.7 percentage points is the largest single change in the series. Gross profit went from 198 to 405 in that year while revenue grew only 10.5%, so unit cost fell faster than price or revenue rose. The data does not say what caused this, so the cause should be confirmed in the 10-K.

In each year, BE was the only company with positive gross profit. FCEL's gross margin was negative in all four years (-22.7%, -8.5%, -32.0% and -16.7%). PLUG's was deeply negative (-27.7%, -57.0%, -99.4% and -34.1%). ERock was positive but lower: 13.4% in 2024 and 20.7% in 2025. BE's 2025 margin of 29.0% exceeded ERock's by about 8 points. The gap in 2025 is that BE earns a positive gross profit on a large base, while the others either lose money on each sale or operate at much smaller scale.

## EBITDA Margin

BE's EBITDA margin was -16.6% in 2022, -10.1% in 2023, 6.1% in 2024 and 1.0% in 2025. The company crossed into positive territory in 2024, and that was the year of its strongest gross margin gain. In 2025, however, EBITDA fell from 89 to 20 while gross profit rose from 405 to 587. The gross profit increase of 182 and the EBITDA decline of 69 together imply that costs below gross profit rose by about 251 (illustrative calculation: 182 plus 69). The data does not identify those costs, so the source of the 2025 decline cannot be determined from the resources provided.

In every year, BE's EBITDA margin was better than the peers'. In 2022 it was -16.6% against FCEL's -91.0% and PLUG's -90.2%. In 2023 it was -10.1% against -60.7% and -141.4%. In 2024 it was 6.1% against FCEL's -98.9%, PLUG's -314.3% and ERock's -28.9%. In 2025 it was 1.0% against FCEL's -88.8%, PLUG's -223.1% and ERock's -14.8%. The 2025 comparison is the one that matters most. BE's margin is barely positive, but the peers are still losing heavily at the EBITDA line, and BE's is the only one at or above zero.

## Return on Invested Capital

BE's ROIC was -25.7% in 2022, -10.9% in 2023, 1.6% in 2024 and -0.7% in 2025. The 2024 reading was the first positive value in the series, and the 2025 reading went negative again, so the improvement has not been sustained. The 2022 value is the worst of the four companies in that year. PLUG recorded -11.1% and FCEL -13.3%.

In 2023 BE's -10.9% was better than FCEL's -12.3% and PLUG's -38.2%. In 2024 BE's 1.6% was the best in the group, ahead of FCEL's -18.3%, PLUG's -83.5% and ERock's -191.7%. In 2025 BE's -0.7% was well ahead of FCEL's -22.8% and PLUG's -96.8%, but ERock's 50.9% was the highest value in the group. ERock's 2025 figure rests on a small invested capital base and a single year of improvement, so it should be read with caution. The data does not include a cost of capital, so the table cannot show whether BE's returns exceed it.

## Free Cash Flow Conversion

The FCF Conversion metric is marked "n/m" for all four companies in every year, so the table gives no direct reading. The underlying figures show the issue. BE's free cash flow was -309 in 2022, -456 in 2023, 33 in 2024 and 57 in 2025. As an illustrative calculation only, free cash flow divided by EBITDA would be about 37% in 2024 (33 divided by 89) and about 285% in 2025 (57 divided by 20). Those ratios are distorted by the small EBITDA base and should not be read as a measure of cash efficiency. The more reliable sign is that BE moved from heavy cash consumption (-456 in 2023) to small positive free cash flow in 2024 and 2025.

The peers did not generate positive free cash flow in any year shown except ERock in 2025 (112, against -33 in 2024). FCEL's free cash flow was negative in all four years (-159, -233, -212 and -148). PLUG's was -1,292, -1,803, -1,063 and -661. On this measure, BE was the only company to reach positive free cash flow for two consecutive years in the series.

## EV/EBITDA and Valuation

BE's EV/EBITDA was "n/m" in 2022 and 2023, 65.11 in 2024 and 1,240.45 in 2025. The peers are all "n/m" in every year, so no peer comparison is possible on this metric. The 2025 figure is not a useful valuation signal. It is the result of an EBITDA denominator of 20 in a year when EBITDA margin was 1.0%. Small changes in EBITDA would move the ratio by large amounts.

EV/Sales gives a usable comparison. BE's EV/Sales was 3.84 in 2022, 3.09 in 2023, 3.95 in 2024 and 12.29 in 2025. FCEL's was 6.99, 3.08, 1.93 and 1.76. PLUG's was 10.71, 4.00, 4.48 and 4.75. ERock's is "n/a" in both years. In 2022 BE's multiple was below FCEL's and PLUG's. In 2023 and 2024 it sat between them. In 2025 it was far above both. As an illustrative calculation, the 2025 EV/Sales of 12.29 multiplied by revenue of 2,024 implies an enterprise value of about 24,900 in the table's units, which is consistent with the EV/EBITDA figure (1,240.45 multiplied by 20 is about 24,800). Over the same period, the implied enterprise value at the 2024 EV/Sales of 3.95 was about 5,800 (illustrative). The enterprise value therefore rose about fourfold in one year while revenue rose 37.3%. The table does not show what drove that re-rating.

P/B tells the same story. BE's P/B was 11.54, 6.62, 9.05 and 31.66 in the four years. FCEL's ranged from 0.29 to 1.70, and PLUG's from 0.94 to 2.81. BE has traded at a substantial premium to both peers in every year, and the premium widened sharply in 2025.

## Conclusion

BE's operating results improved on every measure that the peers also report, and the improvement is real in the data. Revenue grew from 1,199 to 2,024 over the period, gross margin rose from 12.4% to 29.0%, EBITDA moved from -199 to 20, and free cash flow moved from -456 in 2023 to positive values in 2024 and 2025. Against FCEL and PLUG, BE has the best gross margin, EBITDA margin and free cash flow record in every year. Those facts support a premium over the peers' valuations.

The same data does not support a valuation of the size the table implies. The 2025 EV/EBITDA of 1,240.45 rests on EBITDA of 20, which fell 78% from 89 in 2024 even as gross profit rose 45%. ROIC was -0.7% in 2025 after a single positive year in 2024. Illustratively, 2025 free cash flow of 57 against an implied enterprise value of about 24,900 is a yield of roughly 0.2%. The 37.3% revenue growth in 2025 and the 29.0% gross margin explain why investors pay a premium to sales, but the table does not show that current earnings or returns on capital cover the enterprise value.

The view that BE's valuation is justified would be proven wrong if EBITDA margin does not hold above zero in the next reported year, if the 2025 cost increases below gross profit prove to be recurring, or if ROIC does not stay positive. Conversely, a further year of growth near 2025's rate with EBITDA margin returning toward or above its 2024 level would support the current enterprise value on a forward basis. The resources do not contain forward estimates, so that test cannot be run with the data provided.
