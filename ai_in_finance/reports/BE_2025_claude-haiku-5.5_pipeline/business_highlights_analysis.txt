The filing describes one company, Bloom Energy, so the business lines below are its four reported revenue lines in the 2025 versus 2024 comparison. Total revenue rose 37.3% to $2,024.0 million, and total gross margin rose to 29% from 27%.

**Installation:** Revenue grew 66.8% to $204.1 million (2024: $122.3 million), and the gross loss narrowed to $1.9 million from $7.1 million, with the margin improving to (1)% from (6)%. The increase came from the timing of key project milestones on sites that required installation services to meet customers' time-to-power needs, and cost of installation revenue rose 59.1% to $205.9 million.

**Product:** Revenue rose 41.1% to $1,531.3 million (2024: $1,085.2 million), and gross profit rose $139.1 million to $538.4 million, but the product gross margin fell to 35% from 37%. Demand tied to time-to-power needs and a major hyperscaler project through the Brookfield joint venture drove the growth, while margin was held back by $21.9 million of inventory reserves and impairments on Electrolyzer assets, a $12.7 million impairment on construction-in-progress, and a $15.9 million reduction to product revenue from share-based consideration payable to a key hyperscaler customer.

**Service:** Revenue increased 6.9% to $228.3 million (2024: $213.5 million), and gross profit swung to $22.9 million from a loss of $1.4 million, a 10% margin compared with (1)% in 2024. Lower field replacement unit deployment saved $29.4 million and maintenance contract revenue rose $18.6 million, but repair and overhaul costs rose $11.1 million as the aging fleet required more service, maintenance material costs rose $6.4 million, and product performance guarantee costs rose $4.2 million, which management attributed to fleet degradation.

**Electricity:** Revenue rose 14.2% to $60.4 million (2024: $52.8 million), and gross profit nearly doubled to $27.9 million from $13.9 million, lifting the margin to 46% from 26%. The increase was predominantly due to a one-time settlement of a customer contract after redeploying assets for a partner, partly offset by lower straight-line electricity revenue from repowering certain Managed Services sites, so the margin gain should not be read as recurring.
