Bloom's liquidity improved sharply at December 31, 2025, but leverage rose. Current assets reached $3,730.6 million, up from $2,044.1 million, lifting the current ratio to 5.98x from 3.21x, mainly because cash rose to $2,454.1 million (2024: $802.9 million) after the $2,500.0 million 0% convertible notes issued in November 2025. Total debt nearly doubled to $2,992.0 million from $1,529.8 million, and debt to stockholders' equity rose to 3.89x from 2.72x, even though equity grew to $768.6 million from $562.5 million. Net debt fell to $163.8 million from $326.0 million. Current deferred revenue dropped to $101.0 million from $243.3 million, while inventory rose to $643.3 million from $544.7 million, so cash generation now depends on deliveries. Long-term debt of $2,613.7 million makes refinancing and convertible dilution the main risks. This view would be wrong if inventory keeps outpacing revenue or the convertible notes are settled in shares.
