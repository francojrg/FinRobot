**Company Profile**

The provided resources do not state Bloom Energy's founding year. Bloom Energy is a global onsite power company, headquartered in San Jose (Silicon Valley), that designs, manufactures, distributes and operates the Bloom Energy Server, a high-temperature solid oxide fuel cell that converts natural gas, biogas or hydrogen into electricity without combustion or moving parts. It also sells the Bloom Electrolyzer, which uses the same platform to produce hydrogen. The company competes with gas reciprocating engines, small gas turbines, combined-cycle plants and grid-plus-backup systems.

**Core strengths and competitive advantages**
- Time to power: skid-mounted units can be installed in weeks or months, and power within about 90 days in certain project scopes.
- Reliability: fleet availability of about 99.9% for non-redundant installations after 2020, rising to about 99.999% with redundancy.
- AI load compatibility: the system follows variable loads, and the company states it responds at least twice as fast as turbines and engines.
- Emissions: near-zero smog-forming pollutants, and no water use in steady-state operation.
- Shared platform: the Energy Server and Electrolyzer use common supply chain and manufacturing. Bloom holds 380 active U.S. utility patents and 183 pending U.S. applications.

**End markets, geography and customers**
The company serves Data Centers, Commercial & Industrial, and Utilities. The U.S. is its largest market by revenue and installed base; South Korea is second, with about 682 MW deployed. Systems are installed at about 1,100 sites in 9 countries. Three customers or distributors accounted for 43%, 13% and 12% of 2025 revenue; the 43% customer is a related party. Market share is not disclosed in the resources.

**Industry trends, opportunities and challenges**
Opportunities: data center and AI power demand; a 2,300 GW generator interconnection queue at end-2024; and the 30% Section 48E ITC for fuel cells beginning construction after December 31, 2025.
Challenges: the ITC for fuel cells on non-zero carbon fuels expired at the end of 2024; sales cycles have lengthened; and tariffs and rare earth supply from China create supply risk.

**Recent strategic initiatives**
- AEP: a supply agreement for up to 1 GW (announced November 2024; initial 100 MW order).
- Brookfield: a prospective financing framework of up to $5.0 billion over five years (August 2025).
- Oracle: a partnership for on-site power for AI data centers, with a warrant for 3,531,073 shares.
- Capacity: expansion of Fremont manufacturing from 1 GW to 2 GW of annual run rate, targeted for completion by end-2026.

**Summary**

Revenue rose 37.3% to $2,024.0 million in 2025, and gross margin improved to 29% from 27%. Product gross margin fell to 35% from 37%, mainly due to $21.9 million of Electrolyzer inventory reserves and asset impairments, a $12.7 million construction-in-progress impairment, and a $15.9 million reduction in product revenue from hyperscaler share-based consideration. Operating cash flow was $113.9 million, weighed down by a $142.3 million decline in deferred revenue and customer deposits. Cash reached $2,454.1 million after the $2.5 billion 0% notes issuance. The thesis would be challenged if Fremont reaches 2 GW later than end-2026 or if FERC's 2026 findings do not ease interconnection.
