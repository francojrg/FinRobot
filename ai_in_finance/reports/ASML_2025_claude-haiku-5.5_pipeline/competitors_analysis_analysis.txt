**ASML Holding N.V. versus AMAT, LRCX and KLAC: fiscal 2022 to 2025 (with 2026 peer data)**

ASML is the largest company in this group by revenue in 2025, at 32,667 (table units), and it has the most cash-generative recent profile: its free cash flow conversion was 1.20 in 2024 and 1.15 in 2025, the highest in the group in both years. The most important shortfall in ASML's record is the 2024 slowdown. After growing 30.2% in 2023 (from 21,173 in 2022 to 27,558), revenue rose only 2.6% in 2024, to 28,263. Growth recovered to 15.6% in 2025, reaching 32,667. Over the full 2022 to 2025 period, the implied compound annual growth rate is about 15.5% (illustrative, calculated from the endpoints), which is strong, but it was concentrated in 2023 and 2025 rather than spread evenly.

Against peers, ASML's 2023 growth was well ahead of AMAT's 2.8%, and its 2025 growth was ahead of AMAT's 4.4%. In 2024, however, ASML's 2.6% was roughly in line with AMAT's 2.5%, while LRCX (-14.5%) and KLAC (-6.5%) declined. In 2025, LRCX (23.7%) and KLAC (23.9%) grew faster than ASML. Over the 2023 to 2026 window, LRCX's revenue compounded at about 10.0% a year and KLAC's at about 8.9% (illustrative), both calculated from the endpoints. **Gross margin**

ASML's gross margin rose from 50.5% in 2022 to 51.3% in 2023, held at 51.3% in 2024, and reached 52.8% in 2025. The 2024 result was flat, not a decline, but it means the improvement came in two steps, not continuously.

In 2024 the gap to KLAC was 8.7 points (51.3% against 60.0%), and ASML's margin was 3.8 points above AMAT's 47.5% and 4.0 points above LRCX's 47.3%. In 2025 ASML's 52.8% was 4.1 points above AMAT and LRCX (both 48.7%) and 8.1 points below KLAC (60.9%). The steady rise across the group in these four years suggests that cost efficiency improved across the sector, and ASML's gross margin was the second highest in each year.

**EBITDA margin**

ASML's EBITDA margin was 33.5% in 2022, 36.2% in 2023, 35.8% in 2024 and 38.4% in 2025. The 2024 dip of 0.4 points is small, but it is the only year of decline. The weakest data point for ASML is 2023. Free cash flow fell from 7,168 in 2022 to 3,247 in 2023, a decline of about 55%, and FCF conversion dropped to 0.41. ASML's ROIC was 42.4% in 2022, 43.0% in 2023, 32.3% in 2024 and 39.5% in 2025. The 10.7-point drop in 2024 is the largest move in the series, and the 2025 recovery to 39.5% recovers about three-quarters of it. The resource does not explain the 2024 decline.

**Valuation (EV/EBITDA)**

ASML's EV/EBITDA moved from 28.06 in 2022 to 26.73 in 2023, 25.08 in 2024 and 27.29 in 2025. The multiple has stayed in a narrow band, but it has never fallen below 25x in the years provided.

In 2024 the order reversed for two peers: LRCX traded at 28.13x and KLAC at 29.64x, both above ASML's 25.08x, while AMAT stood at 16.73x. In 2025 ASML was again the most expensive in the group, at 27.29x, against 22.89x for KLAC, 18.64x for LRCX and 18.51x for AMAT. **Conclusion**

Across the four years, ASML's operating profile is strong and consistent. It is the largest company by 2025 revenue in this group, its gross margin and EBITDA margin rank second behind KLAC in every year provided, and its ROIC ranked first in 2023 and 2024 and second in 2025. Its FCF conversion was the highest in the group in 2024 and 2025. The shortfalls are specific: revenue growth slowed to 2.6% in 2024, free cash flow fell about 55% in 2023 with conversion at 0.41, and ROIC dropped 10.7 points in 2024. Each of these was followed by recovery, but the resource does not say why they occurred, so the recovery is the only evidence of resilience.

The 2025 valuation is harder to justify on these numbers alone. ASML's 27.29x EV/EBITDA is the highest in the group that year, and the premium is not explained by growth: LRCX and KLAC grew faster in 2025 at lower multiples. ASML's margin lead over AMAT and LRCX (about 3 points on EBITDA margin) and its FCF conversion are real advantages, and they may support a premium, but the data contains no forward estimates, so the premium cannot be tested against expected future growth. The 2025 price-to-earnings ratio of 36.53 for ASML against 26.92 for AMAT, 23.46 for LRCX and 29.49 for KLAC tells the same story.

The thesis would be weakened by several observable developments. Revenue growth returning to low single digits, as in 2024, would undermine the case for a premium that is not supported by growth. A repeat of the 2023 conversion drop, with FCF conversion falling well below 1.0 for a year, would call into question the cash-generation argument. A decline in ROIC from the 2025 level of 39.5% toward the 2024 level of 32.3, or a drop in gross margin from 52.8%, would show that the margin gains are not holding. If KLAC's EBITDA margin and ROIC keep rising while ASML's margin remains second, the premium would be harder to defend relative to a peer with stronger returns on the same metrics.
