# Amazon.com, Inc. (AMZN): FY2025 Equity Research Report

*Data note: Figures below are approximate and compiled from Amazon's reported quarterly results and the FY2025 framework. Verify exact values against the filed Form 10-K before relying on them for investment decisions.*

## Investment Summary

Amazon enters 2026 with a larger and more profitable business than at any point in its history. FY2025 net sales were roughly $717 billion, up about 12% year over year, and operating income expanded faster than revenue as AWS growth re-accelerated and advertising scaled. Diluted EPS was in the range of $7, up from $5.53 in FY2024, helped by operating leverage and a more favorable mix. Free cash flow, however, was compressed by a sharp step-up in capital expenditure tied to AI infrastructure.

We rate AMZN **Buy** with a view that the market is underweighting the durability of AWS growth and the margin contribution from advertising, while the capex cycle remains the central debate. Our assessment rests on three pillars: a re-accelerating cloud franchise, a high-margin advertising business, and a retail operation that has become structurally more efficient.

## Business Overview

Amazon operates through three reportable segments:

- **North America**: Online and physical retail, third-party seller services, advertising, subscriptions, and devices. This is the largest segment by revenue, at roughly $400 billion in FY2025 sales, with operating margins in the mid-single digits.
- **International**: The same business lines across Europe, Japan, India, and other markets. Revenue was roughly $155 billion, with margins improving steadily from near breakeven as the company reduced fulfillment costs and closed loss-making operations.
- **Amazon Web Services (AWS)**: Cloud infrastructure and platform services. AWS generated roughly $129 billion in revenue, growing about 20%, and produced the majority of consolidated operating income.

Revenue mix has shifted toward higher-margin activities. Advertising, which is reported within North America and International, continues to be a major profit driver and now exceeds $60 billion in annual revenue. Third-party seller services and subscriptions (Prime) provide recurring, fee-based income that reduces dependence on low-margin first-party retail.

## Operating Results

**Revenue and growth.** Consolidated net sales grew about 12% in FY2025, versus 11% in FY2024. Growth was broad-based, with AWS, advertising, and third-party seller services all growing faster than the core retail line.

**Margins.** Consolidated operating income rose to roughly $80 billion, implying an operating margin near 11%, up from about 10.7% in FY2024. Gross margin improved as the fulfillment network matured and advertising grew as a share of sales. AWS operating margin was approximately 35%, while North America and International margins remained thin by comparison, at mid-single digits and low single digits respectively.

**Earnings per share.** Net income increased to approximately $75 to $80 billion, supported by operating gains and non-operating items including investment gains on Anthropic and other holdings. Diluted EPS was roughly $7, compared with $5.53 in FY2024. Investors should note that non-operating gains can flatter EPS relative to operating performance.

**Cash flow.** Operating cash flow grew to approximately $130 billion, reflecting higher earnings and working capital discipline. Capital expenditures, including finance leases, rose sharply to well above $100 billion, driven by data center and custom silicon investment for AI workloads. As a result, free cash flow (operating cash flow less purchases of property and equipment) fell well below FY2024 levels, which is the key metric of concern for bears.

**Balance sheet.** Amazon retains a strong balance sheet with substantial cash and marketable securities against moderate long-term debt, and it carries significant lease obligations tied to its logistics and data center footprint. Liquidity is not a constraint.

## Market Position

Amazon is the largest online retailer in the United States and holds a leading position in global cloud infrastructure, where AWS remains the market share leader despite intensifying competition. Its logistics network, including fulfillment centers, sortation hubs, and last-mile delivery, is a durable competitive advantage that is costly for rivals to replicate.

Advertising has become a significant moat. Sponsored products, display, and streaming-related ad inventory give Amazon direct access to purchase-intent data, which few competitors can match. Prime membership continues to anchor customer loyalty and purchase frequency.

Market share in AWS has been stable to slightly eroding in percentage terms, as Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud have grown quickly from smaller bases. However, AWS's absolute revenue growth remains the largest in the industry, and its backlog and AI-related demand indicate continued momentum.

## Risk Assessment

1. **Capital intensity and returns on AI spending.** Capex is rising faster than revenue. If AI demand does not translate into adequate returns, depreciation will weigh on margins and free cash flow for years.
2. **Regulatory and antitrust exposure.** The U.S. Federal Trade Commission and several international authorities are pursuing cases relating to marketplace practices, Prime, and competitive conduct. Adverse rulings could require operational changes or financial penalties.
3. **Competition in cloud.** Microsoft and Google are gaining share in AI workloads, and pricing pressure could compress AWS margins.
4. **Labor and cost inflation.** Wage pressure, benefits costs, and regulatory scrutiny of warehouse working conditions remain ongoing risks.
5. **Macroeconomic sensitivity.** Consumer discretionary spending, tariffs affecting imported goods, and currency fluctuations affect the International segment and retail demand.
6. **Earnings quality.** Gains on equity investments introduce volatility unrelated to core operations.
7. **Execution risk in new markets.** Investments in healthcare, grocery, satellite broadband (Project Kuiper), and robotics carry uncertain payback periods.

## Competitors Analysis

**Microsoft (MSFT)** is the closest peer in cloud and enterprise AI. Microsoft generates a higher consolidated operating margin, around 45%, and its Intelligent Cloud segment is a larger share of total revenue. Microsoft's FCF conversion has been stronger, and its capex-to-revenue ratio is more moderate in relative terms. However, Amazon's revenue scale is roughly 2.5 times larger, and its retail and advertising businesses provide diversification Microsoft lacks.

**Alphabet (GOOGL)** competes with Amazon in digital advertising and cloud. Alphabet's revenue is in the range of $400 billion, with operating margins around 32%, and advertising constitutes the vast majority of its income. Google Cloud is growing at a faster percentage rate than AWS from a smaller base, with margins improving. Amazon's advertising business is smaller than Google's in absolute terms but is growing rapidly and benefits from closer proximity to point-of-sale purchase intent.

**Walmart (WMT)** is the principal retail competitor. With revenue near $680 billion, Walmart is larger than Amazon's retail-heavy segments, but its operating margin is only around 4%, reflecting a grocery-heavy mix. Walmart's e-commerce business has grown quickly and is profitable, and it competes directly with Amazon for household spending. Amazon's margin profile is superior primarily because of AWS and advertising.

In summary, Amazon is the only company in this group that combines a leading cloud franchise, a major advertising platform, and the largest e-commerce operation. This breadth supports its valuation premium, though it also makes the company more complex to analyze.

## Key Takeaways

- FY2025 revenue growth of roughly 12% and EPS near $7 reflect a healthy core business.
- AWS re-accelerated to about 20% growth and remains the profit engine, with margins near 35%.
- Advertising is a high-margin contributor that is increasingly central to profitability.
- Capex surged to record levels, compressing free cash flow and making AI returns the critical variable.
- Retail margins have improved but remain thin, so mix shift toward services is essential to margin expansion.
- Regulatory and competitive risks are meaningful but are unlikely to alter the company's structural position in the near term.

## Investment Theses

**Thesis 1: AWS growth can sustain high-teens to twenties percentage growth.** AI workloads, custom Trainium silicon, and a large enterprise backlog support continued expansion. Successful AWS growth would drive a meaningful share of consolidated profit growth through 2027.

**Thesis 2: Advertising will continue compounding faster than retail.** As a high-margin, data-rich business with limited incremental cost, advertising can push consolidated margins higher even if retail growth moderates.

**Thesis 3: Capex will eventually yield returns that restore free cash flow growth.** Once data center capacity is utilized and depreciation stabilizes relative to revenue, free cash flow should inflect upward. Investors willing to look through the current investment cycle may be compensated.

**Thesis 4 (Risk to thesis): Capital intensity could overwhelm returns.** If AI demand normalizes or competitors undercut pricing, depreciation could outpace revenue growth and compress margins. We would reassess our Buy rating if AWS growth falls below 15% for two consecutive quarters or if free cash flow remains structurally negative.

**Conclusion:** Amazon's FY2025 results demonstrate a scaled, diversified, and increasingly profitable platform. We see the current capex cycle as a justified investment in durable growth, and we maintain a Buy rating on AMZN.
