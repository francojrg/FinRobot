# Alphabet Inc. (GOOG): FY2025 Equity Research Report

*Note: Figures are rounded and approximate, drawn from Alphabet's reported results for fiscal year 2025. Verify exact numbers against the filed Form 10-K before relying on them for investment decisions.*

## Investment Summary

Alphabet enters 2026 with strong fundamentals. Revenue grew roughly 15% to about $400 billion, operating margin held near 32–33%, and diluted earnings per share rose at a double-digit pace. Search remains the profit engine, YouTube and Cloud are growing faster than the core, and the company generates well over $100 billion in annual operating cash flow. The main debate is whether the sharply higher capital expenditure needed for AI infrastructure will compress free cash flow and returns. We rate the shares **Outperform**, viewing the current valuation as reasonable relative to earnings power and the company's diversified growth drivers, with AI monetization as the key variable to monitor.

## Business Overview

Alphabet is a holding company whose principal operating unit is Google. The company reports two main segments:

- **Google Services** includes Search and other, YouTube advertising, Network (partner advertising), subscriptions, platforms and devices (including Google Play, Pixel, and Google One). Services accounts for roughly 85–87% of revenue.
- **Google Cloud** provides infrastructure (Google Cloud Platform), Workspace productivity software, and AI services, including the Vertex AI platform and Gemini models for enterprises.

A third category, **Other Bets**, includes Waymo (autonomous driving), Verily, and other early-stage ventures. It generates small revenue but meaningful operating losses, which the company continues to fund as long-term options.

Revenue is predominantly advertising-based, with the majority generated in the United States, followed by EMEA and APAC. Alphabet's cost base is dominated by technical infrastructure (data centers, servers, networking) and personnel.

## Operating Results

**Consolidated performance (FY2025, approximate):**

- **Revenue:** ~$400 billion, up ~15% year over year, with growth accelerating through the year.
- **Operating income:** ~$130 billion, with operating margin of roughly 32–33%, stable to slightly higher than FY2024 despite rising depreciation from AI infrastructure investment.
- **Net income:** ~$130 billion or more, supported by investment gains and a one-time European Commission fine charge that weighed on certain quarters.
- **Diluted EPS:** roughly $10.50, up from $8.04 in FY2024, aided by operating leverage and share repurchases.
- **Operating cash flow:** comfortably above $150 billion.
- **Capital expenditures:** approximately $90 billion, up sharply from $52.5 billion in FY2024, reflecting data center and AI chip investment.
- **Free cash flow:** roughly $70 billion or more after capex, still substantial but down in growth terms as spending rose faster than operating cash generation.

**Segment performance:**

- **Google Search and other:** ~$220+ billion, growing low-to-mid teens. Management credits AI Overviews and AI Mode with driving more queries, particularly longer and commercial ones, without degrading monetization.
- **YouTube ads:** ~$40+ billion, growing around 10%, supported by Shorts monetization and connected TV viewing.
- **Subscriptions, platforms and devices:** ~$50 billion, growing at a double-digit rate, led by YouTube subscriptions (including YouTube TV and Music/Premium) and Google One storage plans.
- **Google Cloud:** ~$58–60 billion, growing above 30%, the fastest-growing major segment. Operating margin expanded into the low-20% range, up from roughly 11% a year earlier, as scale and AI demand improved utilization. Cloud backlog (contracted but unrecognized revenue) grew substantially, a key forward indicator.
- **Other Bets:** revenue under $2 billion, with operating losses of several billion dollars.

## Market Position

Alphabet holds a dominant position in global search, with a share of roughly 90% of general search queries on a worldwide basis, though this share has come under pressure from AI chat assistants. The company's response has been to integrate Gemini into Search and to grow its consumer AI app, which surpassed roughly 700 million monthly active users during 2025.

In digital advertising, Alphabet competes with Meta and Amazon for performance budgets, and YouTube is the leading streaming video platform by watch time in the United States. Android remains the dominant mobile operating system outside China, and the Chrome browser has a large installed base.

In cloud, Alphabet is the third-largest provider behind Amazon Web Services and Microsoft Azure, but it has gained share in AI workloads thanks to its custom Tensor Processing Units (TPUs), which it sells and deploys for internal and external customers.

## Risk Assessment

The 10-K highlights several material risks:

1. **Antitrust and regulation:** The U.S. Department of Justice has won a ruling that Alphabet illegally maintained a monopoly in general search, and remedies, including possible restrictions on default distribution agreements, remain a significant uncertainty. The ad technology case presents additional remedy risk, including potential divestiture pressure. The European Union continues to pursue competition and digital markets actions, with fines and compliance costs possible.
2. **AI disruption to search:** Generative AI assistants could alter user behavior and reduce query volume or ad load if monetization does not keep pace.
3. **Capital intensity:** Capex approaching $90 billion increases depreciation and raises the risk of overbuilding if AI demand softens.
4. **Advertising cyclicality:** Roughly three-quarters of revenue depends on advertising, which is sensitive to macroeconomic conditions and digital ad pricing.
5. **Privacy and data protection:** Evolving rules on data collection, consent, and cross-border transfers could constrain targeting.
6. **Talent, legal, and geopolitical exposure:** Competition for AI talent is intense, and the company faces litigation, export controls affecting chip supply, and tariff-related cost pressure.

**Microsoft** offers the closest comparison for enterprise AI and cloud. It has higher operating margins and faster Azure growth in absolute terms, but Alphabet's consumer franchise (Search, YouTube, Android) provides a broader and more diversified advertising and subscription base.

**Meta** is the most direct competitor in digital advertising. It has faster revenue growth and a strong return on ad spend improvement from AI-driven ranking, but it lacks a cloud business and has no comparable search franchise. Its heavier relative capex and dependence on a single ad platform make it more volatile.

**Amazon** competes in cloud (AWS) and advertising (retail media). AWS is larger than Google Cloud by revenue but growing more slowly. Amazon's consolidated margins are thinner because of retail, while Alphabet's advertising model delivers superior profitability.

Overall, Alphabet combines Microsoft-like cloud exposure with Meta-like advertising economics, and it holds the most valuable consumer search asset in the group. Its valuation appears reasonable relative to these peers given its earnings growth and balance sheet strength.

## Key Takeaways

- Alphabet delivered accelerating top-line growth in FY2025 with stable margins despite a large step-up in AI investment.
- Google Cloud has become a genuine growth and profit driver, with margins expanding quickly and backlog rising.
- Search has proven resilient to generative AI so far, with AI features increasing engagement and commercial query volume.
- Free cash flow remains strong but is under pressure from capex, making capital allocation discipline a central investor concern.
- Antitrust remedies in the U.S. and EU represent the largest identifiable downside risk to the core business model.

## Investment Theses

**Bull case:** Alphabet is a diversified AI beneficiary. Search monetization is intact, YouTube and subscriptions provide durable secondary growth, and Cloud with custom TPUs could sustain above-30% growth with improving margins. At current multiples, the market underprices optionality in Waymo and Gemini. Expect EPS growth in the low-to-mid teens.

**Bear case:** AI chat assistants erode search query share and pricing power, while capex spending outpaces revenue and compresses free cash flow. Regulatory remedies force changes to default distribution deals that cost billions in annual revenue.

**Our view:** We rate GOOG **Outperform** with a valuation anchored to earnings power, assuming continued mid-teens revenue growth, stable operating margins in the low 30s, and capex plateauing as a share of revenue by 2027. Key monitoring points are search revenue growth versus query trends, Cloud backlog conversion and margin progression, the outcome of antitrust remedies, and free cash flow per share.
