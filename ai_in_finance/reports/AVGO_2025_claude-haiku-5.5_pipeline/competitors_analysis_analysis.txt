**Broadcom (AVGO) combined revenue growth with high margins through fiscal 2025, but its returns and valuation moved in opposite directions.** Revenue rose from 33,203 in fiscal 2022 to 63,887 in fiscal 2025, a compound annual rate of about 24 percent (illustrative, from the endpoints). Gross margin was 67.8 percent and EBITDA margin was 54.3 percent in fiscal 2025. ROIC, however, fell from 24.7 percent in 2023 to 6.4 percent in 2024 and reached only 13.2 percent in 2025. Over the same period EV/EBITDA rose from 11.67 to 51.89. The figures below are as given in the table, which does not state units.

**Data limitations.** The four companies report on different fiscal calendars, and the table gives AVGO fiscal years 2022 to 2025, NVDA 2023 to 2026, MRVL 2023 to 2026, and AMD 2022 to 2025. Direct year-by-year comparisons are therefore possible only for 2023, 2024 and 2025, and for 2022 only against AMD. The table also contains no AVGO figure for 2026. The FCF Conversion line cannot be reproduced from the EBITDA and FCF lines in the table. AVGO's FCF divided by EBITDA is 0.85, 0.86, 0.81 and 0.78 for 2022 to 2025, against stated conversion of 1.42, 1.25, 3.29 and 1.16. NVDA shows the same gap. The stated figures probably use a different denominator, but the table does not define it, so the FCF conversion comparisons below should be read with that caution.

**Revenue and growth.** AVGO's growth accelerated from 7.9 percent in 2023 to 44.0 percent in 2024, then slowed to 23.9 percent in 2025. The 2024 step-up is the main pattern in the series. Against peers, AVGO lagged NVDA sharply in both overlapping years, with NVDA growing 125.9 percent in 2024 and 114.2 percent in 2025. AVGO's 2024 growth exceeded AMD's 13.7 percent and MRVL's decline of 7.0 percent. In 2025, AVGO's 23.9 percent trailed AMD's 34.3 percent and was well above MRVL's 4.7 percent. In 2023 AVGO grew 7.9 percent while AMD shrank 3.9 percent. In scale, AVGO's 2025 revenue of 63,887 was about 49 percent of NVDA's 2025 revenue of 130,497 and about 1.8 times AMD's 34,639. Revenue growth is the one metric where AVGO's record is clearly weaker than the leader's, and the table does not explain the 2024 acceleration or the 2025 slowdown.

**Gross margin.** AVGO's gross margin was 66.5 percent in 2022, rose to 68.9 percent in 2023, then fell to 63.0 percent in 2024 before recovering to 67.8 percent in 2025. The 2024 decline of 5.9 points is the largest adverse move in the company's series. In 2022 and 2023 AVGO led all peers that year, at 66.5 percent against AMD's 44.9 percent, and at 68.9 percent against NVDA's 56.9 percent and MRVL's 50.5 percent. In 2024 and 2025 NVDA's margins were higher, at 72.7 and 75.0 percent. AVGO remained well above AMD (49.4 and 49.5 percent) and MRVL (41.6 and 41.3 percent) in both years. The table does not state the cause of the 2024 dip. Its size matters, however: at the 2023 margin of 68.9 percent, 2024 gross profit would have been about 3,000 higher than the reported 32,509 (illustrative).

**EBITDA margin.** AVGO's EBITDA margin was 57.7 percent in 2022, 57.4 percent in 2023, 46.3 percent in 2024 and 54.3 percent in 2025. The 2024 fall of 11.1 points was steeper than the gross margin decline. In 2024 EBITDA rose 16 percent (from 20,554 to 23,879) while revenue rose 44 percent, so operating costs below gross profit absorbed much of the gross-profit gain. Gross profit grew 31.7 percent that year. The 2025 recovery to 54.3 percent came with EBITDA growth of 45 percent, which outpaced revenue growth of 23.9 percent. Against peers in 2023, AVGO's 57.4 percent beat NVDA's 22.2 percent, MRVL's 27.8 percent and AMD's 17.9 percent. In 2024 NVDA led at 58.4 percent, with AVGO at 46.3 percent still ahead of AMD's 20.0 percent and MRVL's 15.4 percent. In 2025, the most recent year shared with NVDA, NVDA's margin was 66.0 percent against AVGO's 54.3 percent. AVGO was ahead of AMD (21.0 percent) and MRVL (11.3 percent). NVDA's fiscal 2026 margin of 66.9 percent and MRVL's fiscal 2026 margin of 55.4 percent are the latest peer readings. MRVL's jump from 11.3 to 55.4 percent in one year is large, and the table gives no explanation for it.

**FCF conversion.** On the stated basis, AVGO's conversion was 1.42 in 2022, 1.25 in 2023, 3.29 in 2024 and 1.16 in 2025. The 2024 value is an outlier and cannot be reconciled with the table's own FCF and EBITDA, so it should not be read as a real efficiency gain. The more reliable measure is absolute free cash flow, which rose steadily from 16,312 to 26,914. Against peers, AVGO's 2023 stated conversion of 1.25 exceeded NVDA's 0.87 and trailed AMD's 1.31 only slightly. In 2025 AVGO's 1.16 was above NVDA's 0.83 and below AMD's 1.55. AMD's conversion was the highest of the group in 2022, at 2.36 against AVGO's 1.42. MRVL's conversion is n/m for 2023 to 2025 and 0.52 for 2026, so the comparison is limited. On the EBITDA-based measure, AVGO's ratio drifted down from 0.85 to 0.78 over the period, which suggests slightly less cash generated per dollar of EBITDA even as cash flow in absolute terms grew.

**ROIC.** AVGO's ROIC was 21.1 percent in 2022 and 24.7 percent in 2023, the highest levels in the series. It then fell to 6.4 percent in 2024 and recovered to 13.2 percent in 2025, which is still below the 2022 and 2023 levels. The table does not state the invested capital base, so the cause of the decline cannot be separated from the change in the capital base. The fall in 2024 coincides with the margin contraction described above and with the year of the largest revenue increase, which suggests the capital base grew faster than operating profit that year. This is a pattern in the numbers, not a verified cause. Against peers, AVGO's returns were far ahead of AMD in 2022 (21.1 against 1.6 percent) and of NVDA, MRVL and AMD in 2023 (24.7 percent against 10.0, 0.9 and 0.8 percent). In 2024 AVGO's 6.4 percent trailed NVDA's 56.9 percent but exceeded AMD's 2.8 percent and MRVL's negative 2.1 percent. In 2025 AVGO's 13.2 percent again trailed NVDA's 83.3 percent but exceeded AMD's 4.8 percent and MRVL's negative 4.0 percent. NVDA's returns are the group's standard, and AVGO's recent recovery is real but partial.

**EV/EBITDA.** AVGO's multiple rose from 11.67 in 2022 to 18.17 in 2023, 35.75 in 2024 and 51.89 in 2025. In 2022 AVGO was well below AMD's 18.51. In 2023 AVGO's 18.17 was the lowest of the group, below NVDA's 81.93, MRVL's 24.74 and AMD's 58.57. In 2024 AVGO's 35.75 was below NVDA's 42.71, AMD's 37.77 and MRVL's 72.94. In 2025 AVGO at 51.89 was above NVDA's 34.14 and AMD's 47.75, and below MRVL's 155.2. So AVGO moved from the cheapest multiple in its peer set to a multiple above two of its three peers. Over the period, EBITDA rose 81 percent (19,155 to 34,714) while the multiple rose about 4.4 times. Taken together, these imply enterprise value about 8 times higher (illustrative, assuming the multiple applies to same-year EBITDA). Most of that increase came from the multiple rather than from earnings. NVDA shows that a high multiple can fall quickly as earnings grow: its multiple declined from 81.93 to 32.14 while its EBITDA rose from 5,986 to 144,552. MRVL's 2025 multiple of 155.2 sits on EBITDA of only 652, so it reflects depressed earnings rather than a premium.

**Conclusion.** On the metrics in this table, AVGO shows strong profitability and a durable margin profile. Its gross margin has stayed above 63 percent in every year, its EBITDA margin returned to 54.3 percent in 2025, its free cash flow grew every year, and it has led or matched the peers in gross margin and EBITDA margin in most overlapping years. The weaknesses are the 2024 margin contraction, the 2024 drop in ROIC that has only partly reversed, and a revenue growth rate well below NVDA's in both overlapping years. Those weaknesses matter because the valuation has moved the other way. An EV/EBITDA of 51.89 implies that investors now pay well above the level of 2022 and 2023 for each dollar of EBITDA, and that is supportable only if EBITDA growth continues and ROIC climbs back toward the 2023 level. The 2025 numbers provide partial support: EBITDA grew 45 percent and ROIC rose from 6.4 to 13.2 percent. They do not yet show a return to the 24.7 percent level that accompanied the lowest multiple in the series.

Several results would show this view is wrong. If the next year's EBITDA growth falls well below the 23.9 percent revenue growth rate of 2025, the 2025 margin recovery would not be sustained and the multiple would look too high. If ROIC stalls near 13 percent, the company would be earning a return well below its 2022 and 2023 level on a larger capital base. If the EBITDA-to-FCF ratio keeps drifting down from 0.78, cash generation would be lagging earnings. Conversely, a return of ROIC toward 20 percent, with gross margin holding near 68 percent, would indicate that the 2024 dip was temporary and that the current multiple is supported by the earnings profile visible in this table.
