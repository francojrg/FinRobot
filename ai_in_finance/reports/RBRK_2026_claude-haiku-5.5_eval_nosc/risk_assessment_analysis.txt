# Top Three Key Risks for Rubrik, Inc.

Based on the 10-K, the three most material risks are (1) security incidents and the liability they create, given that Rubrik's core business is protecting customer data; (2) concentrated dependence on Channel Partners and a limited set of hardware manufacturers; and (3) the combination of a long record of operating losses, an ongoing revenue-mix transition, and a $1.15 billion convertible note obligation. The filing does not provide cross-industry benchmarks, so the vertical comparisons below rely on the company's own characterization of its market and are qualitative.

## Risk 1: Security Incidents, Platform Failure, and Liability Exposure

**Industry Vertical Risk.** Rubrik sells data security, a category where a single failure is itself the product risk. The filing states that its market is "at an early stage and rapidly evolving," and that the company faces "heightened risk of being targeted by bad actors" because of its ransomware recovery capabilities and marketing. The filing also notes that its customers include heavily regulated verticals such as banking, healthcare, and financial services, and that it is subject to regulation including GDPR, CCPA, HIPAA/HITECH, and emerging AI laws. The reputational consequences are amplified because, as the filing puts it, "an actual or perceived security incident affecting our solutions would be especially detrimental to our reputation and our business." Relative to a vendor whose products are not themselves the security perimeter, this is a higher-consequence exposure, though the filing does not quantify how it compares with other sectors.

**Cyclicality.** Security incidents are not economically cyclical in the usual sense, but demand for protection tends to be shaped by the threat environment and by customer budgets. The filing observes that "if the incidence of cyberattacks were to decline, or enterprises or governments perceive that the general level of cyberattacks has declined, our ability to attract new customers could be adversely affected." Thus the risk is somewhat counter-cyclical to threat levels: rising ransomware activity drives adoption, while a lull could slow it. A high-profile incident would be an idiosyncratic shock rather than a macro cycle.

**Risk Quantification.** The filing supplies several concrete exposure points. The Ransomware Recovery Warranty reimburses eligible customers "up to $10,000,000" for recovery expenses, and the filing warns that widespread claims "could significantly increase our costs." GDPR fines can reach "20 million Euros... or 4% of annual global revenue, whichever is greater," and the UK GDPR allows fines up to 17.5 million pounds sterling. The filing discloses a February 2025 incident in which an unauthorized actor accessed certain log files, which it says did not reach customer-secured data, while cautioning that "there can be no guarantee that any attack in the future will have a similarly minimal impact." Insurance is described as possibly inadequate, with the company stating it "cannot be sure that our insurance coverage will be adequate or sufficient." Given the $1.32 billion revenue base, a 4% GDPR fine on global revenue would be material in scale, though the filing does not estimate any specific loss.

**Downside Protections.** The filing identifies several mitigants: customer agreements "typically contain provisions" limiting liability, the company generally has "limitation of liability provisions in our terms and conditions," it maintains insurance and "seek[s] to insure against these types of claims," and it employs a shared responsibility model under which customers are responsible for configuring and enforcing security features such as multi-factor authentication. These protections are real but explicitly conditional. The filing acknowledges these provisions "may not be effective or enforceable under the laws of some jurisdictions," and that limitations of liability "may not fully or effectively protect us." Rubrik has also obtained FedRAMP certification, which the filing describes as a requirement for certain government sales, a form of regulatory validation that partly offsets reputational risk in the public sector.

## Risk 2: Concentration in Channel Partners and Hardware Manufacturers

**Industry Vertical Risk.** Rubrik's go-to-market is heavily intermediated. The filing states that "a vast majority of sales of our solutions flow through our Channel Partners." Its three largest Channel Partners, Arrow Enterprise Computing Solutions, Exclusive Networks, and Ingram Micro Inc., together generated approximately 68% of revenue in fiscal 2026 and 73% in fiscal 2025. On the hardware side, a large majority of customer data relies on Rubrik-branded Appliances built on servers from Super Micro Computer, Inc. This supply-side dependence is more concentrated than a typical pure software model. The filing also notes that its Channel Partner agreements are non-exclusive, renew automatically in one-year increments, and "may be terminated by either party at any time," and that the partners "do not impose minimum purchase requirements."

**Cyclicality.** Channel concentration amplifies macroeconomic exposure. The filing notes that some partners "may be unable to withstand adverse changes in economic conditions," that weakness in end-user markets "could negatively affect the cash flows of our Channel Partners," and that delayed payments would increase Rubrik's credit risk exposure. Component supply is also cyclical in its own right: the filing refers to "global chip shortages and allocation constraints, in part due to elevated industry demand," which have produced "extended lead times and increased server prices," and it notes that tariffs on China-origin hardware affect both price and supply.

**Risk Quantification.** The 68% and 73% figures are the clearest measure. A decline in the three largest partners' share, or the loss of one of them, would directly affect the majority of sales. The filing also says Supermicro's agreement "does not provide long-term price assurances for certain components," and that some OEMs "carry products that compete with our solutions." Customers outside the United States generated 30% and 31% of revenue in fiscal 2026 and 2025, respectively, and the filing identifies new Channel Partners as necessary for expansion into certain countries, which adds a further dependency. The filing also states that Rubrik "may lose revenue and incur increased costs" if it must qualify a new manufacturer.

**Downside Protections.** The filing points to a few mitigants. Channel Partners "extend our reach, in particular with smaller customers and in geographies where we have less sales presence," and Rubrik has "largely completed" the transition of Appliance sales to its contract manufacturers, which shifts some hardware risk away from its own balance sheet. The company also has the "ability to qualify new commodity servers for Rubrik-branded Appliances" and customers "would have the ability to purchase compatible third-party commodity servers from other OEMs." These are meaningful options, but the filing is candid that switching "may create increased costs or delays." The protections reduce the probability of a sudden disruption more than they reduce its severity if one occurs.

## Risk 3: Path to Sustained Profitability and Leverage Under a Shifting Revenue Model

**Industry Vertical Risk.** Rubrik's financial profile reflects a company still in a high-growth, high-investment phase. Revenue grew from $627.9 million in fiscal 2024 to $886.5 million in fiscal 2025 and $1.32 billion in fiscal 2026. Yet the company reported net losses of $(354.2) million, $(1.15) billion, and $(348.8) million in those years, and an accumulated deficit of $(3.19) billion as of January 31, 2026. The filing attributes ongoing spending to product development, sales expansion, international growth, and AI investments, and it states that it "may incur significant losses in the future." Within software and data security, a company at this stage carries more earnings risk than a mature, profitable peer, though the filing itself does not make that comparison.

**Cyclicality.** The filing describes meaningful sensitivity to economic conditions. It notes that customers have caused "a reduction in multi-year upfront payments," "reduced contract sizes," and "generally increased scrutiny on technology spending," and that "to the extent there is a sustained general economic downturn, a recession... these customer dynamics may be exacerbated." Seasonality adds a second cycle: the company "typically sell[s] a higher percentage of subscriptions... in the fourth quarter of our fiscal year," and "a large portion of our sales occur toward the end of each fiscal quarter." Because subscription revenue is recognized ratably, the effect of a slowdown appears gradually, which the filing notes: decreases in new subscriptions "would negatively affect our revenue in future quarters" rather than immediately.

**Risk Quantification.** Several specific items stand out. The company issued $1.15 billion of Notes in June 2025, which mature in June 2030, and used proceeds to repay $327.9 million under its prior credit facility. The filing states that the company may need to pay cash upon conversion and to repurchase the Notes upon a fundamental change, and that failure to do so "would constitute a default under the Indenture." Federal net operating loss carryforwards total $1.97 billion and state NOLs total $870.7 million, but the filing warns that Section 382 limitations and California's suspension of NOL use may prevent the company from using "a material portion of the NOLs reflected on our balance sheet." Stock-based compensation tied to RSUs "has resulted in and will continue to result in significant increases in our expenses." The company also disclosed a shift in payment terms, with some customers electing "annual, monthly, or consumption payments instead of multi-year upfront payments," which affects period cash flow. Finally, the governance structure compounds the risk for outside investors: Class B holders control approximately 85% of voting power, and the company's directors, officers, and affiliates control approximately 83% of voting power while owning about 23% of common stock.

**Downside Protections.** The strongest mitigant in the filing is the subscription model itself. The company states that "a significant majority of our new and existing customers have adopted RSC on a SaaS subscription basis" and that SaaS subscriptions "represented a majority of our total revenue" by the end of fiscal 2026. Because SaaS revenue is recognized ratably over multi-year terms, revenue is more predictable than a transactional hardware sale. The subscription structure also means that the contracted base is retained over time, and the filing notes that customers "tend to expand their usage... over time as the amount of data they need to protect grows." The company also reports a deliberate debt structure: the Notes carry a 2030 maturity and the company terminated its prior credit facility. The capped call transactions are described as intended to "reduce the potential dilution" from conversions, though the filing notes counterparty risk. Rubrik does not hedge foreign currency, and it states that its solutions are billed in U.S. dollars, which removes direct revenue currency risk. The filing does not report a cash balance or total liquidity in the material provided, so the adequacy of these protections cannot be fully verified from this document alone.

## Overall Assessment

Taken together, Rubrik's risk profile is that of a fast-growing, mission-critical software vendor whose strategic value depends on trust. Its most distinctive vulnerability is that the same features that make its products valuable, including ransomware recovery and broad data coverage, also make it a visible target and expose it to outsized consequences from any failure. Its channel and hardware dependencies introduce a second layer of concentration that is unusual for a software company, and its financial position, while improving in revenue terms, still rests on continued losses, a substantial convertible debt obligation, and the successful conversion of a multi-year base to SaaS. The filing also raises governance concerns: because insiders control the large majority of votes, outside shareholders have limited influence over how these risks are managed. The downside protections the company describes are real but mostly contractual or structural, and the filing repeatedly qualifies them. An investor weighing these risks should expect that the most probable path to a material adverse outcome would be a combination of a security event, a disruption at a major partner or manufacturer, and a slowdown in renewals or expansion that reduces the recurring revenue base before profitability is secured.
