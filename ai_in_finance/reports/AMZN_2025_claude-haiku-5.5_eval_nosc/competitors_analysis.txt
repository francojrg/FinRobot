# Amazon.com, Inc. (AMZN): Financial Metrics Against Microsoft, Alphabet and Walmart

Amazon's profitability and returns improved sharply between 2022 and 2025, but its cash conversion weakened over the same period. Its FCF Conversion of 0.10 in 2025 is the lowest of the four companies that year, and its ROIC of 16.8% is the second-lowest, ahead of Walmart's 17.1% by only 0.3 points. Its EV/EBITDA multiple fell from 24.68 in 2022 to 15.38 in 2025, which is now the lowest of the four in 2025. The resources do not explain the cash conversion decline, so the cause cannot be identified from them.

Fiscal year ends differ across the companies, and the year labels are used here exactly as given. Amazon's data runs from 2022 to 2025, while Microsoft and Walmart also have 2026 values and Alphabet ends at 2025. Amazon therefore has no 2026 comparison. The resource does not state units for the dollar figures, so revenue, EBITDA and FCF are quoted as presented.

## Free Cash Flow Conversion

Amazon's FCF Conversion path is the most important problem in the data. The ratio was n/m in 2022, when FCF was negative at -16,893. It then rose to 1.06 in 2023 and fell to 0.55 in 2024 and 0.10 in 2025. In 2023, Amazon was the highest of the four (Microsoft 0.82, Alphabet 0.94, Walmart 1.03 in the closest comparable years, with Walmart at 1.03 just below Amazon). In 2024, Amazon at 0.55 trailed Microsoft at 0.84, Alphabet at 0.73 and Walmart at 0.97. In 2025, Amazon's 0.10 compares with Microsoft at 0.70, Alphabet at 0.55 and Walmart at 0.65.

The stated FCF Conversion values do not reconcile to FCF divided by EBITDA. Illustratively, 2025 FCF of 7,695 against EBITDA of 165,341 gives about 4.7%, not 0.10. For 2024 the same calculation gives about 26.6%, not 0.55. The resource does not define the denominator, so I use the stated values as given but cannot verify them from the other figures.

Over the same years, Amazon's EBITDA rose from 123,815 in 2024 to 165,341 in 2025, an increase of about 33.5% (illustrative: 41,526 divided by 123,815). Free cash flow fell from 32,878 to 7,695, a decline of about 77% (illustrative). The gap means that earnings growth did not reach the cash line in 2025. The resource contains no capital expenditure or working capital detail, so this document cannot say which items absorbed the cash.

## Return on Invested Capital

Amazon's ROIC moved from -1.2% in 2022 to 12.7% in 2023, 18.1% in 2024 and 16.8% in 2025. The 2025 decline of 1.3 points after the 2024 peak is the first reversal in the series. Against peers, Amazon's ROIC trailed Microsoft and Alphabet in every year shown. In 2023, Amazon's 12.7% compared with Microsoft's 29.2%, Alphabet's 25.1% and Walmart's 11.0%. In 2024, the figures were 18.1% against Microsoft's 28.3%, Alphabet's 29.9% and Walmart's 14.7%. In 2025, Amazon's 16.8% compared with Microsoft's 26.8%, Alphabet's 28.7% and Walmart's 17.1%. Amazon was above Walmart in 2023 and 2024 and marginally below it in 2025.

The ROIC series shows a company that moved from loss to returns in the low to high teens within two years, but its 2025 level is still well below the two software and advertising-heavy peers.

## Revenue and Revenue Growth

Amazon's revenue grew from 513,983 in 2022 to 574,785 in 2023, 637,959 in 2024 and 716,924 in 2025. Reported growth was 11.8% in 2023, 11.0% in 2024 and 12.4% in 2025. Growth therefore re-accelerated in 2025 after a slight slowdown in 2024, and it stayed in a narrow band of 11% to 12.4% across the three years shown.

Against peers, Amazon's growth was below Microsoft in every year where both are available. Microsoft grew 15.7% in 2024, 14.9% in 2025 and 17.8% in 2026. In 2023, Amazon's 11.8% was ahead of Alphabet's 8.7%, and in 2024 Amazon's 11.0% trailed Alphabet's 13.9%. In 2025 Amazon's 12.4% was below Alphabet's 15.1%. Walmart's growth was lower than Amazon's across all overlapping years, falling from 6.0% in 2024 to 5.1% in 2025 and 4.7% in 2026. Amazon's revenue base is the largest of the four in the shared years, but its growth is at the lower end of the group's range.

## Gross Margin

Amazon's Gross Margin rose each year: 43.8% in 2022, 47.0% in 2023, 48.9% in 2024 and 50.3% in 2025. The increase of 6.5 percentage points over three years is the clearest sustained improvement in the Amazon data. The resource does not break down the cost of revenue, so the drivers of the improvement cannot be named from it.

Amazon's gross margin remained well below Microsoft and Alphabet. In 2023 the gap was 22.0 points to Microsoft's 68.9% and 9.6 points to Alphabet's 56.6%. In 2024 Amazon's 48.9% trailed Microsoft's 69.8% by 20.9 points and Alphabet's 58.2% by 9.3 points. In 2025 the gaps were 18.5 points to Microsoft's 68.8% and 9.4 points to Alphabet's 59.7%. Amazon was well above Walmart in each year, where Walmart's margin ranged from 24.1% to 24.9%. Amazon's margin also narrowed the gap to Alphabet in 2025, though it did not close it.

Microsoft's gross margin declined from 69.8% in 2024 to 68.8% in 2025 and 67.9% in 2026, a small reversal in a company that is otherwise at the top of the group.

## EBITDA Margin

Amazon's EBITDA Margin rose from 7.5% in 2022 to 15.6% in 2023, 19.4% in 2024 and 23.1% in 2025. The 2025 figure is more than triple the 2022 level. Against peers, Amazon remained below Microsoft and Alphabet in every year. In 2023, Amazon's 15.6% compared with Microsoft's 49.6%, Alphabet's 31.9% and Walmart's 4.9%. In 2024, it was 19.4% against Microsoft's 53.7%, Alphabet's 38.7% and Walmart's 5.6%. In 2025, Amazon's 23.1% compared with Microsoft's 55.2%, Alphabet's 44.9% and Walmart's 6.2%.

Amazon is clearly ahead of Walmart on this measure, but in the most recent year its EBITDA margin was about 22 points below Alphabet and about 32 points below Microsoft. The gap to Alphabet narrowed from 16.3 points in 2023 to 21.8 points in 2025 instead, meaning Alphabet's margin expanded faster over the period. Amazon's gap to Alphabet widened over the years, even as its own margin improved by 7.5 points.

## EV/EBITDA

Amazon's EV/EBITDA moved from 24.68 in 2022 to 18.34 in 2023, 19.19 in 2024 and 15.38 in 2025. The multiple rose in 2024 while EBITDA continued to grow. In 2023 and 2024, Amazon's multiple was above Alphabet's and below Microsoft's. In 2023, the comparison was 18.34 for Amazon, 24.31 for Microsoft, 17.8 for Alphabet and 14.58 for Walmart. In 2024 it was 19.19 for Amazon, 25.6 for Microsoft, 17.07 for Alphabet and 13.61 for Walmart. In 2025 Amazon's 15.38 was the lowest of the four, compared with Microsoft's 23.98, Alphabet's 21.1 and Walmart's 19.96. In 2022, Amazon's 24.68 was above Alphabet's 13.4, the only other company with a 2022 value.

On this measure, Amazon was the cheapest of the four in 2025, and the market has priced it at a lower multiple than in any prior year in the table. Whether that reflects undervaluation depends on whether the market expects the cash conversion problem to persist. The EV/EBITDA ratio alone does not settle it.

Amazon's EV/Sales multiple moved from 1.84 in 2022 to 3.72 in 2024 and 3.55 in 2025, so the sales-based multiple declined only slightly from its 2024 peak. This suggests that the EV/EBITDA decline in 2025 came from EBITDA growing faster than enterprise value, not from a broad repricing of the business's revenue.

## Conclusion

Across the metrics, Amazon's financial position improved in its profitability and returns. Revenue grew by about 40% from 2022 to 2025 (illustrative: 716,924 divided by 513,983 gives 1.395), its gross margin gained 6.5 points, its EBITDA margin rose from 7.5% to 23.1%, and its ROIC turned positive and reached the mid-to-high teens. Its growth rate of 11% to 12.4% remained below Microsoft and Alphabet, and its margins and returns remained below Microsoft and Alphabet throughout.

The weakest part of the record is cash generation. Free cash flow fell from 32,878 in 2024 to 7,695 in 2025, and FCF Conversion fell to 0.10, the lowest among the four companies. This decline came in a year when EBITDA rose by about a third, so the gap between reported earnings and cash is the central issue in the data.

The current valuation is consistent with the improvement in earnings and inconsistent with the cash flow trend. An EV/EBITDA of 15.38 is the lowest multiple in Amazon's series and the lowest among the four companies in 2025. The reduced multiple is consistent with investors discounting the cash flow weakness and pricing in the lower conversion. It is also consistent with a market that has not yet priced in the full benefit of the margin expansion. The data alone cannot distinguish these views.

Several observations would show this assessment is wrong. If Amazon's FCF Conversion returns to around 0.5 or higher while EBITDA keeps growing, the 2025 cash shortfall would look temporary and the low multiple would look too cheap. If FCF Conversion stays near 0.1 while the EBITDA margin keeps rising, the gap would point to a structural difference between reported earnings and cash, and the multiple would be harder to justify. A reversal in the gross margin trend, which has risen in each year, would also contradict the view that the operating improvements are durable. A decline in ROIC below its 2025 level of 16.8% would indicate that the capital base is growing faster than returns.
