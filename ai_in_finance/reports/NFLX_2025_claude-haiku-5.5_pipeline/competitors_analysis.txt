# Netflix (NFLX) against Warner Bros. Discovery, Paramount Skydance and Disney, fiscal 2022 to 2025

Netflix leads the four companies on margins, returns and growth in the years where the data overlap, and its revenue reached 45,183 in fiscal 2025, up 15.9% from 39,001 in 2024. Its problems are in cash conversion and in a valuation that has roughly doubled its EV/EBITDA multiple since 2022 while the operating metrics improved by smaller amounts. The most important caveat is that the resource does not define the FCF Conversion, Gross Margin or EBITDA metrics, and several of its figures cannot be reproduced from the other figures in the table. Comparisons below use the figures as given and treat them as the companies' own reported measures. The resource gives no currency or unit label, so absolute amounts are discussed as provided.

## Revenue and revenue growth

Netflix revenue rose each year, from 31,616 in 2022 to 33,723 in 2023, 39,001 in 2024 and 45,183 in 2025. Growth slowed to 6.7% in 2023 and then accelerated to 15.6% in 2024 and 15.9% in 2025. The 2023 figure is the weak point of the series. The three-year compound growth rate from 2022 to 2025 works out to roughly 12.6% (illustrative calculation from the endpoints), which is well above the other three companies over the same span.

The comparison companies moved in the opposite direction. Warner Bros. Discovery (WBD) grew 22.2% in 2023 to 41,321, then declined 4.8% in 2024 and 5.1% in 2025, ending at 37,296, only 10.3% above its 2022 level of 33,817. Paramount Skydance (PSKY) grew 5.5% in 2022 to 30,154, then declined 1.7% in 2023 and 1.5% in 2024, to 29,213. Disney (DIS) grew 2.8% in 2024, 3.4% in 2025 and 8.4% in the 2026 figure, rising from 88,898 in 2023 to 102,344. Disney is roughly twice Netflix's size by revenue, while Netflix is larger than WBD and PSKY in each year they overlap.

In 2022, Netflix's revenue of 31,616 was below WBD's 33,817 and above PSKY's 30,154. By 2024 Netflix's 39,001 exceeded WBD's 39,321 only narrowly, and in 2025 Netflix (45,183) was ahead of WBD (37,296) by about 21%. The growth gap is the central fact: Netflix is the only one of the four whose revenue grew in both 2024 and 2025 by more than 15%.

## Gross margin

Netflix's gross margin rose in every year, from 39.4% in 2022 to 41.5%, 46.1% and 48.5% in 2025, an increase of 9.1 percentage points. Gross profit grew faster than revenue after 2023: it rose 12.5% in 2023, 28.2% in 2024 and 22.0% in 2025, reaching 21,908 in 2025.

In 2022 Netflix's gross margin of 39.4% was 0.2 points below WBD's 39.6%. In 2023 Netflix (41.5%) was ahead of WBD (40.6%), and the gap widened to 4.5 points in 2024 (46.1% against 41.6%) and 4.5 points in 2025 (48.5% against 44.0%). Disney's margin was 33.4% in 2023, 35.8% in 2024 and 37.8% in 2025, still well behind Netflix in each year. Paramount's margin fell from 37.9% in 2021 to 34.2% in 2022, 24.5% in 2023 and 29.6% in 2024, so it lagged all others in the years it reported, with 2023 as its low point.

The resource does not say what goes into cost of revenue for each company. Netflix's EBITDA margin is above its gross margin in every year (for example, 67.0% against 48.5% in 2025), which means the two metrics are not measured on the same basis. Gross margin differences between companies should therefore be read as indicative, not exact.

## EBITDA margin

Netflix's EBITDA margin was 64.3% in 2022, 63.8% in 2023, 67.5% in 2024 and 67.0% in 2025. Its first problem is the 2023 dip of 0.5 points, which came in a year when revenue growth slowed to 6.7%. The second is the decline of 0.5 points in 2025 from the 2024 peak. Over the full period the margin improved by 2.7 points. The resource does not disclose the cause of either movement. The 2025 divergence is notable because gross margin rose 2.4 points in the same year while EBITDA margin eased, and the data offers no explanation for that difference.

Netflix's margin was far above the others in every year. WBD's EBITDA margin was 41.9% in 2022, 54.1% in 2023, 29.5% in 2024 and 57.0% in 2025. The 2024 drop to 29.5% is the largest swing in the peer set, and the 2025 recovery to 57.0% did not restore the gap to Netflix, which stood 10.0 points higher. Disney's margin rose from 13.6% in 2023 to 16.0% in 2024, 20.3% in 2025 and 21.2% in 2026, which is a steady improvement but remains about 47 points below Netflix in 2025. Paramount's margin fell from 23.0% in 2021 to 8.5% in 2022, 0.3% in 2023 and negative 16.9% in 2024, when its EBITDA was negative 4,925. Netflix's EBITDA in 2025 (30,255) exceeded Disney's (19,141), despite Disney's revenue being more than twice as large.

## Free cash flow conversion

Netflix's FCF Conversion was 0.36 in 2022, 1.28 in 2023, 0.79 in 2024 and 0.86 in 2025. The 2022 figure was low, and the 2024 decline from 1.28 to 0.79 is the main problem in the series. Free cash flow was flat in 2024 (6,922 against 6,926 in 2023) while revenue grew 15.6%, so the conversion fell. Free cash flow then rose 36.7% to 9,461 in 2025, and conversion recovered to 0.86. The resource does not explain the flat 2024 cash flow.

The definition is the main limitation. Netflix's free cash flow divided by its EBITDA gives 8% in 2022 (1,619 over 20,333), 32% in 2023 and 26% in 2024, none of which match the reported conversion figures. The reported ratios therefore use a different denominator that the resource does not name, so they are not a simple FCF-to-EBITDA ratio. The same pattern appears for Disney: its 2023 conversion is 2.08, but free cash flow of 4,897 is only 40% of its 2023 EBITDA of 12,111.

Against the peers, Disney was higher in 2023 (2.08 against 1.28) and 2024 (1.72 against 0.79), and Netflix was slightly ahead in 2025 (0.86 against 0.81 for Disney and 1.07 for Disney's 2026 figure). WBD's conversion is n/m in 2022 through 2024 and 4.25 in 2025; the resource does not say why the earlier years are not meaningful, and the 2025 figure is high relative to WBD's free cash flow of 3,088 in that year. Paramount's conversion was 0.13 in 2021 and negative 0.13 in 2022, and the resource shows n/m for 2023 and 2024, when its free cash flow was positive but small (147 and 489). Netflix's cash flow was the strongest in absolute terms among these companies in 2025, at 9,461, ahead of Disney's 10,077 only in the 2025 column, where Disney is larger. Disney's 2025 free cash flow of 10,077 exceeded Netflix's 9,461, so Netflix is not the largest generator of free cash flow in that year.

## Return on invested capital

Netflix's ROIC rose every year: 14.4% in 2022, 17.2% in 2023, 23.1% in 2024 and 28.4% in 2025. This is the clearest improvement in the dataset. Netflix's 2025 return is more than four times Disney's 6.8% and seven times WBD's 4.0% in the same year. Over 2022 through 2025, Disney's ROIC rose from 3.3% in 2023 to 7.7% in 2026, a steady gain from a low base. WBD's ROIC was negative in 2022 (negative 6.1%), 2023 (negative 1.5%) and 2024 (negative 9.5%) before turning positive at 4.0% in 2025. Paramount's ROIC was 4.6% in 2022, negative 0.6% in 2023 and negative 16.4% in 2024.

The ROIC gain suggests that Netflix's growth in revenue, gross profit and EBITDA produced returns on capital that the peers did not match in any year. The resource does not provide invested capital, so the ROIC figures cannot be broken down into margin and capital turnover. The rise to 28.4% is consistent with the margin and revenue trends above, but it cannot be attributed to a specific component from this data.

## Valuation: EV/EBITDA and EV/Sales

Netflix's EV/EBITDA was 6.91 in 2022, 10.14 in 2023, 14.78 in 2024 and 13.26 in 2025. The multiple roughly doubled from 2022 to 2024 and eased in 2025. EV/Sales followed the same pattern, rising from 4.44 to 9.98 and then to 8.88. The EV/EBITDA and EV/Sales figures are internally consistent with the EBITDA margin (for example, 8.88 divided by 13.26 gives 67%, matching the 2025 margin), so the valuation inputs and the operating data come from the same basis.

Only Disney has a usable comparison. Disney traded at 15.2 times EBITDA in 2023, 14.81 in 2024, 12.76 in 2025 and 10.25 in its 2026 figure. Relative to Disney, Netflix was cheaper in 2023 (10.14 against 15.2), roughly in line in 2024 (14.78 against 14.81) and slightly more expensive in 2025 (13.26 against 12.76). Netflix's EV/Sales of 8.88 in 2025 compares with Disney's 2.59, so on sales Netflix is priced at about 3.4 times Disney's multiple. That gap reflects the margin difference: Netflix's EBITDA margin is more than three times Disney's, and EV/Sales is EV/EBITDA multiplied by margin.

WBD shows n/a for all years for EV/EBITDA and EV/Sales, and Paramount shows n/a in 2021 through 2024 for EV/EBITDA, with n/m in 2024. The resource therefore does not allow a valuation comparison with these two companies. Judgments that Netflix is over or under-valued against WBD or Paramount cannot be made from this data.

Netflix's P/E ratio rose from 29.64 in 2022 to 40.47 in 2023 and 44.95 in 2024, before easing to 37.06 in 2025. Its P/B ratio rose from 6.32 to 15.41 in 2024 and was 14.87 in 2025. Disney's P/E was 62.83 in 2023, 35.36 in 2024, 16.72 in 2025 and 18.63 in 2026.

To see the scale of the re-rating in simple terms, Netflix's EBITDA grew 5.8% in 2023, 22.3% in 2024 and 15.0% in 2025 (illustrative calculations from the EBITDA figures). The EV/EBITDA multiple rose 114% between 2022 and 2024 while the EBITDA margin rose 3.2 points. The resource contains no share price or enterprise value, so it cannot show how much of the multiple change came from price and how much from the change in EBITDA.

## Conclusion

Netflix's financial profile over these four years is strong on profitability, returns and growth. Its revenue grew 12.6% a year on average (illustrative) from 2022 to 2025, its gross margin rose 9.1 points to 48.5%, its EBITDA margin stayed between 63.8% and 67.5%, and its ROIC increased every year to 28.4%. Against the peers, it has the highest margins and returns in every year the data overlap, and it is the only company in the set whose revenue grew more than 15% in both 2024 and 2025. The weaker points are cash conversion, which fell from 1.28 to 0.79 in 2024 and recovered only to 0.86 in 2025 on a basis the resource does not define, and a flat free cash flow in 2024. The 2025 easing of the EBITDA margin and the 2023 slowdown in revenue growth are the other setbacks in the series.

The valuation is harder to justify on the figures alone. At 13.26 times 2025 EBITDA, Netflix trades about 4% above Disney's 12.76, so the market is paying a premium for Netflix's growth and returns that is only slightly above the level of a company with a far lower margin and growth rate. The multiple has nearly doubled since 2022, while the operating improvements over the same period were a 2.7-point gain in EBITDA margin and a 9.1-point gain in gross margin. The current multiple is therefore supported by the 2025 operating results, which include 15.9% revenue growth, a 67.0% EBITDA margin and a 28.4% ROIC, but it is not explained by the trend in those metrics from 2022 alone. The 2024 peak of 14.78 is the level at which the market already priced in the 2024 improvements.

The view that Netflix's valuation is justified would be wrong if revenue growth fell back toward the 6.7% recorded in 2023, if the EBITDA margin fell further from 67.0%, if FCF conversion stayed below 1.0 for a second year, or if ROIC reversed its rise from 14.4% to 28.4%. Any of these would indicate that the 2024 and 2025 gains were not durable. The data also cannot confirm the valuation against WBD or Paramount, and the fiscal 2026 figures for Disney are labeled in the resource without stating whether they are reported results or estimates, so the Disney comparison for 2026 should be read with that uncertainty in mind.
