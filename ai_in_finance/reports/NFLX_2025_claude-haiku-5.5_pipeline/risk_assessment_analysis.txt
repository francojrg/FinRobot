# Top Three Key Risks for Netflix, Inc.

The 10-K risk factors point to three risks that are most likely to affect results: (1) failure to attract and retain members in a fiercely competitive market, (2) a large and largely fixed content commitment base combined with a planned WBD acquisition financed by substantial new debt, and (3) disruption to content supply from labor disputes, with the major U.S. guild agreements expiring in 2026. The excerpt contains no revenue, margin, or cash flow figures, and it contains no peer or industry data. Comparisons with other industries below are therefore qualitative, drawn only from what the filing states, and the filing's own quantities are the only numbers used.

## Risk 1: Member acquisition, retention and competition

**Industry Vertical Risk.** The filing describes entertainment video as "intensely competitive and subject to rapid change," with competitors ranging from broadcasters and cable operators to streaming providers, video game companies, social media, and piracy services. It states that piracy's "fundamental proposition to consumers" is "virtually all content for free." The filing does not compare this vertical's risk with other sectors, so the relative assessment rests on the filing's language. The competitive pressure described is broader than in a single-product market because the company competes for a share of limited leisure time, not only against other video services.

**Cyclicality.** The filing ties membership cancellations partly to "a perception that they do not use the service sufficiently" and "that they need to cut household expenses," and it says adverse macroeconomic conditions, including inflation, may impair member attraction and retention. Demand for advertising is described as subject to "seasonal, cyclical or other shifts in advertising spend, including the impact of macroeconomic conditions." Because subscription revenue is paid by individual households, a weaker consumer environment would reach revenue through cancellations rather than through a contractual backlog.

**Risk Quantification.** The filing does not quantify membership, churn, or pricing sensitivity, so no rate-based figure can be stated from the excerpt. The filing does identify the mechanisms: slower growth in long-operated or highly penetrated countries, competitor pricing and content, adjustments to pricing and plans (including the lower-priced ad-supported plan), and stricter enforcement against multi-household sharing. It also states that "predicting consumer adoption" of the ad-supported plan and new revenue streams "is inherently difficult given the lack of operating history." The advertising business, which the company describes as having "limited experience and operating history," is the newest of these exposures.

**Downside Protections.** The filing offers only partial mitigants. It cites original and exclusive programming as a differentiator, partner billing arrangements that place the service on cable and telecom set-top boxes, and the fact that many members rejoin or arrive through word of mouth. It does not describe diversification across member segments as a formal protection. The mitigants are real but are described qualitatively, and the filing's own language identifies the fixed content cost base as a limit on how quickly spending can be adjusted if growth slows.

## Risk 2: Leverage, fixed content commitments and the WBD transaction

**Industry Vertical Risk.** The filing describes content commitments as "multi-year" and "largely fixed," with payments "not tied to member usage or the size of our membership base." A business whose largest cost is committed in advance has less ability to cut costs in a downturn than one whose costs scale with revenue. The filing says the company "may be unable to react to any reduction in our cash flows from operations... by reducing our streaming content obligations in the near-term."

**Cyclicality.** The filing links debt capacity to market conditions. It states that "rising interest rates or any disruption in the capital markets could make it more difficult and expensive" to raise capital or refinance, and that fluctuations in currency and inflation can affect obligations. Any refinancing "could be at higher interest rates and may require us to comply with more onerous covenants." Leverage therefore compounds the cyclical risk in Risk 1: weaker revenue, lower cash flow, and tighter credit could occur together.

**Risk Quantification.** The filing provides the following figures as of December 31, 2025:
- Senior notes outstanding: the equivalent of $14.5 billion aggregate principal, some denominated in non-U.S. currencies.
- Revolving credit facility: $3 billion unsecured, with no amount borrowed.
- Total content liabilities on the balance sheet: approximately $5.7 billion, excluding streaming content commitments that do not meet liability recognition criteria, which the filing describes as "significant."

For the WBD transaction, the filing reports commitments of up to a $42.2 billion senior unsecured bridge term loan, a $5 billion revolving credit facility, and a $20 billion delayed draw term loan. Illustratively, these commitments sum to $67.2 billion, which is an arithmetic total of commitments and not an expected borrowing level. The filing also states that the company expects to assume additional WBD debt at closing and that the terms "could vary materially and may include secured debt and/or debt with restrictive covenants." If the transaction does not complete, the company's obligation could include a $5.8 billion termination fee. Illustratively, that fee equals roughly 40 percent of the existing $14.5 billion of notes. The filing also states the company may face "significant" financial and operational adjustments after closing as a condition of regulatory approval.

**Downside Protections.** The $3 billion revolver was undrawn at year end, which provides liquidity capacity that the filing does not quantify against near-term obligations. The filing does not give a maturity schedule, covenant terms, or the ratio of debt to cash flow, so the adequacy of these protections cannot be measured from the excerpt. The filing's statement that "we may need to refinance or restructure" obligations indicates that these protections are not assured.

## Risk 3: Labor disputes and content supply

**Industry Vertical Risk.** The filing states that the company and its partners engage writers, directors, actors and other talent under collective bargaining agreements, and that the WGA agreement expires May 1, 2026, with the SAG-AFTRA and DGA agreements expiring June 30, 2026. Unlike Risks 1 and 2, this risk is event-driven and dated. The filing states that "work stoppages have resulted, and may in the future result, in halted productions and delays," so the risk has occurred before, which distinguishes it from a purely hypothetical exposure.

**Cyclicality.** Labor risk is not directly tied to the economic cycle. It is tied to bargaining cycles in the entertainment industry, which the filing describes as recurring. The filing does not connect labor actions to economic downturns.

**Risk Quantification.** The key dated exposures are the May 1, 2026 WGA expiration and the June 30, 2026 SAG-AFTRA and DGA expirations. The filing identifies three channels of impact: renewal "on terms that are unfavorable to us," strikes or work stoppages that halt production, and higher costs or operating complexity. Because the company's original content is a stated differentiator, a production delay affects the same member-retention mechanism described in Risk 1. The filing does not estimate the cost or duration of a stoppage.

**Downside Protections.** The filing does not describe insurance or contractual protection against production stoppages. It states the company "may not be indemnified against claims or costs" and "may not have insurance coverage" for certain content liabilities, which is a general statement and not specific to labor. The filing also says that the company "may decide" to discontinue or alter production of original content, which indicates that management has some discretion over the content slate, but the excerpt provides no detail on how that discretion would offset a labor-driven delay.

## Overall Assessment

The three risks are linked. Risk 1 determines whether revenue grows; Risk 2 determines how much cash is committed regardless of revenue; and Risk 3 determines whether the content slate can be delivered on schedule. The filing's own language describes a cost base that is fixed in advance and a revenue base that depends on member choices in a competitive market, which is the combination that makes the business sensitive to a slowdown in growth. The WBD transaction, as described, would increase both the debt load and the operating scope in the same period that the 2026 guild agreements expire, so a difficult outcome in one area could amplify the others.

Two additional exposures appear in the filing and deserve mention though they are not among the top three. The company states it does not carry insurance to cover expenses from cybersecurity disruptions or unauthorized access, and that it "cannot easily switch" its AWS operations to another cloud provider. These statements describe concentration and uninsured exposure that the filing treats as material in principle, although it also says that to date unauthorized releases "have not had a material impact."

The view in this assessment would be proven wrong by specific observable events. Renewal of the WGA, SAG-AFTRA and DGA agreements before their expiration dates, on terms the company describes as acceptable, would weaken the labor risk. Continued membership growth in mature markets, or evidence that the ad-supported plan and pricing changes are retaining members without increasing cancellations, would weaken Risk 1. Completion of the WBD transaction on financing terms without restrictive covenants, together with a visible path to reduce the $14.5 billion of notes, would weaken Risk 2. Conversely, a revenue decline that coincided with a draw on the revolver, a strike that delayed releases, or a termination fee becoming payable would confirm the linkage described above. The excerpt does not contain the financial statements needed to measure these outcomes, so the assessment should be checked against the Management's Discussion and Analysis and the notes referenced in the filing.
