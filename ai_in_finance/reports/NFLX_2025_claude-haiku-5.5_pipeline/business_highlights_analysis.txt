Netflix reports one operating segment, so the business lines below are its four regional streaming revenue lines. Total streaming revenue was $45.18 billion in 2025, up 16% from $39.00 billion in 2024 (17% in constant currency). Latin America was the weakest region on a reported basis, so it comes first.

**Latin America (LATAM)**
Revenue was $5.36 billion in 2025, up 11% from $4.84 billion in 2024, the slowest reported growth of the four regions, though it grew 23% in constant currency. The gap comes from foreign exchange: the constant currency adjustment added $457 million to 2025 revenue, which means currency translation reduced reported LATAM revenue by roughly 12 percentage points of growth.

**Europe, Middle East, and Africa (EMEA)**
Revenue was $14.51 billion in 2025, up 17% from $12.39 billion in 2024, and 16% in constant currency. Currency was a modest tailwind: the constant currency adjustment reduced 2025 revenue by $374 million, so reported growth ran about one point ahead of the underlying rate.

**United States and Canada (UCAN)**
Revenue was $19.96 billion in 2025, up 15% from $17.36 billion in 2024, and 15% in constant currency, making UCAN the largest region by revenue. Reported and constant currency growth were the same, so foreign exchange had no effect on the year-over-year change, and the regional hedging adjustments were small.

**Asia-Pacific (APAC)**
Revenue was $5.35 billion in 2025, up 21% from $4.41 billion in 2024, and 22% in constant currency, the second-fastest underlying growth after LATAM. Currency slightly reduced reported growth by about one point, and the 10-K does not break out the regional drivers beyond the company-wide factors of membership growth, price increases, and advertising revenue.
