# Adobe Inc. against Autodesk, Salesforce and Intuit: fiscal 2022 to 2025

Adobe Inc. (ADBE) leads its peer group on EBITDA margin and return on invested capital (ROIC) in the most recent year, and it trades at the lowest EV/EBITDA multiple of the four companies in fiscal 2025, at 13.59. Its weakest points in the data are a dip in fiscal 2024 EBITDA margin and a fiscal 2023 free cash flow (FCF) conversion ratio that was the lowest in the group that year. Adobe's revenue grew steadily, by roughly 10% to 11% in each reported year, which is slower than Intuit Inc. (INTU) and Autodesk, Inc. (ADSK) in fiscal 2025 but faster than Salesforce, Inc. (CRM). The data supports a picture of a highly profitable business whose multiple fell sharply in fiscal 2025 without a stated cause. Two data problems also need flagging before the comparison. The Salesforce fiscal 2023 values look anomalous, and the FCF conversion figures do not reconcile to FCF divided by EBITDA from the same tables (see the note at the end).

## Year-over-year trends for Adobe

Revenue rose from 17,606 in fiscal 2022 to 19,409 in fiscal 2023, 21,505 in fiscal 2024 and 23,769 in fiscal 2025. Reported growth was 10.2%, 10.8% and 10.5%, respectively. Growth has been stable rather than accelerating. The compound annual growth rate from fiscal 2022 to fiscal 2025 is about 10.5% (illustrative, calculated from the revenue figures).

Gross margin improved each year, from 87.7% in fiscal 2022 to 87.9%, 89.0% and 89.3% in fiscal 2023 through fiscal 2025. The step-up in fiscal 2024 was the largest single move.

EBITDA margin was 39.6% in fiscal 2022 and 40.1% in fiscal 2023. It fell to 37.0% in fiscal 2024, then recovered to 41.3% in fiscal 2025. EBITDA in dollars rose from 6,976 to 9,815 over the period, but the fiscal 2024 margin dip shows that cost growth ran ahead of revenue growth in that year. The data does not say why.

FCF conversion was 1.56 in fiscal 2022, 1.28 in fiscal 2023, 1.42 in fiscal 2024 and 1.38 in fiscal 2025. Free cash flow in dollars was 7,396, 6,942, 7,873 and 9,852. The fiscal 2023 decline in FCF was the only year-over-year drop in the series.

ROIC rose steadily from 26.6% in fiscal 2022 to 27.4% and 28.8%, then jumped to 41.4% in fiscal 2025. The fiscal 2025 step is the largest move in the metric and is not explained in the resources. Because the figure rose 12.6 percentage points in one year, it warrants checking against the underlying invested capital figures before it is treated as a trend.

EV/EBITDA was 22.9 in fiscal 2022, 35.32 in fiscal 2023 and 28.4 in fiscal 2024. It fell to 13.59 in fiscal 2025. The fiscal 2025 multiple is the lowest in the series. Price-based metrics moved the same way: the P/E ratio fell from 41.74 in fiscal 2024 to 19.17 in fiscal 2025, and the price-to-book ratio fell from 16.13 to 11.38.

## Competitor comparison by year

**Revenue.** Adobe was the second-largest company in the group in every year with data for all four companies. In fiscal 2025 its revenue of 23,769 trailed only Salesforce's 37,895 and exceeded Intuit's 18,831 and Autodesk's 6,131. Adobe's growth rate of 10.5% in fiscal 2025 was below Intuit's 15.6% and Autodesk's 11.5%, and above Salesforce's 8.7%. In fiscal 2024, Adobe's 10.8% growth was ahead of Autodesk's 9.8% and Salesforce's 11.2% was marginally higher, while Intuit grew 13.3%.

**Gross margin.** In fiscal 2025, Adobe's 89.3% ranked second behind Autodesk's 90.6%, and well ahead of Intuit's 79.6% and Salesforce's 77.2%. The same ordering holds in fiscal 2023 and fiscal 2024. Adobe's gap to Autodesk narrowed from 2.5 points in fiscal 2023 to 1.3 points in fiscal 2025, which is a result of Adobe's improvement rather than a decline at Autodesk, whose margin stayed between 90.4% and 91.0%.

**EBITDA margin.** Adobe led the group in fiscal 2025 at 41.3%, ahead of Intuit at 31.3%, Salesforce at 29.4% and Autodesk at 25.3%. The lead also held in fiscal 2024 (37.0% against Intuit's 28.1%, Salesforce's 28.6% and Autodesk's 23.0%) and in fiscal 2023 (40.1% against Intuit's 28.1%, Autodesk's 23.3% and Salesforce's 18.0%). The 2023 Salesforce figure is discussed in the data note below.

**FCF conversion.** In fiscal 2025, Adobe's 1.38 was slightly below Salesforce's 2.01 and Intuit's 1.57, and above Autodesk's 1.35. In fiscal 2024, Adobe's 1.42 was behind Salesforce's 2.3 and Intuit's 1.56, and ahead of Autodesk's 1.38. In fiscal 2023, Adobe's 1.28 was the lowest of the four companies with values, below Autodesk's 2.46 and Intuit's 2.01. Salesforce's fiscal 2023 value of 30.35 is not usable as given. Adobe's ratio has lagged most peers for most of the period, though the gap is small in fiscal 2024 and 2025.

**ROIC.** In fiscal 2025, Adobe's 41.4% was well ahead of Autodesk's 22.4%, Intuit's 15.8% and Salesforce's 9.2%. In fiscal 2024, Adobe's 28.8% led Autodesk's 21.7%, Intuit's 12.9% and Salesforce's 7.3%. In fiscal 2023 Adobe's 27.4% was second to Autodesk's 25.8%, with Intuit at 11.0% and Salesforce at 2.0%.

**EV/EBITDA.** In fiscal 2025 Adobe's 13.59 was the lowest in the group. Autodesk traded at 43.63, Intuit at 37.85 and Salesforce at 29.73. In fiscal 2024, Adobe's 28.4 was above Salesforce's 27.82 but below Intuit's 40.25 and Autodesk's 43.43. In fiscal 2023, Adobe's 35.32 sat between Salesforce's 30.45 and Intuit's 36.44, with Autodesk at 40.26.

## Metric-specific insights

**EBITDA margin.** Adobe's profitability is the clearest strength in the data. In fiscal 2025 its 41.3% margin was 10 points above Intuit's 31.3% and 16 points above Autodesk's 25.3%. The margin is also the highest reported for Adobe across the series, apart from fiscal 2023. The fiscal 2024 dip to 37.0% is the one year where Adobe's margin fell, and the data does not give a cause. The fiscal 2025 recovery suggests the dip was not structural, but one year of recovery does not establish that.

**EV/EBITDA.** The fiscal 2025 multiple of 13.59 is the lowest in the group. Illustratively, if the multiple and EBITDA were measured at the same date, the implied enterprise value fell by roughly 41% from fiscal 2024 to fiscal 2025 (13.59 times 9,815 against 28.4 times 7,957), while EBITDA rose 23%. The resources do not say at what date each multiple was measured, so this comparison should be treated as directional. Relative to peers, Adobe screens as the cheapest of the four on this measure in fiscal 2025, while it screened in the middle of the group in fiscal 2023 and 2024. Whether that makes Adobe undervalued depends on whether its margins and returns hold, which the data supports for the years shown but which cannot be confirmed beyond fiscal 2025 for Adobe because the resources contain no fiscal 2026 figures for Adobe.

**FCF conversion.** Adobe's cash conversion has been in a narrow band of 1.28 to 1.56 over four years, with no clear trend. Its fiscal 2023 reading was the weakest in the data and the fiscal 2024 and 2025 readings were close to peers. Adobe has not been a conversion leader in this data set. Its cash generation in dollars, however, rose from 6,942 in fiscal 2023 to 9,852 in fiscal 2025.

**Gross margin.** Adobe's gross margin rose 1.6 points over the period to 89.3%. Costs of revenue grew more slowly than revenue in each year after fiscal 2023, and the step-up in fiscal 2024 indicates a year of notable cost leverage. The margin remains close to Autodesk's and well above the other two companies, so the difference in cost structure is a feature of the peer group, not only of Adobe's own trajectory.

**ROIC.** Adobe's ROIC rose from 26.6% to 41.4% across the four years. It is the highest return in the group in fiscal 2024 and 2025, and a near-match to Autodesk in fiscal 2023. A ROIC of 41.4% indicates that each dollar of invested capital produced a large return in fiscal 2025, which is consistent with the high EBITDA margin. The fiscal 2025 jump is large enough that it should be checked against the invested capital base. If the figure reflects a smaller capital base rather than higher operating profit, the signal is weaker than it appears.

**Revenue and revenue growth.** Adobe's revenue grew at a steady rate of about 10% to 11% per year over the period, adding about 6,163 in revenue dollars from fiscal 2022 to fiscal 2025. Growth did not accelerate, and it did not slow, which makes the trajectory predictable in this data. Intuit's growth rose from 13.3% to 15.6% in fiscal 2025 before easing to 13.9% in fiscal 2026, and Autodesk's growth accelerated to 17.5% in fiscal 2026. Adobe's growth profile is therefore slower than the two fastest peers, but its base is far larger than Autodesk's and Intuit's.

## Conclusion

Adobe's financial profile in this data is strong. It combines the highest EBITDA margin in the group in fiscal 2024 and 2025, the highest ROIC in the group in the same years, a gross margin second only to Autodesk, and revenue growth that held near 10% to 11% for four years. Cash conversion is adequate but not a differentiator, and the fiscal 2023 cash flow dip and the fiscal 2024 margin dip are the two weak points in the series.

The fiscal 2025 EV/EBITDA of 13.59 is the lowest in the group, and the fundamentals in the data do not explain the decline in that multiple. Revenue growth and margins in fiscal 2025 were at or near their highest levels for the period, so the drop in the multiple points to a lower valuation relative to earnings rather than to weaker operations. The data therefore supports the view that Adobe's fundamentals justify a multiple at or above the peer median, and it contradicts any view that the business has deteriorated. It does not establish that the current multiple is wrong, because the data does not show the share price date, the forward earnings expectations, or any fiscal 2026 Adobe results.

The view would be wrong if any of the following appear. EBITDA margin in the next reported year falls back toward the fiscal 2024 level of 37.0%. Revenue growth slips below 10% without an offsetting margin gain. ROIC retreats materially from 41.4% once invested capital is confirmed. Or the fiscal 2025 EV/EBITDA is revised once the measurement date and calculation basis are verified.

**Data notes.** The Salesforce fiscal 2023 figures look inconsistent with the rest of its series. Its EBITDA margin of 18.0% is followed by 28.6% in fiscal 2024, its FCF conversion of 30.35 is far outside every other reading, and its P/E of 799.86 is an outlier. These values were used only as reported and were not relied on for conclusions. The FCF conversion ratios also do not equal FCF divided by EBITDA from the same tables. For Adobe in fiscal 2025, 9,852 divided by 9,815 is about 1.00, compared with the stated 1.38. The definition of FCF conversion is not given in the resources, so the stated ratios were treated as reported. Adobe has no fiscal 2026 data in the resources, so the comparison for that year is limited to the three peers.
