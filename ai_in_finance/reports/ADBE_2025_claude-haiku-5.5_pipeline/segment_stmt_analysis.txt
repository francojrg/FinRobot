# Adobe Inc.: Segment Analysis, Fiscal 2025 (FY2025 vs. FY2024)

Adobe reports three segments: Digital Media, Digital Experience, and Publishing and Advertising. The filing does not disclose segment operating profit, so the analysis below relies on segment revenue and subscription revenue.

## Digital Media

Segment revenue grew $1.79 billion, or 11%, to $17.65 billion from $15.86 billion. Subscription revenue rose 12% to $17.39 billion. Management attributed the growth to Creative Cloud Pro, flagship apps, and Acrobat. Digital Media ARR reached $19.20 billion, up 11.5%. Segment operating profit is not disclosed.

## Digital Experience

Segment revenue rose $498 million, or 9%, to $5.86 billion from $5.37 billion. Subscription revenue rose 11% to $5.41 billion, driven by GenStudio solutions and Adobe Experience Platform. Growth trailed Digital Media's 11%. Segment operating profit is not disclosed.

## Publishing and Advertising

Segment revenue fell $19 million, or 7%, to $256 million from $275 million, continuing a decline from $300 million in FY2023. Subscription revenue fell 4% to $106 million. The filing gives no specific cause. The segment holds legacy products, including eLearning, web conferencing, and Adobe Advertising.

## Company-Wide Cost, Profit, and Policy Context

Net income was $7.13 billion, up 28% from $5.56 billion. Reported operating income was $6.74 billion in FY2024, which included the $1 billion Figma termination fee; FY2025 operating income of $8.71 billion is 29% higher. Excluding that fee, total operating expenses would have risen from $11.41 billion to $12.51 billion, about 10% (illustrative calculation). Sales and marketing rose 13% to $6.49 billion, mainly from advertising. Research and development rose 9% to $4.29 billion, mainly from compensation. Subscription cost of revenue rose 13% to $2.03 billion, with hosting and data center costs contributing 8 points. The effective tax rate fell to 18% from 20% because the prior-year fee was not deductible. The OECD 15% global minimum tax had no material effect in FY2025. The 2025 U.S. Tax Act restores immediate R&D expensing from FY2026, and management expects lower cash tax rates after FY2025.

## Gaps in the Provided Resources

The filing provides no market share data, no named strategic partners, and no segment-level cost allocation. The only named transaction is the pending Semrush acquisition for about $1.9 billion in cash, expected to close in the first half of FY2026. A slowdown in Digital Media ARR growth below the reported 11.5% would weaken the main thesis above.
