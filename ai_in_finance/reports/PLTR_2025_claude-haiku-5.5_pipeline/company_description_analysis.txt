**Palantir Technologies Inc.: company profile**

Founded in 2003, Palantir Technologies Inc. is a software company that provides platforms for integrating data, decisions, and operations at scale for government and commercial customers. It began building software for U.S. intelligence counterterrorism work and later extended it to commercial enterprises. Revenue was $4.5 billion in 2025, up 56% from $2.9 billion in 2024, and the customer count rose to 954 from 711.

**Products and strengths:** Foundry is the core data operations platform, built around the Ontology, which maps data, logic, and actions into a shared operating representation. Gotham serves defense and intelligence users. AIP connects third-party large language models to customer data with governance and audit controls. Apollo handles continuous delivery across environments. In 2025, gross margin was 82% (84% excluding stock-based compensation), and contribution margin was 66%, up from 60%.

**Competition:** The 10-K states that the company competes mainly with customers' internal software development. It also names large enterprise software vendors, government contractors, and system integrators. Some competitors have greater name recognition, larger sales budgets, and more resources. The company says it targets large, difficult projects that smaller vendors avoid.

**End market and customers:** In 2025, 54% of revenue came from government and 46% from commercial customers. U.S. customers provided 74% of revenue, and trailing-twelve-month U.S. revenue reached $3.3 billion, up 75%. The average revenue from the top twenty customers was $93.9 million, up 45% from $64.6 million. The 10-K does not name major customers or report market share, so market position cannot be measured from these resources.

**Trends, opportunities, and challenges:** Customers facing recent crises have shifted toward software that can be deployed in days rather than the months or years internal builds require. Government budgets and timing remain uncertain, and many contracts allow termination for convenience. Geopolitical conflicts, interest rates, and currency moves are cited as risks.

**Strategic initiatives:** AIP has been deployed since 2023, supported by AIP bootcamps and a Developer Tier. The company is building industry operating systems in sectors such as airlines, shipbuilding, and healthcare, and has a strategic partnership with Fujitsu. In January 2026 it terminated its share repurchase program after buying back $75.0 million of stock in 2025.

---

**Summary**

Palantir's 2025 revenue rose 56% to $4.5 billion, and operating income reached $1.4 billion against $310 million in 2024. Growth comes from government and commercial customers in roughly equal measure. Risks include termination-for-convenience clauses, uncertain government budgets, and competition from customers' internal builds. The 10-K gives no market share figure. The view would be wrong if remaining deal value growth (up 105% to $11.2 billion) fell below revenue growth, if the top-twenty customer average stopped rising, or if contribution margin reversed from 66%.
