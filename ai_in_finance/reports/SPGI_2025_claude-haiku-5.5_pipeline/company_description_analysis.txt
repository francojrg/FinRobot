**Company overview and industry**

Incorporated in December 1925 under New York law, S&P Global Inc. is a global, diversified provider of benchmarks, data, analytics and workflow solutions to the capital, energy and commodity, and automotive markets. It runs five businesses: Market Intelligence, Ratings, Energy, Mobility and Indices. 2025 revenue was $15,336 million, up 8% from $14,208 million in 2024. Subscription revenue was 51% of the total.

**Core strengths and competitive advantages**

Indices and Ratings are the highest-margin segments. In 2025, Indices' operating margin was 69% and Ratings' was 64%. Consolidated operating margin was 42%, against 39% in 2024. Indices' asset-linked fees rose 15% to $1,206 million, driven by ETF and mutual fund assets. Ending ETF assets reached $5.480 trillion, up 25%.

**Market position and customers**

The filing describes the company as a "leading" provider in several businesses, including ETF benchmarks. It does not disclose market share figures, and it does not identify customer concentration or whether customers are blue-chip. Mobility states that its products serve all global OEMs, most of the world's leading suppliers and the majority of the top North American dealerships. Geographically, the U.S. was 61% of 2025 revenue ($9,322 million). Europe contributed $3,531 million, Asia $1,640 million and the rest of the world $843 million.

**Trends, opportunities and challenges**

Total billed issuance at Ratings rose 11% to $4,327 billion, with high-yield up 17% and investment-grade up 11%. Management points to private markets, energy expansion, wealth and AI as growth areas. Challenges include regulation: the EU AI Act, DORA, the EU ESG Ratings Regulation (applying from mid-2026) and GDPR fines of up to 4% of global revenue. Mobility's non-subscription revenue was held back by tariff and EV-related budget tightening and lower recall activity. Market Intelligence faces competition from established firms and fast-moving disruptors.

**Strategic initiatives and response to conditions**

Acquisitions in late 2025 included With Intelligence for $1.8 billion, ORBCOMM's AIS business, ARC Research and McKinsey PriceMetrix. The company sold OSTTRA in October 2025 for a $270 million pre-tax gain. It sold the EDM and thinkFolio businesses in January 2026. Mobility is to be spun off in mid-2026. Shareholder returns were $5.0 billion of buybacks in 2025 and a quarterly dividend of $0.97. Operating-related expenses grew 5%, below revenue growth of 8%. Severance charges were $157 million in 2025. The OSTTRA gain contributed 8 percentage points of the 16% operating profit growth.

---

**Summary**

S&P Global's 2025 revenue grew 8% to $15,336 million, and operating profit grew 16% to $6,478 million. About 8 points of that profit growth came from the OSTTRA gain, so the MD&A's figure of 11% excluding one-time items is the better guide to underlying growth. Ratings and Indices carry the highest margins, and debt issuance and ETF assets drove their growth. Mobility's non-subscription revenue is exposed to tariffs and EV uncertainty, and it is to be spun off in mid-2026. The view would be wrong if issuance growth reverses, if ETF asset growth stalls, or if the regulatory costs cited in the filing rise materially.
