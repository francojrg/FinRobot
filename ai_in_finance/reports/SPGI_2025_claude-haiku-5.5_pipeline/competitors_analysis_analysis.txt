# S&P Global Inc. (SPGI): Financial Metrics Against Moody's, MSCI and FactSet

S&P Global is the largest company in this group by revenue and generated the most free cash flow, but it trails its closest peers on gross margin, EBITDA margin and return on invested capital (ROIC) in every year shown. Its revenue growth slowed to 7.9% in 2025, the lowest of its three growth years. Its EV/EBITDA of 20.74 in 2025 sits below Moody's Corporation (MCO) and MSCI Inc. (MSCI) and above FactSet Research Systems Inc. (FDS). The metrics support a discount to the higher-margin, higher-return peers. They do not, on their own, show whether the current multiple is fair, because the resources contain no growth forecasts for SPGI.

Fiscal year ends differ across the companies, and the resources label years as given. SPGI and MCO have data for 2022 through 2025. MSCI has the same range. FDS has 2023 through 2026 and no 2022 figures, so the 2022 comparison covers only MCO and MSCI. SPGI has no 2026 figure, so the FDS 2026 values have no SPGI counterpart. The resources do not state whether the 2026 figures are reported or projected.

## Revenue and Revenue Growth

SPGI revenue rose from 11,181 in 2022 to 12,497 in 2023, 14,208 in 2024 and 15,336 in 2025. Growth was 11.8% in 2023, 13.7% in 2024 and 7.9% in 2025. The problem in the data is the 2025 slowdown. Growth fell by 5.8 percentage points in a single year, and the 2025 rate is below the 8.9% at MCO and the 9.7% at MSCI. The resources do not give a cause for the slowdown, so none is assumed here.

Scale is the clearest advantage. In 2025 SPGI revenue of 15,336 is about twice MCO's 7,718, about 4.9 times MSCI's 3,134 and about 6.6 times FDS's 2,322. Peer growth followed different paths. MCO grew 8.2% in 2023 and 19.8% in 2024 before slowing to 8.9% in 2025. MSCI grew 12.5%, 12.9% and 9.7%. FDS grew 5.6% in 2024, 5.4% in 2025 and 6.7% in 2026. SPGI's 2025 growth is faster than FDS's but slower than MSCI's, and it is below MCO's 2024 peak in the same year.

## Gross Margin

SPGI gross margin improved steadily, from 66.4% in 2022 to 66.9% in 2023, 69.3% in 2024 and 70.2% in 2025. The gain of 3.8 percentage points over three years is the most consistent improvement among the SPGI metrics, and the 2024 step of 2.4 points is the largest single-year move.

The margin is still below the peers that report comparable figures. MCO was 70.5% in 2022 and 74.4% in 2025, so the gap narrowed from 4.1 points to 4.2 points, meaning it did not narrow at all over the period. MSCI was 82.0% in 2022 and 82.4% in 2025, roughly 12 to 16 points above SPGI. FDS was 53.3% in 2023, 54.1% in 2024, 52.7% in 2025 and 50.6% in 2026, so its margin has been falling since 2024. SPGI sits between FDS and MCO, and the resources do not explain the cost structure behind these differences.

## EBITDA Margin

The 2022 to 2025 EBITDA margin path for SPGI is uneven. It was 53.8% in 2022, fell to 41.2% in 2023, recovered to 47.7% in 2024 and reached 50.2% in 2025. The 2023 drop of 12.6 points is the largest problem in the SPGI series. Revenue rose 11.8% that year, yet EBITDA fell from 6,019 to 5,148, a decline of 14.5% (illustrative calculation from the reported figures). The resources do not explain why EBITDA fell in 2023, and this analysis does not infer a reason.

Against peers, SPGI was above MCO in 2022 (53.8% against 42.7%) and below MCO in each later year. In 2023 the gap was 3.3 points (41.2% against 44.5%), in 2024 it was 1.2 points (47.7% against 48.9%), and in 2025 it was 0.2 points (50.2% against 50.4%). The most recent year therefore shows SPGI at parity with MCO and well below MSCI, which reached 61.3% in 2025. FDS reached 41.6% in 2025, so SPGI leads FDS by about 8.6 points on this measure. The trend is positive, but the margin is roughly where MCO was in 2025 and has not returned to its 2022 level.

## EV/EBITDA

SPGI's EV/EBITDA went 19.02 (2022), 27.57 (2023), 22.91 (2024) and 20.74 (2025). The 2023 peak was driven by the multiple, since the EBITDA decline that year lowered the denominator. The multiple has compressed for two years running and is now 1.7 points above its 2022 level.

Against MCO and MSCI, SPGI traded at a discount in every year. In 2025 the discount was about 16% to MCO (20.74 against 24.59) and about 17% to MSCI (20.74 against 24.97), both illustrative calculations. In 2022 MCO traded at 24.45 and MSCI at 30.08, so the discount was already present. Against FDS, SPGI traded at a premium in every overlapping year: 22.91 against 19.70 in 2024 and 20.74 against 15.81 in 2025, a premium of about 31% in 2025 (illustrative). FDS's multiple fell from 22.85 in 2023 to 13.48 in 2026.

On these figures SPGI is not cheap relative to the group. It is cheaper than MCO and MSCI, which have higher margins and returns, and more expensive than FDS, which has a lower EBITDA margin and a lower gross margin. Whether either relationship is mispriced cannot be determined from the data.

## FCF Conversion

FCF conversion for SPGI was 0.77 in 2022, 1.36 in 2023, 1.44 in 2024 and 1.22 in 2025. The 2022 figure is the weak point. It was the lowest of the group that year, below MCO's 0.87 and MSCI's 1.17. The resources do not define the denominator, so the ratio is reported as given.

From 2023 onward SPGI converted more of its reported measure into free cash flow than most peers. In 2024 its 1.44 was the highest among the four companies, ahead of MCO at 1.22 and MSCI at 1.25. In 2025 SPGI's 1.22 was above MCO's 1.05, MSCI's 1.21 and FDS's 1.03. Free cash flow rose from 2,514 in 2022 to 5,565 in 2024 and then eased to 5,456 in 2025. Conversion is therefore strong but has moved down from its 2024 peak, and the 2025 decline in free cash flow is small. FDS's 2026 figure of 1.33 is the highest in the table, but it is one year and has no SPGI counterpart.

## ROIC

SPGI ROIC was 7.9% in 2022, 6.9% in 2023, 9.9% in 2024 and 11.4% in 2025. The 2023 dip against 2022 is a problem in the series. Recovery since then has been steady, but the 2025 level is the lowest of the four companies in every year shown. MCO rose from 15.8% to 24.3% over the same years. MSCI rose from 28.9% to 38.7%. FDS was 16.1% in 2023, 18.0% in 2024 and 18.1% in 2025, and then 17.2% in 2026.

The gap to MCO has widened. It was 7.9 percentage points in 2022 (15.8% against 7.9%) and reached 12.9 points in 2025 (24.3% against 11.4%). The gap to FDS went from 8.2 points in 2023 to 6.7 points in 2025. SPGI's returns are improving, but they are improving more slowly than those of the companies it is compared with. Since ROIC is tied to the capital base, the resources do not show whether the low level reflects acquisitions or investment that has not yet earned a return. Those causes are not in the data.

## Conclusion

SPGI's financial profile is that of a large, cash-generative business with improving margins and returns that are still below the peer group. Revenue has grown from 11,181 to 15,336 over three years. Gross margin has risen by about 3.8 points and EBITDA margin has recovered from its 2023 low to 50.2%. FCF conversion has been above 1.2 for two years. These are real strengths. The weaknesses are equally clear: growth slowed to 7.9% in 2025, the 2023 EBITDA decline has not been explained in the resources, gross and EBITDA margins still trail MCO and MSCI, and ROIC of 11.4% is the lowest in the group and has fallen further behind MCO.

The valuation is consistent with this profile. At 20.74 times EBITDA in 2025, SPGI trades below MCO and MSCI, whose higher returns and margins support higher multiples. It trades above FDS, which has a lower margin profile, and the premium over FDS is harder to justify on the metrics here, since FDS has a higher ROIC. The data is consistent with the market pricing SPGI's scale and cash generation, and with its lower returns, at a middle multiple. The data does not contain growth estimates, so it cannot test whether the current multiple is fair.

Several outcomes would show this view to be wrong. If 2026 revenue growth stays at or below 8%, the 2025 slowdown would look like a trend, not a one-year pause, and the premium to FDS would be harder to defend. If ROIC does not move meaningfully above 11.4% in the next reported year, the gap to MCO would keep widening and the discount to MCO and MSCI would be more justified. If EBITDA margin falls back below 50%, the 2025 recovery would be reversed. If gross margin continues to rise toward MCO's level, the cost gap would be closing and the case for a narrower discount would strengthen.
