# Danaher (DHR) Versus Thermo Fisher (TMO), Agilent (A) and Revvity (RVTY), Fiscal Years 2022 to 2025

Danaher's fiscal 2025 results show a company that has stopped shrinking but has not regained its earlier scale or returns. Revenue in fiscal 2025 was 24,568, still 7.8% below the 26,643 reported in fiscal 2022 (illustrative calculation from the table). Over the same period EBITDA fell from 9,482 to 6,945, a decline of 26.8%, and free cash flow fell from 7,401 to 5,260, a decline of 28.9%. Despite this, Danaher had the highest gross margin and the highest EBITDA margin among the four companies in fiscal 2025, and its EV/EBITDA multiple of 25.47 was the highest in the group. The data therefore shows a premium valuation resting on margin strength, while the operating trends that would normally support such a premium have weakened. The resources do not state the causes of these movements, so the explanations below describe what the figures show and do not attribute motives.

## Revenue and Revenue Growth

Danaher's revenue declined sharply in fiscal 2023, falling 10.3% to 23,890 from 26,643. It was flat in fiscal 2024 at a 0.1% decline, to 23,875, and then grew 2.9% to 24,568 in fiscal 2025. The decline was concentrated in one year, and the two years since have shown only modest movement. Relative to peers, Danaher's 2023 contraction was the second steepest in the group, behind Revvity's 16.9% drop. Thermo Fisher declined 4.6%, and Agilent declined 0.2%. In fiscal 2024 Danaher was flat, Thermo Fisher was up 0.1%, Revvity was up 0.2%, and Agilent fell 4.7%. In fiscal 2025 Danaher's 2.9% growth trailed Agilent's 6.7%, Revvity's 3.7% and Thermo Fisher's 3.9%. Danaher has the largest revenue base in the group, but its growth in the most recent year was below three of the four companies' rates shown in the data, and its revenue remains below its fiscal 2022 level.

## Gross Margin

Danaher's gross margin moved from 60.8% in fiscal 2022 to 58.7% in fiscal 2023, recovered to 59.5% in fiscal 2024, and eased to 59.1% in fiscal 2025. The fiscal 2023 decline of 2.1 percentage points is the main movement, and the margin has held in a narrow band since. Gross profit followed the same pattern in dollar terms: it fell 13.3% from 16,188 to 14,034 in fiscal 2023, and reached 14,523 in fiscal 2025, still below the fiscal 2022 level.

Danaher ranked first in gross margin in every year of the data. In fiscal 2022 it led Revvity (60.1%) by 0.7 points, and Agilent (54.4%) and Thermo Fisher (42.2%) were well behind. In fiscal 2023 the gap to Revvity (56.0%) was 2.7 points. In fiscal 2024 Danaher (59.5%) led Revvity (55.8%) and Agilent (54.3%). In fiscal 2025 Danaher (59.1%) led Revvity (54.8%), Agilent (52.4%) and Thermo Fisher (40.9%). Thermo Fisher's margin is about 18 points below Danaher's, which reflects a materially different cost structure. The data does not explain that difference. Danaher's gross margin is consistently high, but in fiscal 2025 it is still 1.7 points below its fiscal 2022 level.

## EBITDA Margin

Danaher's EBITDA margin declined in every year, from 35.6% in fiscal 2022 to 31.4%, 30.5% and 28.3% in fiscal 2023, 2024 and 2025. The fiscal 2025 figure is 7.3 points below the starting point. The decline is steeper than the gross margin decline over the same period (1.7 points), which means operating costs below gross profit absorbed a larger share of revenue.

Against peers, Danaher's margin position has changed. In fiscal 2022 Revvity (35.7%) was marginally ahead of Danaher, and both were well above Agilent (27.8%) and Thermo Fisher (26.6%). In fiscal 2023 Danaher (31.4%) led the group, with Revvity at 25.9%, Thermo Fisher at 25.9% and Agilent at 25.0%. In fiscal 2024 Danaher and Revvity tied at 30.5%, with Agilent at 28.8% and Thermo Fisher at 26.9%. In fiscal 2025 Danaher's 28.3% was the highest, ahead of Revvity (26.8%), Agilent (26.4%) and Thermo Fisher (25.8%). Danaher still leads on profitability, but the lead is narrower than the 2.4-point gap it held in fiscal 2023 versus its nearest peer, and the 2025 gap to Revvity is 1.5 points. Danaher's margin has fallen faster than its lead has narrowed in the sense that its margin decline has been the largest in the group. Revvity's margin also fell from its fiscal 2022 peak, but it rebounded in fiscal 2024 and then moved lower in 2025.

## EV/EBITDA

Danaher's EV/EBITDA multiple rose from 19.62 in fiscal 2022 to 24.64 in fiscal 2023, 24.76 in fiscal 2024 and 25.47 in fiscal 2025. This is an increase of about 30% over the period (illustrative calculation). The multiple rose while EBITDA fell, so the market value of the enterprise, which the data does not state directly, held up better than EBITDA over the period.

Relative to peers, Danaher was the second-lowest multiple in fiscal 2022, ahead only of Revvity (18.47), with Thermo Fisher at 20.18 and Agilent at 22.33. From fiscal 2023 onward Danaher carried the highest multiple in the group every year. In fiscal 2023 it was 24.64, against Revvity at 23.33, Thermo Fisher at 20.95 and Agilent at 18.38. In fiscal 2024 it was 24.76, against Agilent at 20.93, Thermo Fisher at 19.54 and Revvity at 18.60. In fiscal 2025 it was 25.47, against Agilent at 23.43, Thermo Fisher at 21.51 and Revvity at 17.42. The premium to the peer average in fiscal 2025 is roughly 4 turns of EBITDA when compared with the average of the other three companies (illustrative: the average of 23.43, 21.51 and 17.42 is about 20.79). On this measure Danaher is priced above its peers in every year of the data. Whether that is over- or undervaluation depends on whether the earnings base will recover, and the next section bears on that question.

## FCF Conversion

Danaher's FCF conversion rose from 1.03 in fiscal 2022 to 1.21, 1.36 and 1.46 in the following years. Over the same period its free cash flow fell each year, from 7,401 to 5,781, 5,296 and 5,260. The conversion metric therefore rose only because its denominator fell faster than free cash flow. The resources do not define the denominator. The conversion figures also do not equal free cash flow divided by EBITDA, which for Danaher in fiscal 2025 would give about 0.76 (illustrative: 5,260 divided by 6,945). The metric should be read as a ratio with an undisclosed base, and the trend is only as reliable as that base.

Relative to peers, Danaher's conversion was the highest in fiscal 2025 at 1.46, ahead of Revvity at 2.11 only if Revvity's figure were excluded, which it is not. Revvity's conversion was 2.11 in fiscal 2025 and 2.00 in fiscal 2024, which is above Danaher, but Revvity's free cash flow in those years was small (509 and 542) and its conversion reflects a very small or unusual base. In fiscal 2023 Revvity's conversion was 0.01 on free cash flow of 10. Thermo Fisher was 0.94 in fiscal 2025, down from 1.15 in fiscal 2024, and Agilent was 0.88 in fiscal 2025. Danaher's conversion trend is therefore favorable on the metric, but its absolute free cash flow has declined by about 29% since fiscal 2022.

## ROIC

Danaher's ROIC fell from 9.6% in fiscal 2022 to 6.2% in fiscal 2023, edged up to 6.3% in fiscal 2024 and declined to 5.4% in fiscal 2025. The fiscal 2025 figure is 4.2 points below fiscal 2022. The largest drop came in fiscal 2023, and the company has not recovered from it. This pattern matches the revenue and EBITDA declines.

Danaher's ROIC was below Thermo Fisher in every year: 9.9% against 9.6% in fiscal 2022, 9.0% against 6.2% in fiscal 2023, 9.5% against 6.3% in fiscal 2024, and 8.7% against 5.4% in fiscal 2025. Agilent's ROIC was the highest in the group in every year, falling from 16.4% to 13.9%. Revvity's ROIC was the lowest, at 5.0%, 2.3%, 3.4% and 3.1%. Danaher's fiscal 2025 ROIC is therefore well below the two larger peers, and only above Revvity. Its capital is being deployed at a lower return than in fiscal 2022, and the gap to Thermo Fisher has widened from 0.3 points to 3.3 points.

## Conclusion

Danaher's financial position is strong in margin terms and weaker in trend terms. Its gross margin of 59.1% and EBITDA margin of 28.3% in fiscal 2025 are the highest in the group, and it has the largest revenue base among the four companies. Those are real strengths. The problems are equally clear. Revenue is 7.8% below fiscal 2022, EBITDA is down 26.8%, free cash flow is down 28.9%, and ROIC has fallen from 9.6% to 5.4%. In each case the largest decline came in fiscal 2023, and the years since have shown stabilization rather than recovery. Revenue growth of 2.9% in fiscal 2025 is below the 3.7% to 6.7% growth at the other three companies.

Set against those operating results, a 25.47 EV/EBITDA multiple is the highest in the group and about 30% higher than the fiscal 2022 level, even though EBITDA is lower. The valuation therefore assumes that the earnings decline is temporary or that the company's margin and cash conversion will support a higher multiple than its returns currently justify. The data supports part of that view: margins are at peer-leading levels and conversion is high. It does not yet show the revenue growth or return recovery that would normally support a premium of this size. The data does not contain forward estimates, so the valuation cannot be tested directly against expected earnings.

Several results would show this view to be wrong. If revenue returns above the fiscal 2022 level of 26,643 without a corresponding margin improvement, the multiple would be supported by growth and not by margin, which would change the assessment. If the EV/EBITDA multiple remains above 25 while EBITDA continues to fall in the next reporting year, the premium would be harder to justify on the figures in this data. If ROIC does not recover toward the fiscal 2022 level of 9.6%, the company's capital efficiency would remain below Thermo Fisher's, and the premium would rest on margins alone. Conversely, a fiscal 2026 result showing EBITDA and free cash flow growing from their fiscal 2025 levels, with ROIC rising, would support the current valuation.
