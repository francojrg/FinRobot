**Company overview**

The 10-K does not state a founding year. It says the idea for Danaher originated in the early 1980s, when founders Steven M. and Mitchell P. Rales envisioned a business that would generate long-term value. Danaher is a global science and technology company with more than 15 operating companies in three segments: Biotechnology, Life Sciences and Diagnostics. It operates in the life sciences tools and clinical diagnostics industry, selling instruments, consumables, software and services, largely through direct sales. 2025 sales were $24.6 billion, up 3.0% from 2024.

**Core strengths and key products**

- The 10-K describes a high level of products sold on a recurring basis, and consumables drove core growth in Biotechnology and Diagnostics in 2025.
- The Danaher Business System (DBS) is the operating framework across all businesses.
- Key products include bioprocessing filtration, resins and single-use systems (Cytiva, Pall); mass spectrometry and flow cytometry (SCIEX, Beckman Coulter); custom DNA/RNA (IDT, Aldevron); molecular diagnostics (Cepheid); and blood gas and clinical lab systems (Radiometer, Beckman Coulter).

**Market position, end markets and customers**

Management believes Danaher holds leadership positions in many of its markets. The 10-K gives no market share figures. Customers include pharmaceutical and biopharmaceutical companies, CDMOs, hospitals, reference laboratories and academic institutions. The 10-K does not name major customers or state customer concentration, except for "two large customers" in one Life Sciences product line. Sales were 59% outside the United States and high-growth markets were about 29% of sales.

**Trends, challenges and responses**

Tariffs cost less than $300 million in 2025 and were largely offset. U.S. funding cuts affected academic and emerging biotech demand. China volume-based procurement reduced Diagnostics prices by 1.0%. Competition is rising from well-resourced entrants and low-cost manufacturers. In response, Danaher reorganized Life Sciences in Q3 2025, recorded a $432 million trade name impairment, issued CHF 1.2 billion of Swiss bonds, and paid no acquisition cash in 2025 after $558 million in 2024.

**Summary**

Danaher's 2025 sales grew 3.0% to $24.6 billion, but diluted EPS fell to $5.03 from $5.29 as impairments and Life Sciences weakness reduced profit. Biotechnology core sales rose 6.5%, while Life Sciences core sales fell 1.5% and its operating margin dropped to 7.1%. Operating cash flow was $6.4 billion, and the company returned $3.1 billion through buybacks and $878 million in dividends. The view would be wrong if Biotechnology consumables growth slows or Life Sciences core sales keep declining in 2026.
