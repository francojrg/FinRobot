# Monster Beverage Corporation (MNST): Financial Metrics Against Celsius Holdings, Inc. (CELH), The Coca-Cola Company (KO), and Keurig Dr Pepper Inc. (KDP), Fiscal 2022 to 2025

Monster Beverage Corporation grew revenue faster than The Coca-Cola Company and Keurig Dr Pepper Inc. in fiscal 2025, expanded its gross margin every year from 2022 to 2025, and raised its ROIC to 22.5% in 2025 from 17.1% in 2022. Its EBITDA margin reached 30.5% in 2025, above Keurig Dr Pepper's 25.3% but below The Coca-Cola Company's 39.0%. The weakest points in the data are the 2024 year, when EBITDA slipped and revenue growth slowed to 4.9%, and a valuation that stands at 28.77 times EBITDA in 2025, roughly 60% above The Coca-Cola Company and more than double Keurig Dr Pepper. The data does not include any explanations for the company-level changes, so the causes discussed below are limited to what the figures show.

Two comparability caveats apply throughout. The four companies report on different fiscal year ends, so the same label may cover different calendar periods. In addition, the FCF Conversion figures do not reconcile to the FCF and EBITDA amounts in the same tables. For example, Monster's 2025 FCF of 1,941 divided by its 2025 EBITDA of 2,534 gives about 0.77, not the 1.02 reported. The definition of FCF Conversion is not in the resources, so the reported ratios are used as given and are not recalculated.

## Revenue and Revenue Growth

Monster's revenue rose from 6,311 in 2022 to 7,140 in 2023, 7,493 in 2024, and 8,294 in 2025. Growth was 13.1% in 2023, slowed to 4.9% in 2024, and then recovered to 10.7% in 2025. The 2024 slowdown is the main weak point in the revenue record. The 2025 rebound restored growth to roughly the 2023 pace.

Against the peers, Monster's 2025 growth of 10.7% exceeded Keurig Dr Pepper's 8.2%, The Coca-Cola Company's 1.9%, and was below Celsius's 85.5%. In 2024 Monster's 4.9% was ahead of The Coca-Cola Company's 2.9% and Celsius's 2.9%, but behind Keurig Dr Pepper's 3.6% in that year's ranking only in the sense that Keurig Dr Pepper's rate was lower; the correct comparison is that Monster grew faster than Celsius and The Coca-Cola Company but slower than Keurig Dr Pepper's 3.6%? No: 4.9% is higher than 3.6%, so Monster was ahead of all three in 2024. In 2023, Monster's 13.1% trailed only Celsius's 101.7%. In 2022, growth is not available for any company.

Over the full period, revenue for Monster rose from 6,311 to 8,294, an increase of about 31%. Illustrative arithmetic on these endpoints gives a compound annual growth rate of roughly 9.5% over the three years from 2022 to 2025. The corresponding figures are about 3.7% for The Coca-Cola Company (43,004 to 47,941) and about 5.7% for Keurig Dr Pepper (14,057 to 16,603). Celsius grew from 654 to 2,515, an illustrative rate of roughly 57% a year, which reflects a much smaller starting base. The resources do not state what drove Celsius's revenue jump in 2023 or 2025.

## Gross Margin

Monster's gross margin improved in every year: 50.3% in 2022, 53.1% in 2023, 54.0% in 2024, and 55.8% in 2025. The total gain of 5.5 percentage points is steady and shows no reversal. In 2022 Monster trailed The Coca-Cola Company (58.1%) and was ahead of Celsius (41.4%) and Keurig Dr Pepper (52.1%) only in the case of Keurig Dr Pepper, which it exceeded by 1.8 points on that date. In 2023, Monster's 53.1% was below The Coca-Cola Company's 59.5% and Keurig Dr Pepper's 54.5%, and above Celsius's 48.0%. In 2024, Monster (54.0%) was still below Keurig Dr Pepper (55.6%) and The Coca-Cola Company (61.1%) and above Celsius (50.2%). By 2025, Monster's 55.8% exceeded Keurig Dr Pepper's 54.2% and remained below The Coca-Cola Company's 61.6%. Celsius's 50.4% was the lowest of the four in 2025.

Celsius improved its gross margin by 9.0 points between 2022 and 2025, the largest gain in the group, but its level remains below Monster's. Keurig Dr Pepper's margin declined from 55.6% in 2024 to 54.2% in 2025, the only year-over-year drop among the group in 2025. Monster's margin therefore moved in the opposite direction to Keurig Dr Pepper's in the most recent year.

## EBITDA Margin

The 2024 year shows the main problem in Monster's profitability record. EBITDA fell from 2,022 in 2023 to 2,011 in 2024, and the margin dropped from 28.3% to 26.8%, even as revenue grew 4.9%. Monster's 2022 EBITDA margin of 26.1% was the lowest of its own series, so the 2024 level was a return to a previously reached low-to-mid range, not a new floor.

In 2025 the margin recovered to 30.5%, EBITDA rose to 2,534, and the company posted its highest margin in the four-year series. Against the peers in 2025, Monster's 30.5% was above Keurig Dr Pepper's 25.3% and Celsius's 8.1%, and below The Coca-Cola Company's 39.0%. The Coca-Cola Company's margin rose from 32.2% in 2022 to 39.0% in 2025 and is the clear leader in the group. In 2022 Monster's 26.1% trailed The Coca-Cola Company's 32.2% and Keurig Dr Pepper's 22.2% was lower; Monster was ahead of Keurig Dr Pepper in that year. In 2023 Monster's 28.3% was ahead of Keurig Dr Pepper (26.8%) and behind The Coca-Cola Company (34.1%). In 2024 the ranking was Monster 26.8%, Keurig Dr Pepper 22.0%, The Coca-Cola Company 33.6%, and Celsius 14.9%.

Celsius's margin peaked at 22.4% in 2023 and fell to 8.1% in 2025 while its EBITDA stayed near 200 in 2024 and 2025. Its revenue grew much faster than its EBITDA over that period, so the margin declined even though the company's absolute revenue and EBITDA both rose from 2023 to 2025.

## EV/EBITDA

Monster's EV/EBITDA multiple was 31.42 in 2022, 28.54 in 2023, 24.86 in 2024, and 28.77 in 2025. The multiple compressed in 2023 and 2024 and then rose again in 2025. The 2025 figure is the highest in the series except for 2022.

Against the peers, Monster traded at a higher multiple than The Coca-Cola Company and Keurig Dr Pepper in every year. In 2025 the gap was 28.77 against 17.96 for The Coca-Cola Company and 12.68 for Keurig Dr Pepper. As an illustrative calculation, Monster's multiple is about 60% above The Coca-Cola Company's and about 127% above Keurig Dr Pepper's. Celsius's multiple was 59.09 in 2025, the highest in the group, after 26.30 in 2024 and 40.29 in 2023. Its 2022 multiple is not meaningful because its EBITDA was negative. In 2024 Monster (24.86) traded at a higher multiple than The Coca-Cola Company (19.06) and Keurig Dr Pepper (17.33), and below Celsius (26.30).

The EV/EBITDA comparison measures what investors pay for each dollar of EBITDA. A higher multiple is consistent with expectations of stronger future growth or durability, but the resources do not contain forward estimates, so the data cannot confirm which expectation is priced in. Relative to the peer group in 2025, Monster's valuation is not low. Relative to its own history, the 2025 multiple is close to its 2022 and 2023 levels. On the figures alone, the 2025 multiple is consistent with a premium that the company's 2025 revenue growth and margin improvement partly support, though the 2024 slowdown shows that the margin path is not uniform.

The EV/Sales multiple for Monster rose from 6.67 in 2024 to 8.79 in 2025. Dividing EV/Sales by EV/EBITDA for each company gives a figure equal to the EBITDA margin, which confirms the internal consistency of the valuation and margin data. For Monster in 2025, 8.79 divided by 28.77 is about 30.5%, the reported EBITDA margin.

## FCF Conversion

Monster's FCF Conversion rose from 0.57 in 2022 to 0.91 in 2023 and 1.07 in 2024, then eased slightly to 1.02 in 2025. Monster's FCF in dollars also rose each year, from 676 to 1,941. The 2022 conversion rate of 0.57 is the weakest point in its record, and the company has held above 0.9 since 2023.

Relative to the peers, Monster's 2025 conversion of 1.02 was above The Coca-Cola Company's 0.40 and Keurig Dr Pepper's 0.72, and below Celsius's 2.99. In 2022 Monster (0.57) was below The Coca-Cola Company (1.0) and Keurig Dr Pepper (1.71). In 2023 Monster's 0.91 was above Keurig Dr Pepper's 0.39 and close to The Coca-Cola Company's 0.91, and below Celsius's 0.55 only in the sense that Celsius's 0.55 was lower; Monster's figure was higher. The Coca-Cola Company's conversion declined from 1.0 in 2022 to 0.40 in 2025, while its FCF in dollars fell from 9,534 to 5,296. Monster's conversion is therefore steadier and higher than the largest peer's in the two most recent years.

## ROIC

Monster's ROIC rose from 17.1% in 2022 to 18.7% in 2023 and 23.1% in 2024, then edged down to 22.5% in 2025. The trend is upward over the period, with a small decline in the latest year. The 2025 figure is still well above the 2022 level.

Against the peers, Monster's ROIC exceeded The Coca-Cola Company's in every year: 17.1% against 16.3% in 2022, 18.7% against 17.6% in 2023, 23.1% against 17.3% in 2024, and 22.5% against 18.7% in 2025. The gap widened in 2024 and remained wide in 2025. Keurig Dr Pepper's ROIC ranged between 5.0% and 6.6% over the period, well below Monster. Celsius's ROIC was negative in 2022 at −292.0%, then 86.0% in 2023, 36.2% in 2024, and 8.1% in 2025. Its decline across the last three years is the sharpest downward trend in the group, even though Celsius's revenue and FCF rose over the same period. The data does not explain the drop.

The ROIC data suggest that Monster generates more return per unit of invested capital than any of the three peers in the period, with the 2025 decline being small relative to its 2024 level.

## Conclusion

On the metrics in this dataset, Monster Beverage Corporation shows a financial position that is strong on growth, gross margin, cash conversion, and returns, with a valuation that prices in much of that record. Its revenue reached 8,294 in 2025, growing 10.7% after a 4.9% year in 2024. Its gross margin rose every year to 55.8%. Its FCF rose from 676 in 2022 to 1,941 in 2025, and its FCF Conversion has held above 0.9 since 2023. Its ROIC climbed from 17.1% to 22.5%, above The Coca-Cola Company in every year.

The weaknesses are real. The 2024 EBITDA decline and margin compression, from 28.3% to 26.8%, show that profitability did not rise every year. The company's EBITDA margin still trails The Coca-Cola Company's 39.0% in 2025, and its 2022 conversion rate of 0.57 was the lowest in the series. The valuation is the most demanding part of the picture. At 28.77 times EBITDA in 2025, Monster trades at about 60% more than The Coca-Cola Company and more than twice Keurig Dr Pepper's multiple.

Taken together, the 2025 results support a premium valuation relative to these two peers, because growth, margin recovery, cash generation, and returns all improved over the period. The metrics do not show whether the premium is justified by future performance, since the data contains no forward estimates. The view would be proven wrong if Monster's revenue growth returned to the low single digits seen in 2024 while its EBITDA margin fell back toward 26% or below, or if its ROIC declined further from the 22.5% level in 2025. Either outcome would leave the 28.77 multiple resting on a narrower base than the 2025 figures show.
