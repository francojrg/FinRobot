# Moody's Top Three Key Risks (Based on the FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A)

The three risks that matter most are regulatory and litigation exposure across both segments, the dependence of Moody's Investors Service (MIS) transaction revenue on debt issuance volumes, and competitive and technological disruption, including from Gen AI and free information. The risk factors contain no revenue, margin or segment profit figures, so each risk below is assessed qualitatively. The only numbers are the balance sheet and tax figures the filing states directly, and the comparisons across industries that the instruction requests cannot be made from this document, so the comparisons below are internal to Moody's.

## Risk 1: Regulatory and Litigation Exposure

**Industry vertical risk.** Within Moody's, MIS carries the heavier direct regulatory burden. The filing states that MIS is subject to formal regulation and periodic inspections in the EU, U.K., Australia, Singapore, Japan and Hong Kong, and that the Reform Act and Dodd-Frank Act directly increase SEC oversight of CRAs. Moody's Analytics (MA) faces a different form of exposure. Its sanctions, KYC and financial-crime products and its data products are subject to data-protection and data-access rules, and its bank customers pass third-party risk requirements such as DORA down to vendors. The filing notes that these requirements have prompted customers to "seek to and may further revise" their terms with MA.

**Cyclicality.** Legal exposure rises in the same conditions that reduce issuance. The filing states that when the market value of credit-dependent instruments has declined or defaults have occurred, "the number of investigations and legal proceedings that Moody's has faced has increased significantly," citing the 2007-2008 financial crisis. Legal costs therefore tend to increase during downturns, the same period in which Risk 2 reduces revenue.

**Risk quantification.** The filing enumerates several specific exposures. These include EU AI Act compliance costs and fines, the EU regulation on ESG rating activities adopted in November 2024, DORA (effective in the EU in January 2025), the CCPA and CPRA in the United States, and the potential for new investigations related to private credit. The filing also cites Note 19 for ongoing investigations and civil litigation, but Note 19 is not included in the resource, so the size of these matters cannot be assessed. Two statements in the filing bear directly on severity. First, Moody's "may not have adequate insurance or reserves to cover these risks." Second, "the existence and magnitude of these risks often remain unknown for substantial periods of time." The filing also states that failure to achieve early dismissals means "aggregate legal defense costs... increase substantially."

**Downside protections.** These are weak by the filing's own account. The compliance programs are described, but the filing states they "may not be fully effective," and no insurance coverage amounts or reserve levels are disclosed. The one mitigant the filing points to is early dismissal of cases, which it describes as a condition it does not always achieve. This is the risk where the unknowns are largest and the filing offers the least quantification.

## Risk 2: Dependence on Debt Issuance Volumes

**Industry vertical risk.** The filing ties this exposure specifically to credit-rating revenue: "A majority of Moody's credit-rating-based revenue is transaction-based, and therefore it is especially dependent on the number and dollar volume of debt securities issued in the capital markets." The filing does not describe MA's subscription products in the same terms, so this risk is concentrated in MIS. The competitive landscape is also shifting. Issuers are increasingly selecting unrated securities, ratings from financial advisors rather than traditional CRAs, and non-bank lending that involves no CRA rating.

**Cyclicality.** This is the most cyclical of the three risks. The filing lists the drivers: interest rates, market volatility, declining growth, currency devaluations, changes in tax law, mortgage rate volatility, widening credit spreads, and defaults by significant issuers. The filing also states that cost reductions "may be difficult or impossible to obtain in the short term" because of rent, technology, compliance, compensation and other fixed costs. The result is operating leverage: when issuance falls, revenue falls while much of the cost base remains.

**Risk quantification.** The balance sheet figures give a bounded view of the impairment side of this risk. At December 31, 2025, goodwill was $6,368 million and intangible assets were $1,866 million. The filing states that about 94% of these balances sit in MA and about 6% in MIS, primarily related to ICRA. As an illustrative calculation, the combined $8,234 million implies roughly $7,740 million in MA and roughly $494 million in MIS. The MIS-facing impairment exposure is therefore small in carrying value. The larger balance sheet concern is an MA impairment, which the filing ties to "failure to achieve business objectives and financial projections." The filing also states that goodwill testing "requires significant management estimates and judgment" and that the potential for impairment "is increased during periods of economic uncertainty."

**Downside protections.** The filing offers limited structural protection for MIS. It states that a significant portion of revenue comes from foreign sources, which provides some geographic diversification, but it gives no figure. The filing does not disclose the revenue split between MIS and MA, so the share of revenue that is transaction-based cannot be sized. The cost-reduction program is described as potentially insufficient, which means it is not a reliable offset.

## Risk 3: Competitive and Technological Disruption

**Industry vertical risk.** The filing describes the markets for credit ratings, research, credit risk management, business intelligence and analytics as "highly competitive and characterized by rapid technological change." Competition in MIS includes other CRAs, investment banks, brokerage firms and in-house research operations. Some governments in certain countries provide support to local agencies, and the Reform Act was designed to encourage new NRSRO entrants. In MA, the competitive threats include free or lower-cost information, Gen AI tools that could lower prices, and consulting firms and technology providers, some of which are also suppliers to Moody's.

**Cyclicality.** Competitive pressure is less directly tied to the credit cycle than Risk 2, but the filing links it to adverse conditions. It states that "a challenging business environment and consolidation among both competitors and customers... may enhance the market power of competitors and reduce the Company's customer base." Pricing pressure therefore tends to intensify when issuance is weak, the same period in which Risk 2 bites.

**Risk quantification.** The filing contains no quantitative data on market share, pricing or customer attrition. The specific risks it enumerates are price competition in several segments, the formation of new NRSROs, consolidation in structured finance and commercial real estate, customers building proprietary risk systems, the growth of free information, and the dependence on suppliers who are also competitors. The filing states that "any inability of Moody's to compete successfully... may have a material adverse effect."

**Downside protections.** The filing identifies brand and reputation as "key competitive strengths" and states that Moody's seeks to compete primarily on quality. Proprietary data and intellectual property are also cited as strategic assets. However, the filing itself weakens the intellectual property protection by noting that legal and technological protections are inadequate or changing in some jurisdictions, and that some laws require publication of information the company currently sells. Regulation does not clearly act as a shield here. The filing describes the Reform Act as designed to increase competition. The filing does not describe the contract terms or duration for MA subscriptions, so the extent to which subscriptions protect revenue cannot be assessed from this document.

## Overall Assessment

These three risks compound one another. A downturn that reduces debt issuance (Risk 2) raises litigation exposure (Risk 1), since the filing links investigations to defaults and market declines, and it strengthens pricing pressure from competitors and customers (Risk 3). Fixed costs remain in place throughout, as the filing acknowledges. The regulatory and litigation risk is the least quantifiable of the three, and the filing states that its magnitude may remain unknown for long periods, so it carries the most uncertainty about timing and size. The issuance risk is the most cyclical and is concentrated in MIS, although the filing's own balance sheet split suggests that the goodwill exposure to MIS is small. The competitive risk is the hardest to size because the filing gives no market data at all.

Cybersecurity and third-party technology dependence are close to the top three, and the filing treats them as significant, but they were not ranked above these three because the filing describes them largely as operational risks without financial sizing.

The view that Moody's is most exposed through regulation and litigation would be weakened by evidence that MIS's transaction-based revenue is a smaller share of total revenue than the filing's "majority" language implies, by early dismissals of litigation that limit defense costs, or by Note 19 disclosures showing that the pending matters are immaterial. The view that competition is a major risk would be weakened by evidence that Moody's has maintained pricing and retained customers through a period of Gen AI-driven competition. The issuance-cycle view would be challenged by evidence that the company can reduce its fixed cost base in a downturn, which the filing says it has so far found difficult in the short term. The MA goodwill view would be challenged by annual impairment tests that continue to show no impairment in periods of weak customer demand.
