**Decision Solutions (MA):** Revenue rose 12% to $1,692 million in 2025 from $1,516 million, and ARR grew 10% to $1,579 million. Growth came mainly from Insurance (revenue up 15% to $685 million) and KYC (up 19% to $438 million), while Banking grew only 3% to $569 million because transaction revenue fell 18%, a result of MA's shift to cloud-hosted subscriptions.

**Research & Insights (MA):** Revenue rose 7% to $995 million from $926 million, with U.S. revenue up 6% and international revenue up 9%. ARR grew 8% to $1,002 million, driven by sales growth in credit research product offerings.

**Data & Information (MA):** Revenue rose 7% to $912 million from $853 million, but organic constant currency growth was 5% after a $17 million foreign exchange benefit. ARR grew 7% to $917 million, supported by continued demand for ratings data feeds and company data applications.

**Corporate Finance (CFG, MIS):** Revenue rose 9% to $2,132 million from $1,950 million, and transaction revenue added $144 million, reflecting higher investment-grade issuance under tight credit spreads and high-yield refinancing activity. Recurring revenue rose $38 million, mainly from annual price increases and a higher number of monitored credits.

**Structured Finance (SFG, MIS):** Revenue rose 8% to $558 million from $518 million, with U.S. and international revenue each up 8%. Management attributed the increase to growth across all asset classes, supported by tight credit spreads and strong investor demand.

**Financial Institutions (FIG, MIS):** Revenue rose 4% to $759 million from $727 million, with international revenue up 10% and U.S. revenue down 1%. Strong issuance from infrequent banking-sector issuers was partly offset by lower insurance-sector issuance from infrequent issuers, which had been strong in the prior year.

**Public, Project & Infrastructure Finance (PPIF, MIS):** Revenue rose 13% to $635 million from $564 million, the fastest growth among the MIS ratings lines, with U.S. revenue up 16% and international revenue up 7%. Higher U.S. Public Finance issuance in the state, regional and healthcare sectors, together with higher investment-grade infrastructure issuance from utilities, drove the increase.
