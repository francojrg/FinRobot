# Moody's Corporation (MCO): Financial Metrics Against SPGI, MSCI and MORN, FY2022 to FY2025

Moody's Corporation reported revenue of 7,718 in fiscal 2025, up from 5,468 in fiscal 2022, and its EBITDA margin rose from 42.7% to 50.4% over the same period. Its return on invested capital (ROIC) climbed from 15.8% to 24.3%. The two weak points in the data are a fall in free cash flow (FCF) conversion to 1.05 in fiscal 2025, the lowest of the four companies that year, and an EV/EBITDA multiple of 24.59, which is above the peer group. Both need explanation before the company's strengths can be weighed against them. The resources do not state fiscal year-end dates for any company, so all comparisons below use the fiscal year labels as given.

## Revenue and Revenue Growth

Revenue rose in every year, from 5,468 in fiscal 2022 to 5,916 in fiscal 2023, 7,088 in fiscal 2024 and 7,718 in fiscal 2025. Growth was 8.2% in fiscal 2023, 19.8% in fiscal 2024 and 8.9% in fiscal 2025. The fiscal 2024 jump is an outlier: it is more than double the rate in either adjacent year, and the resources do not say what drove it. Over the full period, revenue grew about 41% (illustrative: 7,718 divided by 5,468), or roughly 12% a year compounded.

Against peers, Moody's was the slowest grower in fiscal 2023 apart from MORN, which grew 9.0%. In fiscal 2023 MCO grew 8.2%, against 12.5% for MSCI Inc. (MSCI), 11.8% for S&P Global Inc. (SPGI) and 9.0% for Morningstar, Inc. (MORN). In fiscal 2024 Moody's growth of 19.8% led the group, ahead of MSCI at 12.9%, SPGI at 13.7% and MORN at 11.6%. In fiscal 2025 Moody's grew 8.9%, second behind MSCI's 9.7% and ahead of SPGI at 7.9% and MORN at 7.5%. Over the full period Moody's compounded revenue at a rate similar to MSCI and above SPGI and MORN (illustrative: about 12% against 12%, 11%, and 9%). Its absolute revenue scale is also the second largest in the group, behind SPGI's 15,336 in fiscal 2025.

## Gross Margin

Gross margin for Moody's improved in every year: 70.5% in fiscal 2022, 71.5% in fiscal 2023, 72.6% in fiscal 2024 and 74.4% in fiscal 2025. The fiscal 2025 gain of 1.8 points was the largest single-year move in the series, and it came in a year when revenue growth slowed to 8.9%. Gross profit grew 11.7% in fiscal 2025 (from 5,143 to 5,745), faster than revenue, so costs of revenue were growing more slowly than sales.

MSCI had the highest gross margin in every year, between 82.0% and 82.4%. Moody's ranked second in each year. The gap to MSCI narrowed from 11.5 points in fiscal 2022 to 8.0 points in fiscal 2025. Moody's stayed ahead of SPGI in every year, with a margin gap of 3.3 to 4.6 points. Moody's also stayed well ahead of MORN, whose margin ranged from 58.3% to 61.0%.

## EBITDA Margin

Moody's EBITDA margin was 42.7% in fiscal 2022, 44.5% in fiscal 2023, 48.9% in fiscal 2024 and 50.4% in fiscal 2025. EBITDA grew faster than revenue in each of the last three years. EBITDA reached 3,891 in fiscal 2025, up 12.2% on fiscal 2024, while revenue rose 8.9%. The margin gain is therefore a result of cost growth running below revenue growth, not of a one-off item visible in the data.

Moody's ranking shifted across the period. In fiscal 2022 SPGI (53.8%) and MSCI (60.4%) were both above Moody's, and only MORN (17.2%) was below it. In fiscal 2023 SPGI's margin fell to 41.2% on lower EBITDA (5,148 against 6,019 in fiscal 2022), while revenue rose, so Moody's moved above SPGI. The resources do not explain the SPGI decline. In fiscal 2024 Moody's (48.9%) was again ahead of SPGI (47.7%) and well ahead of MORN (31.4%), but behind MSCI (61.2%). In fiscal 2025 Moody's (50.4%) was level with SPGI (50.2%), 10.9 points above MORN (29.7%) and 10.9 points below MSCI (61.3%). The margin lead over SPGI in fiscal 2025 is only 0.2 points, so it should not be read as a durable separation.

## FCF Conversion

FCF conversion for Moody's was 0.87 in fiscal 2022, 1.17 in fiscal 2023, 1.22 in fiscal 2024 and 1.05 in fiscal 2025. The fiscal 2025 decline is the main problem in the data. Free cash flow rose only 2.1%, from 2,521 to 2,575, while EBITDA rose 12.2%. Cash generation did not keep pace with earnings in that year. The resources do not identify the cause, so this analysis cannot say whether it reflects working capital, taxes, capital spending or timing.

Against peers, Moody's ranked third in fiscal 2022 (behind MORN at 2.39 and MSCI at 1.17), third in fiscal 2023 (behind MORN at 1.40 and SPGI at 1.36), third in fiscal 2024 (behind SPGI at 1.44 and MSCI at 1.25) and last in fiscal 2025, where SPGI (1.22), MSCI (1.21) and MORN (1.18) were all above it. The fiscal 2022 figure for MORN is an outlier and should be read with care, since MORN's FCF was 168 against EBITDA of 322.

The conversion figures do not reconcile to FCF divided by EBITDA. For Moody's in fiscal 2025, 2,575 divided by 3,891 gives 0.66, not 1.05. The resources do not define the metric, so the reported figures are used as given. Any conclusion about cash efficiency depends on that definition, which should be confirmed before the metric is used for decisions.

## ROIC

Moody's ROIC rose in every year: 15.8% in fiscal 2022, 18.2% in fiscal 2023, 21.1% in fiscal 2024 and 24.3% in fiscal 2025. The company gained 8.5 points over three years, and the gain was steady rather than concentrated in one year. This pattern suggests that growth in operating profit was coming from a growing base of capital that was earning more per dollar, not from a one-time improvement.

MSCI led on ROIC in every year, from 28.9% to 38.7%. Moody's was second in every year. SPGI's ROIC was 7.9% in fiscal 2022, dipped to 6.9% in fiscal 2023, then reached 11.4% in fiscal 2025. MORN's ROIC rose from 5.0% in fiscal 2022 to 17.6% in both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2025, so its improvement stopped in the final year. Moody's ROIC was 6.7 points above MORN in fiscal 2025 and 12.9 points above SPGI. The gap to MSCI, 14.4 points in fiscal 2025, has narrowed from 13.1 points in fiscal 2022 only partly. In fiscal 2022 the gap was 13.1 points (28.9 against 15.8), and in fiscal 2025 it was 14.4 points, so it widened, not narrowed. This is a point the investor should note: MSCI's ROIC lead has grown in absolute terms.

## EV/EBITDA

Moody's EV/EBITDA multiple was 24.45 in fiscal 2022, 29.11 in fiscal 2023, 26.14 in fiscal 2024 and 24.59 in fiscal 2025. The multiple peaked in fiscal 2023 and has fallen in the two years since. Over the same period EBITDA grew 48% (from 2,630 to 3,891 between fiscal 2023 and fiscal 2025). The multiple fell because EBITDA grew faster than the enterprise value implied by the ratio. The resources do not report enterprise value or share price, so the split between a change in market value and a change in earnings cannot be determined.

Relative to peers, Moody's ranked third in fiscal 2022, above only SPGI (19.02), and second in each of the three later years. In fiscal 2022 MORN (31.51) and MSCI (30.08) were higher. In fiscal 2023 MORN (31.78) was higher and MSCI (28.52) and SPGI (27.57) were lower. In fiscal 2024 MSCI (29.10) was higher and SPGI (22.91) and MORN (20.78) were lower. In fiscal 2025 MSCI (24.97) was just above Moody's, and SPGI (20.74) and MORN (12.99) were below it.

For fiscal 2025 the peer average is about 19.6 (illustrative: the simple average of 20.74, 24.97 and 12.99), and the median is 20.74. Moody's multiple is about 26% above the simple average and about 19% above the median (both illustrative). MORN's multiple fell from 31.51 to 12.99 over the period, which narrows the peer average and makes the premium look larger than it would against a stable peer set. The data does not show whether MORN's fall reflects a lower share price, higher EBITDA, or both.

On a multiple basis, Moody's does not look undervalued against this peer set in any year. The data does not establish whether it is overvalued, because that requires an assessment of growth and risk that these metrics alone do not provide.

## Conclusion

Moody's Corporation's financial record over fiscal 2022 to fiscal 2025 is strong on the measures that reflect profitability and returns. Revenue grew roughly 41% over three years, gross margin rose 3.9 points to 74.4%, EBITDA margin rose 7.7 points to 50.4%, and ROIC rose 8.5 points to 24.3%. Each of these gains was steady across the period, with the exception of the fiscal 2024 revenue spike, whose cause the resources do not identify.

The weaknesses are narrower but real. FCF conversion fell to 1.05 in fiscal 2025, the lowest in the peer group, because free cash flow grew far more slowly than EBITDA. The company's EBITDA margin in fiscal 2025 is level with SPGI and well below MSCI, and MSCI's ROIC lead has widened in absolute terms. Growth in fiscal 2023 was the slowest in the group, apart from MORN's, and in fiscal 2025 growth was below MSCI's.

The EV/EBITDA multiple of 24.59 is a premium to the peer median of 20.74 (illustrative: about 19% above) and sits near MSCI's 24.97. The historical record supports part of that premium: Moody's has the second-highest gross margin and the second-highest ROIC in every year, and its EBITDA has grown faster than the peer set's median. The record does not by itself justify the premium, because the FCF conversion decline in fiscal 2025 runs against the case that earnings are turning into cash at the same rate.

The view that Moody's quality supports its valuation would be proven wrong if fiscal 2026 data showed FCF conversion staying near 1.05 or below while EBITDA continued to grow in the low double digits, or if ROIC stopped rising while the multiple held above 24. Confirmation would come from FCF conversion recovering toward the 1.2 level of fiscal 2024, and from ROIC continuing to rise above 24.3%. Before drawing conclusions, the definition of the FCF conversion metric and the drivers of the fiscal 2024 revenue jump and the fiscal 2025 cash shortfall should be confirmed, because the resources do not contain them.
