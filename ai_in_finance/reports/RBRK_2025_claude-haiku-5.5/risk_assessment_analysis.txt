## Top Three Key Risks

The filing points to three risks that matter most for an investor. The first is the transition of the revenue base to Rubrik Security Cloud (RSC), which makes the timing and quality of revenue hard to forecast while the company is still loss-making. The second is the security and reputational exposure inherent in being a data security provider, where a single failure could be costly. The third is dependence on a small number of third parties for distribution, hardware, and hosting. The filing does not include peer-group or cross-industry data, so the comparisons below are qualitative and drawn only from what the 10-K says.

### Risk 1: Revenue Transition to RSC and Continued Operating Losses

**Industry Vertical Risk.** The filing describes the market for data security solutions as "at an early stage and rapidly evolving," and says it is difficult to predict its growth, customer adoption rates, or the success of competing products. That positions the company in a new, unproven category rather than an established one. The 10-K does not compare this vertical's risk profile with other industries, so any ranking against other sectors must rest on the company's own disclosures. On those disclosures, the business sits at the high end of execution risk because it must simultaneously build a market, change its revenue model, and compete with incumbents that already hold installed customer bases.

**Cyclicality.** The filing ties demand to macroeconomic conditions. It states that deterioration in general economic conditions "has in the past caused, and may in the future cause," customers to delay or cut security and IT operations spending. It also notes that the company has seen longer sales cycles, a reduction in multi-year upfront payments, and reduced contract sizes due to macroeconomic uncertainty. Revenue is further affected by seasonality, because a higher share of subscriptions is sold in the fourth fiscal quarter, which the company attributes to customers' procurement and budgeting cycles. The company's transition from term licenses to SaaS also means that some revenue is recognized upfront today and will shift to ratable recognition over time, so reported growth can diverge from underlying demand.

**Risk Quantification.** The financial figures show both growth and pressure. Revenue rose from $599.8 million in fiscal 2023 to $627.9 million in fiscal 2024 and $886.5 million in fiscal 2025. Net losses were $(277.7) million, $(354.2) million, and $(1,154.8) million over the same years, and the accumulated deficit grew from $(1,682.5) million to $(2,837.3) million in fiscal 2025. The company expects revenue growth to fluctuate as more revenue is recognized ratably. It also says that the sale of Rubrik-branded Appliances will continue to decline and will weigh on revenue and profitability through the fiscal year ending January 31, 2027. Stock-based compensation tied to RSUs is also expected to increase expenses significantly. The company notes that it may need to offer customers Subscription Credits, and that unused credits could lead to dissatisfaction or non-renewal. Finally, it has seen an increase in customers choosing annual or consumption payments rather than multi-year upfront payments, which adds cash flow volatility. Taken together, these factors make the company's near-term earnings and cash generation difficult to model with confidence.

**Downside Protections.** Some mitigants exist. Subscription revenue from SaaS is recognized ratably, which can smooth revenue once a large base is established, and the company notes that strong customer retention leads to greater usage over time. The company also has a defined base of multi-year contracts, with a typical subscription term of three years. Still, the filing indicates that these protections are only partially effective. Because revenue from new subscriptions shows up gradually, any slowdown in new sales or renewals will not appear fully in the same period, but it will affect future quarters. Additionally, the company's accumulated losses and $1,346.1 million in federal NOL carryforwards would only be useful if the company reaches profitability, and the filing warns that Section 382 limitations and state-level restrictions may restrict their use.

### Risk 2: Security Incidents, Product Defects, and Liability Exposure

**Industry Vertical Risk.** The company's risk is structurally different from that of a company whose products are not themselves targets. The 10-K states that because the company's business is "focused on providing data security services," an actual or perceived security incident "would be especially detrimental to our reputation and our business." Its solutions are part of the supply chain of many organizations, which makes them an attractive target for threat actors. The filing also says the company faces "heightened risk of being targeted by bad actors" because of its ransomware recovery capabilities and marketing. Regulatory exposure adds to this, given data protection obligations under the GDPR, CCPA, HIPAA, and other regimes, along with potential fines of up to 20 million euros or 4% of annual global revenue under the EU GDPR. Compared with a company whose product is not inherently security-sensitive, this profile places the risk of a single event at the high end.

**Cyclicality.** This risk is not cyclical in the usual sense. It does not rise and fall with the economy, but the frequency of threats and the importance customers place on resilience do affect demand. The filing notes that demand is affected by "increased activity by or prevalence of cybersecurity bad actors, including the use of ransomware." It also warns that if cyberattack incidence declines, or enterprises believe that it has declined, the company's ability to attract customers could be impaired. In that sense, demand depends partly on threat levels, which are unpredictable, rather than on the business cycle.

**Risk Quantification.** The 10-K discloses two prior incidents. In March 2023, a malicious third party accessed a limited amount of information in a non-production testing environment. In February 2025, the company observed anomalous activity on a server containing log files, some of which were accessed by an unauthorized actor. The company states that neither incident involved customer-secured data or internal code. The Ransomware Recovery Warranty provides customers with up to $10,000,000 in recovery expense reimbursement for eligible failures, and the company warns that widespread claims under that program could "significantly increase our costs." Customer liability caps are generally in place, but the filing notes that "in rare cases" the company has agreed to exceptions, and that these provisions may not be enforceable in every jurisdiction. The company also acknowledges that "it is virtually impossible for us to entirely eliminate the risk of such security incidents." It further notes that the company relies on public cloud providers, including GCP, Azure, and AWS, whose outages or breaches could create liability for the company as well. The filing also discloses an ongoing DOJ investigation related to government contracts. This matter could result in "substantial fines, penalties, or other financial exposure," as well as potential exclusion from federal contracting.

**Downside Protections.** The company identifies several mitigants. Its agreements typically limit liability, it maintains insurance against certain claims, it has a formal AI governance committee, and it has invested in certifications such as FedRAMP. The 10-K also explains the shared responsibility model, under which customers are responsible for implementing and configuring security features such as multi-factor authentication. However, the filing repeatedly qualifies each of these protections. Insurance "may not adequately limit our exposure," liability caps "may not be effective or enforceable," and the company cannot be certain that coverage will remain available on commercially reasonable terms. Because the company's core value proposition is security, an incident could damage the brand in ways that contractual and insurance protections cannot fully offset.

### Risk 3: Concentration in Channel Partners, Hardware Suppliers, and Cloud Hosting

**Industry Vertical Risk.** The company's distribution, hardware, and infrastructure model is more concentrated than many software businesses. The three largest Channel Partners, Arrow Enterprise Computing Solutions, Exclusive Networks, and Ingram Micro Inc., together with their affiliates, generated approximately 73% of revenue in fiscal 2025 and 76% in fiscal 2024. A large majority of customer enterprise data relies on Rubrik-branded Appliances built on servers supplied by Super Micro Computer, Inc. The company also relies on third-party cloud providers for "substantially all of the infrastructure" supporting its data security solutions. This structure gives the company broad reach, but it also means that the company's risk profile is partly determined by the health and decisions of a few outside parties. Compared with a company that controls its own delivery and hosting, the company has less direct control over availability, costs, and customer relationships.

**Cyclicality.** Channel partner health is directly sensitive to economic conditions. The 10-K notes that some Channel Partners "may be unable to withstand adverse changes in economic conditions," and that weakness in end-user markets could delay their payments and increase the company's credit risk. Supply chain risks are also tied to external conditions, including tariffs, trade disputes, geopolitical tensions, and pandemics. The company's cloud costs are also tied to negotiated pricing with providers, and the filing notes that increased adoption could raise those costs and "negatively impact our gross margins." These are not economic cycles in the traditional sense, but they amplify cyclical pressure when conditions weaken.

**Risk Quantification.** The key quantitative exposures are the 73% and 76% channel concentration figures for fiscal 2025 and fiscal 2024, respectively. The company states that its agreements with these partners are non-exclusive, renew automatically in one-year terms, and may be terminated by either party at any time. Channel Partners also purchase on a purchase order basis without minimum purchase requirements. The company's Manufacturers have an impact as well: the filing warns that the company's orders "may only be rescheduled or canceled under certain limited conditions," and that inaccurate demand forecasts could lead to cancellation charges or penalties. Customer orders may also be deprioritized relative to Manufacturers' other obligations. The company further notes that some of its OEMs carry products that compete with its data security solutions. Finally, the company acknowledges that it has "limited" ability to monitor third-party information security practices, and that "these third parties have not had and may not have adequate information security measures in place."

**Downside Protections.** Several protections are mentioned in the filing. The company's customer base is diversified across the portion of revenue that is not concentrated in its three largest Channel Partners, and customers outside the United States generated 31% of total revenue in fiscal 2025 and 32% in fiscal 2024, which provides some geographic breadth. The company has also largely transitioned Rubrik-branded Appliance sales to its contract manufacturers, which shifts certain hardware execution risk away from the company. Customers may also purchase compatible third-party commodity servers from other OEMs if Supermicro cannot supply the company, and the company has the ability to qualify new commodity servers. Channel Partners extend the company's reach into smaller customers and regions where it has limited direct presence. These mitigants are meaningful, but the filing makes clear that alternative suppliers may bring increased costs or delays, and that replacing a major Channel Partner "with limited or no notice" would be difficult. The company's protections are therefore real but incomplete.

## Overall Assessment

Taken together, the three risks reinforce one another. The revenue transition to RSC makes financial results harder to forecast, while the company's distribution concentration and reliance on outside hardware and cloud providers make execution more difficult during that transition. A security incident would hit the company's core brand more directly than it would affect a business whose products are not themselves security tools, and the company's contractual and insurance protections are explicitly described as imperfect. The company's historical revenue growth is substantial, and its contract structure, global customer base, and diversified product offering provide some insulation. However, the 10-K does not present these factors as offsetting. It consistently frames them as conditional on the company successfully completing its migration to RSC, sustaining customer retention, and avoiding a material security event. For an investor, the central question is not whether the business is growing but whether it can convert that growth into durable, predictable profit while maintaining the trust its security products depend on. The filing acknowledges that it has not yet demonstrated that it can do so.
