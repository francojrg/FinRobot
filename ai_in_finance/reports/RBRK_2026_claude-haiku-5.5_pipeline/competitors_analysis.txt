Rubrik, Inc. (RBRK) grew its revenue fastest of the group in the most recent periods, while its profitability remains negative on an EBITDA basis. The comparison below uses Commvault Systems, Inc. (CVLT), SentinelOne, Inc. (S), and Netskope, Inc. (NTSK). The resource does not state the currency or unit scale of the monetary figures, so revenue, gross profit, EBITDA, and FCF are discussed as reported. It also does not give fiscal year-end dates, so the comparisons are made by the fiscal year labels as provided. The data covers RBRK, CVLT, and S from 2023 through 2026, and NTSK from 2024 through 2026.

Revenue and revenue growth for RBRK show a clear acceleration. Revenue rose from 600 in 2023 to 628 in 2024, a modest gain of 4.7%, then jumped to 887 in 2025 (41.2% growth) and reached 1,316 in 2026 (48.5% growth). Revenue more than doubled over the period. Among the peers, CVLT's growth was steadier but slower, moving from 7.0% in 2024 to 18.6% in 2025 and 18.9% in 2026, with revenue of 1,184 in 2026. S grew 47.1% in 2024 but decelerated to 32.2% in 2025 and 21.9% in 2026, and its 2026 revenue of 1,001 remains below RBRK's. NTSK's growth was 32.3% in 2025 and 31.7% in 2026, reaching 709 in 2026. In 2026, RBRK's 48.5% growth was the highest in the peer set by a wide margin, and its revenue was the largest, well ahead of CVLT. The one caveat is that RBRK's 2024 growth was only 4.7%, so the acceleration is recent rather than sustained across the full window.

Gross margin for RBRK has been volatile but is high. It was 69.7% in 2023, rose to 76.9% in 2024, fell back to 70.0% in 2025, and then climbed to 80.1% in 2026. CVLT is the margin leader in every year, between 81% and 83%, with a slight decline to 81.2% in 2026. In 2023, RBRK (69.7%) trailed CVLT (82.7%) but was ahead of S (65.8%). In 2024, RBRK's 76.9% was below CVLT's 81.9% but above S (71.1%) and NTSK (59.8%). In 2025, RBRK's 70.0% trailed CVLT (82.0%) and S (74.3%) but remained above NTSK (64.6%). In 2026, RBRK's 80.1% was close to CVLT's 81.2% and well above S (74.1%) and NTSK (68.1%). The 2025 dip in RBRK's margin coincides with the year of its largest EBITDA loss, but the resource does not explain the cause, so the reason for the dip cannot be established from the data. The 2026 recovery to 80.1% is a meaningful signal of improving cost efficiency at the gross level.

EBITDA margin is where RBRK's profile is weakest. Its EBITDA was -235 in 2023 (a margin of -39.2%), -273 in 2024 (-43.5%), -1,078 in 2025 (-121.6%), and -272 in 2026 (-20.7%). The 2025 loss is far larger than the other years and is not explained in the resource. The 2026 margin of -20.7% is a substantial improvement, the best RBRK has recorded in this window, and it is the least negative among the loss-making peers. CVLT is the only consistently profitable company in the group, moving from -0.6% in 2023 to 10.8% in 2024, 9.1% in 2025, and 9.0% in 2026. S improved steadily from -83.6% in 2023 to -25.4% in 2026, and NTSK's margin was -64.7% in 2024, -37.9% in 2025, and -85.6% in 2026, a deterioration in the latest year. In 2026, RBRK's -20.7% lags CVLT's 9.0% but outperforms S (-25.4%) and NTSK (-85.6%). In 2025, RBRK's -121.6% was the weakest in the group, well below S (-34.9%) and NTSK (-37.9%).

EV/EBITDA cannot be assessed for RBRK, because its value is n/m in every year, reflecting negative EBITDA. The only peer with a numeric EV/EBITDA is CVLT, at 45.51 in 2024, 73.63 in 2025, and 30.4 in 2026. CVLT's multiple peaked in 2025 and compressed sharply in 2026 as its EV/Sales fell from 6.7 to 2.73 while its EBITDA margin held near 9%. Against CVLT, the valuation signal is that CVLT trades at a much higher EBITDA multiple in 2024 and 2025 and at a more moderate multiple in 2026. S and NTSK are also n/m on EV/EBITDA, so there is no like-for-like EBITDA-based comparison for RBRK. Using EV/Sales as the available proxy, RBRK's multiple was 15.85 in 2025 and 9.18 in 2026. That is well above CVLT's 2.73 and S's 4.56 in 2026, and modestly above NTSK's 8.8 in 2026. The premium is consistent with RBRK's superior growth, but RBRK's EV/Sales was 15.85 in 2025, so the multiple has already compressed substantially. Across the years for which S's EV/Sales is available, it was 9.99 in 2023, 12.77 in 2024, 9.29 in 2025, and 4.56 in 2026, which shows that high-growth names in this group have experienced strong multiple compression. Relative to growth, RBRK's 2026 EV/Sales of 9.18 against 48.5% growth looks less stretched than NTSK's 8.8 against 31.7% growth, and CVLT appears cheapest on this basis at 2.73 against 18.9% growth. These comparisons are derived from the reported figures and are not a full valuation.

FCF conversion is the metric with the least usable data. RBRK, S, and NTSK are all n/m in every year, so the ratio cannot be used to compare them. CVLT's conversion is 1.18 in 2024, 2.68 in 2025, and 3.36 in 2026, and it is the only numeric series in the group. However, these figures do not reconcile with the FCF and EBITDA values reported for CVLT, since FCF divided by EBITDA gives about 2.2 in 2024, 2.2 in 2025, and 2.2 in 2026. The conversion definition must therefore differ from a simple FCF-to-EBITDA ratio, and the resource does not state it, so the trend should be treated cautiously. On an absolute FCF basis, RBRK's cash generation improved markedly. Its FCF was -15 in 2023, -25 in 2024, 22 in 2025, and 238 in 2026. The 2026 figure implies an FCF margin of roughly 18% on 1,316 of revenue, which is close to CVLT's roughly 20% (237 on 1,184). S generated 52 of FCF in 2026, about 5% of revenue, and NTSK generated only 12 in 2026, about 2% of revenue. RBRK's cash flow turning positive while its EBITDA remains negative is notable, but the resource does not explain the gap between the two measures.

ROIC for RBRK was 21.1% in 2023, 19.5% in 2024, 356.1% in 2025, and -30.4% in 2026. The 2025 value is an extreme outlier and is probably distorted by the unusually large loss in that year, which suggests that the denominator or numerator behaved abnormally; the resource does not give the components, so this cannot be confirmed. The 2026 reading of -30.4% is the more meaningful signal of current capital efficiency, and it indicates that the business is not yet earning a return on the invested capital given its operating losses. CVLT's ROIC was -5.9% in 2023, then 22.5% in 2024, 23.5% in 2025, and 8.3% in 2026, so its returns have also declined recently. S's ROIC was negative across all four years, between -22.7% and -12.9%, improving gradually. NTSK's ROIC was 27.9% in 2024 and 20.9% in 2025 before falling to -42.8% in 2026. Over the window, RBRK's 2023 and 2024 returns were comparable to CVLT's best years and NTSK's early years, but its 2026 reading is the weakest of the profitable-era peers apart from NTSK's sharp drop.

The overall financial picture for RBRK is one of rapid growth, strong gross margins, and a clear improvement in cash generation, offset by persistent EBITDA losses and negative returns on invested capital in the latest year. Its revenue acceleration to 48.5% growth in 2026 is the strongest in the peer group, its gross margin of 80.1% is close to the best in the group, and its FCF turned meaningfully positive in 2025 and 2026. Against that, its 2026 EBITDA margin of -20.7% and ROIC of -30.4% show that profitability has not yet caught up with the top line, and the anomalous 2025 EBITDA and ROIC readings make the trend harder to interpret.

The valuation is only partly justified by these metrics. The 9.18 EV/Sales multiple is well above CVLT and S, and it sits above NTSK, which is consistent with the market pricing RBRK's superior growth and expanding gross margin. Because RBRK's EV/EBITDA is n/m in every year, the market's valuation cannot be checked against earnings power, and the sustained EBITDA losses leave the premium dependent on the company continuing to convert growth into profit. CVLT, which generates consistent positive EBITDA and FCF and trades at a much lower EV/Sales multiple, illustrates what a profitable, lower-growth profile commands in this group. In short, RBRK's metrics support a premium for growth and improving unit economics, but they do not yet demonstrate the profitability that would fully justify its current valuation.
