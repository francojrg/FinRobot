At December 31, 2025, the current ratio was 1.38 (current assets of $18.80 billion over current liabilities of $13.65 billion), down from 1.69 in 2024 and 1.86 in 2022. Current liabilities rose $3.49 billion while current assets rose $1.62 billion. Other payables reached $6.96 billion (from $5.93 billion), and MD&A describes about $7.0 billion of customer booking funds with matching receivable and payable balances, so much of the working-capital decline is mechanical. Total debt was flat at $2.07 billion, but $2.00 billion of it is now current: the 0% notes due March 2026. Equity fell to $8.20 billion from $8.41 billion because $3.8 billion of buybacks exceeded $2.5 billion of net income, and the retained deficit widened to $5.50 billion. Cash and short-term investments of $11.01 billion exceed total debt by about $8.9 billion (illustrative). Liabilities to equity rose to 1.71x from 1.49x. Leverage is low; the key test is repaying the 2026 notes from cash while sustaining buybacks.
