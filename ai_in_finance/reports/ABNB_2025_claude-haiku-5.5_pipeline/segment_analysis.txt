# Segment Analysis: Airbnb, FY2025 vs. FY2024

The filing reports four geographic segments, defined by the location of the host's listing. It does not disclose segment profit or segment costs, so net profit is analyzed at the company level below.

**North America**
Revenue rose 4% to $5,196 million from $5,006 million, and its share of total revenue fell from 45% to 42%. GBV grew 7% to $40,295 million, and Nights and Seats Booked grew 3% to 158 million. Average nights per booking were 4.1, the highest of any region.

**EMEA**
Revenue rose 14% to $4,729 million from $4,135 million, the largest dollar gain of any region at $594 million. GBV increased 15% to $34,162 million, and nights rose 7% to 215 million. The filing attributes the company's 3% ADR increase mainly to EMEA, where ADR rose 8%. Average nights per booking were 3.8.

**Latin America**
Revenue rose 20% to $1,160 million from $969 million, the fastest growth of any region, lifting its share from 9% to 10%. GBV grew 20% to $8,542 million, and nights rose 18% to 90 million. Average nights per booking were 3.6. The filing does not disclose regional costs or profit.

**Asia Pacific**
Revenue rose 17% to $1,156 million from $992 million, and its share held at 9%. GBV grew 16% to $8,274 million, and nights rose 15% to 70 million. Average nights per booking were 3.3, the lowest of any region. The filing does not disclose regional costs or profit.

**Consolidated profit and cost pressures**
Revenue grew 10% to $12,241 million, but net income fell 5% to $2,511 million from $2,648 million, and net margin declined from 24% to 21%. Sales and marketing rose 20% to $2,588 million, with field operations and policy up 43% to $993 million. Product development rose 14% to $2,354 million, mainly from a $293 million increase in payroll. Stock-based compensation rose 13% to $1,592 million. Cost of revenue rose 11%, including a $188 million increase in merchant fees on higher pay-in volumes. Lower interest income ($705 million vs. $818 million) and a $72 million swing in other expense also reduced pre-tax income.

**Items the filing does not support**
The filing gives no market share figure; regional revenue share is the only share measure. It names no strategic partner except an unnamed data hosting provider with a commitment of at least $1.7 billion through 2031. It does not quantify the effect of the services and redesigned experiences launched in May 2025. On policy, the OBBBA (July 4, 2025) changed R&D expensing and foreign-derived intangible income taxation, and led to a $213 million CAMT valuation allowance. Non-income tax matters added $74 million to general and administrative expense.
