Airbnb is the only company in the resources. The filing reports results by geographic region rather than by offering (stays, experiences, services), so the business lines below are the four regions. Each region has one summary sentence and one explanatory sentence.

**North America:** Revenue rose 4% to $5.2 billion in 2025 from $5.0 billion in 2024, the slowest growth of the four regions, and its share of total revenue fell from 45% to 42%. Nights and Seats Booked grew 3% to 158 million and GBV grew 7%, which the filing does not explain, though it attributes the company-wide growth to "strong growth across all regions." The region's 4.1 average nights per booking was the highest of the four regions, but its volume growth was the weakest.

**EMEA:** Revenue rose 14% to $4.7 billion, making it the second-largest region after North America, and its share of revenue rose from 37% to 39%. Nights and Seats Booked grew 7% to 215 million and GBV grew 15%, and the filing states that EMEA's 8% ADR increase was the primary driver of the company's 3% ADR increase, so growth came from both more bookings and higher prices.

**Latin America:** Revenue rose 20% to $1.2 billion, the fastest revenue growth of the four regions, lifting its share of total revenue from 9% to 10%. Nights and Seats Booked grew 18% to 90 million and GBV grew 20%, and the filing attributes this to the "strong growth across all regions" without giving region-specific causes. Average nights per booking were 3.6, below North America and EMEA.

**Asia Pacific:** Revenue rose 17% to $1.2 billion, and the region held 9% of total revenue in both years. Nights and Seats Booked grew 15% to 70 million, and GBV grew 16%, but the region's 3.3 average nights per booking was the lowest of the four, and the filing does not explain the difference.
