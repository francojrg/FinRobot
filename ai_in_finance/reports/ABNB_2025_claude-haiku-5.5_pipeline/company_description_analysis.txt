**Company and industry**
Founded in 2007, Airbnb, Inc. is a global online marketplace that provides stays, experiences, and services connecting guests and hosts in over 220 countries and regions. It reports over 5 million hosts and 2.5 billion guest arrivals to date. The industry is online travel and short-term rental marketplaces.

**Core strengths**
Scale is the main advantage. Nights and Seats Booked rose 8% to 533 million in 2025, and Gross Booking Value (GBV) rose 12% to $91.3 billion. Service fees are collected at booking, which produced Free Cash Flow (FCF) of $4.6 billion, a 38% margin. Cash and short-term investments totaled $11.0 billion. AirCover for Hosts provides property damage protection of up to $3 million per stay.

**End market, customers, and market share**
Revenue by region in 2025: North America $5.2 billion (42% of total, up 4%); EMEA $4.7 billion (39%, up 14%); Latin America $1.2 billion (10%, up 20%); Asia Pacific $1.2 billion (9%, up 17%). The filing gives no market share figure and no customer concentration data. No single city exceeded 2% of revenue. Named competitors include Booking Holdings, Expedia Group, Marriott, Hilton, and Viator.

**Trends, opportunities, and challenges**
Costs outpaced revenue. Costs and expenses rose 13% against 10% revenue growth, and operating margin fell to 21% from 23%. Sales and marketing rose 20%, led by field operations and policy (up 43%). Interest income fell 14% on lower rates. Regulation limits supply: New York City's 2023 rules amount to a de facto ban on short-term rentals. Growth outside North America is the main opportunity.

**Strategic initiatives and response**
In May 2025 Airbnb launched services and redesigned experiences, and it rolled out an app with unified search, AI personalization, and Pay Less Upfront and Reserve Now, Pay Later. It substantially completed a technology stack rebuild and expanded AI in fraud detection and customer service. It repurchased $3.8 billion of stock (29.7 million shares) in 2025, with $5.6 billion remaining. The filing does not describe cost reductions.

---

**Summary**
Airbnb grew bookings strongly in 2025 but converted less of that growth into profit. Revenue rose 10% to $12.2 billion and GBV rose 12%, yet net income fell 5% and operating margin fell two points to 21%, because sales and marketing and stock-based compensation grew faster than revenue. Growth is fastest outside North America, and cash of $11.0 billion covers the $2.0 billion of convertible notes due March 2026. Margins should stabilize if cost growth slows toward revenue growth. Illustratively, each one-point change in Adjusted EBITDA margin on 2025 revenue equals about $122 million. This view would be wrong if 2026 costs again grow faster than revenue without margin recovery.
