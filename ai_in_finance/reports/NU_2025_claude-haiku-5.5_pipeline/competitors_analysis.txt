# Nu Holdings Ltd. (NU): Financial Metrics Against Inter & Co, SoFi and PagSeguro, Fiscal Years 2022 to 2025

Nu Holdings grew revenue faster than any peer in the set, from 2,971 in fiscal 2022 to 10,627 in fiscal 2025, a 3.6-fold increase (about 53% a year, illustrative compound rate). Growth has slowed each year, though: 89.6% in 2023, 46.8% in 2024 and 28.5% in 2025. Its multiples expanded over the same period, so the market has paid more for each unit of revenue even as growth slowed. The figures below use the units of the source table, which does not state a currency, and the fiscal year labels as given. The resources do not confirm that fiscal year ends are aligned across companies, so the year-by-year comparisons are approximate.

## Data coverage

The resource reports Nu's gross profit, gross margin, EBITDA, EBITDA margin, ROIC and EV/EBITDA as n/a or n/m in every year. The same is true of Inter and SoFi for gross margin, EBITDA margin, ROIC and EV/EBITDA. Only PagSeguro has values for those metrics. Peer comparison on those five measures is therefore not possible, and PagSeguro's figures serve only as a reference profile for a traditional payments processor. Revenue, revenue growth, FCF, FCF conversion, EV/Sales, P/E and P/B are available for all four companies, so the comparison below rests on those metrics. The resource also does not define FCF conversion, so the conversion figures are taken as reported.

## Nu Holdings: year-over-year trends

Revenue growth slowed in every year, and the absolute increase in revenue was nearly flat. Revenue rose by 2,661 in 2023, 2,638 in 2024 and 2,357 in 2025. A business whose dollar gains are shrinking will show falling percentage growth even while it keeps adding scale, and that is what the data show. Growth of 28.5% in 2025 is still high, but the trend is clearly downward.

Free cash flow moved the other way. It rose from 641 in 2022 to 1,089 in 2023, 2,224 in 2024 and 3,160 in 2025, roughly 4.9 times the 2022 level. Growth in 2025 was 42% (3,160 against 2,224), slower than the 104% gain in 2024 but still substantial. FCF conversion stayed close to 1 in each year: 1.06 in 2023, 1.13 in 2024 and 1.10 in 2025. The conversion ratio held steady while FCF grew, which points to cash generation keeping pace with the reported base.

Valuation multiples rose sharply. EV/Sales went from 4.32 in 2022 to 4.84 in 2023, eased to 4.46 in 2024 and then rose to 5.86 in 2025, a 31% increase in one year. Price-to-book climbed from 3.91 in 2022 to 6.20, 6.53 and 7.20 in 2025. The P/E ratio was 39.27 in 2023, fell to 25.68 in 2024 and rose to 28.63 in 2025. The 2024 decline in P/E and the 2025 rebound are consistent with reported earnings growing faster than the price in 2024 and the price rising faster than earnings in 2025, but the resource does not give Nu's earnings, so that is an inference from the ratios alone.

## Competitor comparison, year by year

In 2022, Nu's revenue of 2,971 was below Inter's 3,563 and PagSeguro's 15,335, and above SoFi's 1,574. By 2023 Nu had passed Inter (5,632 against 4,753), and by 2025 it led both Inter (8,401) and SoFi (3,613). PagSeguro, at 20,411 in 2025, remained the largest by revenue.

On revenue growth, Nu led the set in 2023 (89.6% against 33.4% for Inter, 34.0% for SoFi and 4.0% for PagSeguro) and in 2024 (46.8% against 34.7% for Inter, 23.9% for SoFi and 17.9% for PagSeguro). In 2025 the order changed. SoFi grew 38.3%, Inter 31.3%, Nu 28.5% and PagSeguro 8.5%. Nu's growth in its most recent year was below both Inter and SoFi, and this is the clearest sign in the data that its growth lead is narrowing.

On FCF conversion, Nu was the only company with a stable ratio near 1 in every year that the data cover. Inter's ratios were 24.05 in 2023, 3.58 in 2024 and 5.46 in 2025. The 2023 figure implies a very small denominator relative to FCF of 7,270 (illustratively, about 302 if the ratio is FCF divided by a base). SoFi's ratio was negative in 2024 (-2.57) and 2025 (-8.3) because its FCF was negative. PagSeguro's ratio was 1.22 in 2023, -2.71 in 2024 and 2.50 in 2025, mirroring its own negative FCF year. Nu's conversion is the most consistent in the set, but Inter's FCF in absolute terms was larger than Nu's in 2023 (7,270 against 1,089) and in 2025 (7,160 against 3,160).

On valuation, Nu's 2025 EV/Sales of 5.86 was second only to SoFi's 8.38, and well above Inter (2.89) and PagSeguro (2.73). Nu's 2025 P/B of 7.20 was the highest in the set, ahead of SoFi (3.17), Inter (2.02) and PagSeguro (0.99). Its 2025 P/E of 28.63 sat between Inter's 15.62 and SoFi's 67.13, and well above PagSeguro's 6.85.

## Metric-specific insights

**EBITDA margin.** Only PagSeguro reports this metric: 39.4% in 2022, 41.6% in 2023, 41.1% in 2024 and 42.8% in 2025. The resource does not give Nu's margin, so a profitability comparison with Nu is not possible. Nu's FCF and FCF conversion are the only cash-based profitability signals available for it.

**EV/EBITDA.** Nu's EV/EBITDA is n/m in every year, so the most direct valuation check cannot be applied to Nu. PagSeguro's multiple was 4.12 in 2022, 6.57 in 2023, 6.63 in 2024 and 6.38 in 2025. As a cross-check on that series, PagSeguro's 2025 EV/Sales of 2.73 on revenue of 20,411 implies an EV of about 55,720, and its EV/EBITDA of 6.38 on EBITDA of 8,743 implies about 55,780 (both illustrative and consistent with each other). For Nu, EV/Sales is the nearest available proxy, and on that basis Nu traded at a premium to Inter and PagSeguro in every year from 2022 to 2025 and at a discount to SoFi in every year. Whether Nu is over- or undervalued cannot be determined from these data, because the resource gives no fair-value estimate.

**FCF conversion.** Nu's conversion of about 1.06 to 1.13 is consistent across the three years. Inter's conversion is volatile, and SoFi's and PagSeguro's are negative in the years their FCF was negative. For PagSeguro, the resource reports FCF of -5,737 in 2024 after 2,011 in 2023 and 5,286 in 2025, but does not explain the cause. The data show the swing without a stated reason.

**Gross margin.** Only PagSeguro reports this metric: 51.3% in 2022, 49.0% in 2023, 49.3% in 2024 and 52.5% in 2025. Gross profit fell slightly in 2023, from 7,864 to 7,816 (down 0.6%), while revenue grew 4.0%, so the margin dropped by 2.3 points. Gross profit then rose to 9,266 in 2024 and 10,714 in 2025, and the margin recovered to its highest level in the series in 2025. The resource does not state why the 2023 margin fell, so no cause is assigned here. Nu has no gross margin data.

**ROIC.** PagSeguro's ROIC fell from 17.6% in 2022 to 10.6% in 2023 and has been roughly flat since (9.9% in 2024 and 10.0% in 2025). The decline in 2023 was the largest single-year change in the series, and the stable level since then suggests returns have settled rather than continued to fall. The resource provides no ROIC figure for Nu, so its capital efficiency cannot be judged on this measure.

**Revenue and revenue growth.** Nu's revenue rose each year and its growth rate fell each year, from 89.6% to 46.8% to 28.5%. The growth in its most recent year was lower than Inter's and SoFi's. SoFi's growth reaccelerated from 23.9% in 2024 to 38.3% in 2025, and the resource does not explain that change. PagSeguro's revenue growth was 4.0% in 2023, 17.9% in 2024 and 8.5% in 2025. Over the full period, the revenue compound rate from 2022 to 2025 was about 53% for Nu, 33% for Inter, 32% for SoFi and 10% for PagSeguro (all illustrative, calculated from the table values).

## Conclusion

Nu's financial profile in these data is one of rapid scale and steady cash generation, with a clear slowdown. Revenue has grown from 2,971 to 10,627 in three years, and FCF has risen almost fivefold to 3,160, with FCF conversion close to 1 in each year. Those facts support a business that is converting growth into cash. Against that, the rate of growth has fallen from 89.6% to 28.5%, Nu's 2025 growth trailed both Inter and SoFi, and the resource contains no profitability or return metrics for Nu, so its margins and returns cannot be checked against peers.

The valuation is the element that the operating data do least to support. Nu's EV/Sales rose from 4.32 to 5.86 while its growth rate fell, and in 2025 it traded at roughly twice Inter's and PagSeguro's EV/Sales despite growing more slowly than Inter and far more slowly than SoFi. A premium of that size needs the growth to hold or the margins to rise, and the resource shows neither. Price-to-book reached 7.20, the highest in the group, on a company whose reported return data are absent.

The view would be wrong if the next fiscal years show revenue growth holding above roughly 25% with FCF conversion staying near 1, which would indicate that the deceleration has stopped and the multiple is backed by durable cash flow. The view would be strengthened if Nu's FCF conversion fell below 1 or FCF growth stalled, since that would leave the premium EV/Sales unsupported by cash generation. Investors who want to test the current valuation directly will need Nu's EBITDA, gross profit and ROIC, which the resource does not supply.
