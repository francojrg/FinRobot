The filing reports one consolidated group, so the business lines below are the reported revenue lines plus the country operations. Figures are for 2025 versus 2024, in US$ millions unless stated.

**Credit card interest income:** Interest income on credit cards rose 20.9% to 4,597.8, the slowest of the three large interest lines against 37.0% growth in total revenue. The card receivables balance grew to 21.7 billion from 14.6 billion, but expected credit loss on card receivables rose 17.0% (342.9), and the filing states that total ECL on cards and loans was 15.4% of receivables in both years.

**Loan interest income:** Loan interest income rose 57.4% to 4,784.3 from 3,038.7, the largest dollar increase of any interest line (+1,745.6). Loans to customers reached 9.4 billion from 5.3 billion (+77%), and loan expected credit loss rose 58.0% (+666.7), which the filing attributes mainly to portfolio growth.

**Interest on deposit-funded assets:** Interest income on other assets at amortized cost rose 78.6% to 2,284.6, the fastest-growing interest line, driven by voluntary deposit growth and higher securities held at amortized cost. The cost of that funding is the main risk to the spread: interest expense on deposits rose 65.5% to 3,876.5 while deposits grew 45.3% in US dollars (29.0% FX-neutral), and the filing does not report the net spread directly.

**Credit and prepaid card income:** Card income rose 24.6% to 1,720.3, and the filing states it equalled 1.2% of purchase volume. Purchase volume grew 13% in reported dollars but 17% FX-neutral, and the gap reflects a weaker real against the dollar in 2025 average rates (R$5.586 versus R$5.392 per US$ in 2024).

**Late fees:** Late fee income rose 33.9% to 385.0 from 287.5, tracking a 58% growth in the portfolio during 2025. Because these fees are charged when credit card bills are not paid by the due date, the line grows with the receivables base and depends on repayment behavior.

**Insurance commission and other fee income:** Insurance commission rose 22.0% to 35.5 and other fee and commission income rose 5.5% to 199.2, together about 10% of fee income. Other fee income includes prepaid recharges, boleto commissions, cash withdrawal fees and marketplace commissions, and its slow growth against 37.0% total revenue growth shows these lines are not yet a major driver of results.

**Brazil:** Brazil had 113 million customers at year-end 2025, about 62% of the population aged 18 and above, and its NPL 15-90 days rate was 3.9% and NPL 90+ days rate was 6.6%. The filing does not split revenue or expenses by country, and it reports a ratio of about 14,314 customers per employee against 1,234 for incumbent banks.

**Mexico and Colombia:** Mexico reached 14.1 million customers and Colombia surpassed 4 million, roughly double the prior year, with Colombian deposits at US$2.5 billion after the Cuenta Nu launch in mid-2024. The filing reports no country-level revenue or profit, so these operations can only be assessed through customer, deposit and card-issuance metrics.

**United States (Nubank N.A.):** The OCC granted conditional approval on January 29, 2026, but the bank has not commenced banking operations and still needs FDIC and Federal Reserve approvals. The filing contains no revenue, customer or cost data for this entity, so it contributes no reported performance in 2025.
