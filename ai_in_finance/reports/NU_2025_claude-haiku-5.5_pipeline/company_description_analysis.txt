## Company Overview

Founded in 2013, Nu Holdings Ltd. (Nubank) is a digital financial services platform, incorporated in the Cayman Islands in 2016, that provides credit and prepaid cards, deposit accounts, personal and secured loans, investments, insurance, and travel and mobile services through one app. It had 131.0 million customers at December 31, 2025, up 15.0% from 114.2 million a year earlier: 113 million in Brazil, 14.1 million in Mexico, and over 4 million in Colombia.

**Industry and end market:** Retail banking in Brazil, Mexico and Colombia, where the five largest incumbents hold 68% to 80% of loans and deposits. The company estimates the retail financial revenue pool in these markets at US$227.9 billion in 2025, up 7% (11% FX-neutral), and its share at about 5%.

**Market position and customers:** Nu held 14.7% of Brazil's card purchase volume in 2025, up from 14.2% in 2024 (ABECS data). Its customers are individuals and SMEs. The filing does not disclose customer concentration or any major customer.

**Core strengths:** Most customers were acquired organically (80% to 90% per year). Customer acquisition cost was US$7.4 in 2025, with paid marketing about 32% of it. Monthly ARPAC rose to US$13.3 from US$11.3. Net churn averaged 0.1% per month. The company runs its own NuCore platform and NuX credit engine, and deposits reached US$41.9 billion, all acquired directly.

**Trends and challenges:** Pix transaction volume rose 29% in 2025. Digital banks held over 8% of Brazilian individual loans by September 2025, up from below 1% in 2018. Brazil's GDP growth slowed to 2.3% from 3.4%, and average CDI rose to 14.3% from 10.8%.

**Recent initiatives:** More than 100 products launched in 2025, including Brazilian payroll loans and a Colombian subscription card. Nubank N.A. received conditional OCC approval on January 29, 2026. The company acquired Hyperplane (AI) in July 2024 and made a minority investment in Tyme Group in December 2024.

## Summary

Nu grew 2025 revenue 37.0% to US$15.8 billion and net income 45.6% to US$2.87 billion. Gross margin fell to 42.0% from 45.6%, because expected credit losses (up 32.7%) and deposit interest expense (up 65.5%) grew faster than gross profit. Credit losses held at 15.4% of receivables. Management expects 2026 operating costs to rise from international expansion. Deposits grew 45.3% in US dollars but 29% FX-neutral, so currency moves inflated the reported figures.
