# Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc. (TMO) Against Danaher Corporation (DHR), Agilent Technologies, Inc. (A), and IQVIA Holdings Inc. (IQV), Fiscal 2022 to 2025

TMO's fiscal 2025 revenue of 44,557 is still 0.8% below its fiscal 2022 level of 44,915, even though it grew 3.9% in fiscal 2025. Its EBITDA margin of 25.8% in fiscal 2025 is the lowest of the four companies except IQV's 21.2%, no, wait: IQV is lower at 21.2%, so TMO ranks third of four behind Danaher Corporation's 28.3% and Agilent's 26.4%. Its gross margin, ROIC, and free cash flow conversion have all weakened in the most recent year, while its enterprise value to EBITDA multiple rose to 21.51, the highest in its own four-year series. The units for the figures are not labeled in the resource, so scale is described only in the units as presented. Fiscal year ends may differ across these companies, and the resource does not state them, so each comparison below uses the fiscal labels as given.

## Revenue and Revenue Growth

TMO's revenue fell from 44,915 in fiscal 2022 to 42,857 in fiscal 2023, a decline of 4.6%. Fiscal 2024 was essentially flat at 42,879 (growth of 0.1%), and fiscal 2025 rose to 44,557 (growth of 3.9%). The recovery is real but incomplete: two years of modest growth have not restored the fiscal 2022 base. The decline was broad across the group. Danaher Corporation's revenue fell 10.3% in fiscal 2023, the steepest drop among the four, and Agilent's revenue fell 4.7% in fiscal 2024. IQVIA Holdings Inc. was the only company that grew in every year, with growth of 4.0%, 2.8%, and 5.9%, and its fiscal 2025 revenue of 16,310 is 13.2% above its fiscal 2022 figure.

TMO remains the largest company in this group by revenue. Its fiscal 2025 revenue is about 1.8 times Danaher Corporation's 24,568, and about 6.4 times Agilent's 6,948. Scale does not translate into faster growth here. In fiscal 2025, Agilent grew 6.7% and IQVIA Holdings grew 5.9%, both faster than TMO's 3.9%. Danaher Corporation's 2.9% was slower than TMO's rate.

## Gross Margin

TMO's gross margin fell from 42.2% in fiscal 2022 to 39.9% in fiscal 2023, the period of its lowest revenue. It recovered to 41.3% in fiscal 2024 and eased to 40.9% in fiscal 2025. Gross profit followed the same path: 18,971 in fiscal 2022, 17,100 in fiscal 2023, 17,702 in fiscal 2024, and 18,239 in fiscal 2025. Fiscal 2025 gross profit is still 3.9% below fiscal 2022, even though revenue is only 0.8% lower. The margin has therefore not fully returned to its prior level, and the 2025 dip of 0.4 points means the recovery stalled.

Relative to peers, TMO's gross margin is well below Danaher Corporation's 59.1% and Agilent's 52.4% in fiscal 2025. It is above IQVIA Holdings Inc.'s 33.3%. The gap to Danaher Corporation was 18.6 points in fiscal 2022 and 18.2 points in fiscal 2025, so it has narrowed only slightly. The resource does not explain the cause of the margin movements, so this analysis does not attribute them to specific factors.

## EBITDA Margin

TMO's EBITDA margin was 26.6% in fiscal 2022, 25.9% in fiscal 2023, 26.9% in fiscal 2024, and 25.8% in fiscal 2025. The fiscal 2025 figure is 0.8 points below fiscal 2022 and 1.1 points below fiscal 2024. Absolute EBITDA moved from 11,942 to 11,079 to 11,535 to 11,507. The fiscal 2025 result is 3.6% below fiscal 2022 and essentially flat against fiscal 2024 (down 0.2%). Because revenue rose 3.9% in fiscal 2025 while EBITDA was flat, the margin fell. This is the most direct evidence that the fiscal 2025 revenue growth did not carry through to profit.

In fiscal 2025, Danaher Corporation's EBITDA margin of 28.3% is 2.5 points above TMO's, and Agilent's 26.4% is 0.6 points above. IQVIA Holdings Inc. at 21.2% is 4.6 points below TMO. The competitive picture has shifted over time. In fiscal 2022, Danaher Corporation led the group at 35.6%, but its margin has declined in each year since, to 31.4%, 30.5%, and 28.3%. TMO's margin has been more stable, and it has sat in a narrow range of 25.8% to 26.9% throughout the period. Against Danaher Corporation, TMO's gap closed from 9.0 points in fiscal 2022 to 2.5 points in fiscal 2025, but this came from Danaher Corporation's decline rather than from TMO improving.

## Free Cash Flow Conversion

TMO's free cash flow was 6,911 in fiscal 2022, 6,927 in fiscal 2023, 7,267 in fiscal 2024, and 6,293 in fiscal 2025. The fiscal 2025 figure is 13.4% below fiscal 2024, which is the largest single-year decline in TMO's free cash flow in this series. FCF conversion, as given in the resource, was 0.99, 1.16, 1.15, and 0.94. The resource does not define the denominator, so the conversion ratio cannot be reconciled to the other figures. The fiscal 2025 reading of 0.94 means free cash flow was below the base the ratio uses, after two years above 1.0.

The peer comparison is unfavorable for TMO in fiscal 2025. IQVIA Holdings Inc. converted at 1.51, Danaher Corporation at 1.46, TMO at 0.94, and Agilent at 0.88. Danaher Corporation's conversion rose in every year, from 1.03 to 1.46, while its free cash flow fell from 7,401 to 5,260. This means its conversion gains came from a lower earnings base rather than from higher cash generation, and that pattern should be checked against the denominator before drawing conclusions. TMO's conversion has been at or near the group's middle in most years.

## Return on Invested Capital

TMO's ROIC was 9.9% in fiscal 2022, 9.0% in fiscal 2023, 9.5% in fiscal 2024, and 8.7% in fiscal 2025. The fiscal 2025 figure is the lowest in TMO's series and is 1.2 points below fiscal 2022. The decline is consistent with the margin and cash flow pattern: returns are lower because profit and cash generation did not grow with revenue. In fiscal 2025, Agilent's ROIC of 13.9% is far above the others. IQVIA Holdings Inc. reported 8.8%, just above TMO's 8.7%, and Danaher Corporation reported 5.4%, the lowest in the group. Agilent's ROIC has declined each year, from 16.4% to 13.9%, but it remains well ahead of the rest of the group.

TMO's ROIC has been close to IQVIA Holdings Inc.'s for much of the period. In fiscal 2023, IQVIA Holdings Inc. was ahead at 10.0% against TMO's 9.0%, and in fiscal 2024 the gap was 9.6% against 9.5%. Danaher Corporation's ROIC fell from 9.6% in fiscal 2022 to 5.4% in fiscal 2025, which means TMO was ahead of Danaher Corporation in every year of the series by a widening margin. The recent trend for TMO is a decline in returns on the invested capital base, and the resource does not state how that base changed.

## EV/EBITDA and Valuation

TMO's enterprise value to EBITDA multiple was 20.18 in fiscal 2022, 20.95 in fiscal 2023, 19.54 in fiscal 2024, and 21.51 in fiscal 2025. The fiscal 2025 figure is the highest in TMO's series. The multiple rose by about 2.0 turns in one year while EBITDA was flat. That rise is a market judgment about future earnings, not a result the four-year data have yet shown.

Comparing across companies, Danaher Corporation's multiple has risen steadily from 19.62 to 25.47, the highest in the group in fiscal 2025. Agilent's multiple was 23.43 in fiscal 2025, and IQVIA Holdings Inc.'s multiple fell from 17.13 to 10.46 over the period, the lowest in the group. In fiscal 2022, TMO's 20.18 was below Agilent's 22.33 and above Danaher Corporation's 19.62 and IQVIA Holdings Inc.'s 17.13. In fiscal 2023, TMO's 20.95 sat between Danaher Corporation's 24.64 and Agilent's 18.38 and above IQVIA Holdings Inc.'s 16.74. In fiscal 2024, TMO's 19.54 was below Danaher Corporation's 24.76 and Agilent's 20.93 and above IQVIA Holdings Inc.'s 13.63. In fiscal 2025, TMO's 21.51 is below both Danaher Corporation and Agilent and above IQVIA Holdings Inc.

As an illustration, the simple average of the three peers' fiscal 2025 multiples is 19.79, so TMO's 21.51 is about 9% above that average. The median of the three peers is 23.43, so TMO is about 8% below the median. The result depends on which peer is used as the reference. The data do not establish whether TMO is over or undervalued. What the data do show is that TMO's multiple has risen while its margin, return, and cash flow measures have weakened, so the valuation is resting more on expected recovery than on the four-year record.

## Year-by-Year Competitive Position

In fiscal 2022, TMO was mid-pack. Its revenue of 44,915 was the largest in the group, its gross margin of 42.2% was third behind Danaher Corporation's 60.8% and Agilent's 54.4%, its EBITDA margin of 26.6% was second behind Danaher Corporation's 35.6%, its FCF conversion of 0.99 was below IQVIA Holdings Inc.'s 1.45 and Danaher Corporation's 1.03, and its ROIC of 9.9% was ahead of Danaher Corporation's 9.6% and IQVIA Holdings Inc.'s 7.8% but behind Agilent's 16.4%.

In fiscal 2023, TMO's revenue decline of 4.6% was the second-smallest among the four companies after Agilent's 0.2% decline, and its gross margin of 39.9% was second from the bottom. Its EBITDA margin of 25.9% was third, behind Danaher Corporation's 31.4% and Agilent's 25.0% was lower. Its ROIC of 9.0% was third, behind IQVIA Holdings Inc.'s 10.0%.

In fiscal 2024, TMO's revenue was flat, while IQVIA Holdings Inc. grew 2.8% and Agilent declined 4.7%. Its gross margin of 41.3% was third. Its EBITDA margin of 26.9% was second behind Danaher Corporation's 30.5%, and its free cash flow of 7,267 was the group's highest in absolute terms, although the resource's conversion ratio of 1.15 was below IQVIA Holdings Inc.'s 1.54 and Danaher Corporation's 1.36.

In fiscal 2025, TMO ranks third on gross margin, third on EBITDA margin, third on FCF conversion, and third on ROIC. It is the largest company by revenue and its growth of 3.9% ranks third behind Agilent and IQVIA Holdings Inc. The fiscal 2025 position is weaker than fiscal 2024 on margin, cash conversion, and returns.

## Conclusion

TMO's four-year record shows a company that absorbed a revenue decline in fiscal 2023, returned to growth in fiscal 2025, and has not yet converted that growth into profit or returns. Revenue in fiscal 2025 is still below fiscal 2022. EBITDA is flat over fiscal 2024 to fiscal 2025 and below fiscal 2022. The EBITDA margin, gross margin, ROIC, and free cash flow all weakened in the most recent year. The balance of these measures is stable rather than deteriorating sharply, and the business remains the largest in the group, but it does not show the improvement that the highest multiple in its series would require.

The valuation is therefore the central question. An enterprise value to EBITDA multiple of 21.51 implies that the market expects earnings to grow from the fiscal 2025 base. The four-year data do not yet support that expectation, because EBITDA has not grown since fiscal 2024. The multiple is below Danaher Corporation's and Agilent's, which is consistent with the two peers having stronger margins or returns, and above IQVIA Holdings Inc.'s, which has grown more steadily but at lower margins.

The view that TMO's valuation is justified would be proven wrong if the next reported year shows revenue growth below about 4% with the EBITDA margin staying under 26.9%, the fiscal 2024 level. It would also be contradicted if FCF conversion remains below 1.0 and ROIC stays under 9.5%, the fiscal 2024 level. Conversely, a return to EBITDA margin above 26.9% and ROIC above 9.5%, alongside revenue growth sustained near the fiscal 2025 rate of 3.9%, would support the current multiple, because the earnings base would then be expanding in line with the valuation.
