# Thermo Fisher Scientific: Top Three Risks

The filing's three most consequential exposures are, in order: (1) the value of $65.19 billion of acquisition-related goodwill and intangible assets, which depends on integration and future cash flows; (2) leverage of $39.38 billion in debt bounded by a 3.5:1.0 interest coverage covenant; and (3) demand for its products from customers whose spending follows budgets, research funding and government policy, which the filing says can be cyclical. The filing does not provide revenue, operating income, EBITDA, interest expense, cash flow or total assets, so the quantification below is limited to the balance-sheet and debt figures and the one currency figure the filing gives. Where I use these figures in a ratio, I label it as illustrative.

## Risk 1: Acquisition-Driven Asset Values and Integration

The filing states that the company has recorded goodwill of approximately $49.36 billion, indefinite-lived intangible assets (primarily trade names) of $1.23 billion, and definite-lived intangible assets of $14.60 billion, all as of December 31, 2025. Combined, these total roughly $65.19 billion (illustrative sum of the three figures). The filing says that the realizability of these assets "will depend on the future cash flows of these businesses," and that those cash flows "depend in part on how well we have integrated these businesses." It also warns that acquisitions may be made "at a substantial premium over the fair value of the net identifiable assets of the acquired company," and that the company may not realize anticipated cost savings or synergies. The consequence of a shortfall is an impairment charge, which the filing says could be "material."

**Industry vertical risk.** The filing does not benchmark acquisition risk against other sectors. Within its own disclosures, the risk is specific to a company that has grown mainly through acquisition. Goodwill is a non-cash asset, so an impairment reduces reported equity and earnings without reducing cash. That makes the risk an accounting and valuation risk first, and a cash risk only to the extent that the acquired businesses underperform. The filing also notes that antitrust and other regulatory approvals may delay or block deals, which adds execution uncertainty to the acquisition pipeline.

**Cyclicality.** Impairment testing is tied to operating losses or a significant decline in earnings at an acquired business. A cyclical downturn in an end market that depresses those earnings would therefore raise impairment exposure, and the filing acknowledges that "certain of our businesses operate in industries that may experience periodic, cyclical downturns." The filing does not quantify how much of the $65.19 billion sits in cyclical units, so the cyclical share of this risk cannot be measured from the document.

**Downside protections.** The filing describes annual goodwill assessments and tests whenever circumstances indicate impairment, which are monitoring controls rather than protections. It does not describe the reporting-unit structure, the headroom in those tests, or any sensitivity analysis, so the cushion before an impairment is triggered is unknown.

**What would prove this view wrong.** The view would be overstated if the acquired businesses continue to produce the cash flows that support their carrying values and the company reports no impairment charges across the annual tests. Conversely, a goodwill or trade-name impairment, or a disclosed earnings decline at a major acquired unit, would confirm that integration and acquisition returns are the binding constraint.

## Risk 2: Leverage and Covenant Compliance

The company had approximately $39.38 billion of outstanding indebtedness as of December 31, 2025, and a revolving credit facility providing up to $5.00 billion of unsecured multi-currency credit. Illustratively, total debt is about 60% of the $65.19 billion of acquired goodwill and intangibles, and fully drawing the facility would raise potential borrowings to about $44.38 billion. The filing requires a minimum Consolidated Net Interest Coverage Ratio of 3.5:1.0, measured as Consolidated EBITDA to Consolidated Net Interest Expense at each fiscal quarter-end. A breach would be an event of default, which could permit acceleration of that debt and, through cross-default provisions, of other debt. The filing also says the company "may also obtain additional long-term debt and lines of credit," which would increase leverage further.

**Industry vertical risk.** The filing does not compare its leverage with peers or with other industries. Its own description of the risk is that leverage limits the company's ability to "acquire new products and technologies through strategic acquisitions," which links this risk directly to Risk 1. Debt funds acquisitions, and acquisitions produce the intangible assets that carry impairment risk.

**Cyclicality.** The covenant measures EBITDA against interest expense, so a fall in earnings in a downturn tightens the ratio at the same time that refinancing may become harder. The filing says the company's ability to comply "is dependent on our future performance, which is subject to prevailing economic conditions," and also cites foreign exchange and interest rates as factors beyond its control. Because the filing gives no EBITDA or interest expense figure, the headroom above 3.5:1.0 cannot be calculated from the document.

**Risk quantification.** The data points are the $39.38 billion of debt, the $5.00 billion facility, the 3.5:1.0 covenant and the cross-default mechanism. The filing does not disclose the maturity profile, the interest rate mix or the current coverage ratio.

**Downside protections.** The $5.00 billion revolver is a source of liquidity that the company can draw, subject to its covenant conditions. The filing does not describe cash balances, free cash flow or the refinancing schedule, so the size of the protection relative to the debt cannot be assessed.

**What would prove this view wrong.** The leverage risk would be overstated if the company reports EBITDA comfortably above 3.5 times net interest expense and extends or refinances its maturities without strain. It would be confirmed by a reported coverage ratio approaching the 3.5:1.0 floor, a drawdown of the revolver to fund operations rather than acquisitions, or a rating action.

## Risk 3: Demand Cyclicality Tied to Customer and Government Spending

The filing states that "certain of our businesses operate in industries that may experience periodic, cyclical downturns," and that demand for some products depends on the capital spending policies of customers including pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies, laboratories, universities, healthcare providers, government agencies and research institutions. It names public policy spending priorities, available resources, cost reimbursement policies and product and economic cycles as drivers. It also notes that some customer spending "fluctuates based on budget allocations and the timely passage of the annual federal budget," and that the federal government entered a shutdown in October 2025. The filing adds that macroeconomic conditions can cause order cancellations or delays, excess inventory, pricing pressure and longer collection cycles. On currency, the filing reports that translation "had a favorable effect of $0.37 billion on revenues" in 2025, which shows how exposure to exchange rates can move reported results in either direction. The filing does not give total revenue, so this amount cannot be expressed as a percentage.

**Industry vertical risk.** The filing describes its markets as "characterized by rapid and significant technological changes," which means product obsolescence is a second competitive risk alongside cyclicality. It also operates under extensive FDA, DEA and EMA regulation, and says that changes in FDA oversight of drug discovery "could have an adverse effect on the demand for these products." The filing does not place these exposures in a ranking against other sectors, so a comparative judgment about the vertical's risk level cannot be drawn from the document alone.

**Cyclicality.** The filing describes a sequence of effects from weaker economic conditions: reduced demand, longer sales cycles and "slower adoption of new technologies." It also says that inflation "continue[s] at elevated levels" in the U.S. and in other countries where the company operates. Customer budgets that are set annually, and government funding that can slow during a budget impasse, make the timing of demand less predictable than the long-run trend.

**Risk quantification.** The data points are the October 2025 federal shutdown, the $0.37 billion favorable currency translation in 2025 and the list of customer categories. The filing does not quantify the share of revenue derived from government-funded customers or from cyclical end markets.

**Downside protections.** The customer base is broad, spanning pharma, biotech, academic, healthcare and government customers, which spreads demand across several funding sources. Some government sales are made under agreements, which the filing describes as subject to specific statutes and regulations. The company says it "may seek to minimize the impact of price increases through higher prices to customers and various cost-saving measures." These mitigants are described qualitatively; the filing gives no data on their magnitude.

**What would prove this view wrong.** The cyclicality concern would be overstated if organic growth in its served markets holds up through periods of budget uncertainty and if order cancellations and delays remain limited. It would be confirmed by reported declines in demand from academic or government customers after the shutdown, rising order delays, or a reversal of the favorable currency effect that flatters reported revenue.

## Overall Assessment

The filing's risk profile is driven less by any single operating weakness and more by the interaction of its three exposures. The company's growth has depended on acquisitions, which created $65.19 billion of goodwill and intangibles and were financed in part by $39.38 billion of debt. The value of that balance sheet depends on the same acquired businesses performing through the cycles that the filing says affect its customers. A downturn in customer or government spending would reduce earnings, which would tighten the coverage covenant and raise impairment exposure in the same period. The filing does not provide the earnings and cash flow data needed to measure how much headroom exists against these pressures, so the assessment rests on the structure of the risks rather than on their current magnitude. The filing also says it does not expect the OECD Pillar Two minimum tax rule to have a material effect on its effective tax rate, which removes one potential pressure from this list, though the filing acknowledges that tax rates may still move for other reasons.
