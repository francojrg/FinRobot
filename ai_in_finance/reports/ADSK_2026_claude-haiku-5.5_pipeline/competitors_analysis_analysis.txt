# Autodesk (ADSK): Financial Metrics Against Bentley Systems, PTC, and Procore, FY2023 to FY2026

Autodesk's revenue rose from 5,005 in 2023 to 7,206 in 2026, and its 2026 growth of 17.5% was the fastest in its series. Its margins held steady and its EBITDA margin improved to 27.6% in 2026. The weaker results are its profitability relative to PTC, its reported FCF conversion, which swings widely, and a sharp fall in its EV/EBITDA multiple in 2026. Across the years available, Autodesk's gross margin is the highest of the four companies, but its EBITDA margin and ROIC trail PTC's recent figures. The data supports a view of a large, high-margin business that is growing faster and converting its growth into higher EBITDA, while the valuation drop in 2026 is not explained by anything in the resources.

## Problems first: where Autodesk lags or weakened

Autodesk's EBITDA margin slipped in 2024, from 23.3% in 2023 to 23.0%, before rising to 25.3% in 2025 and 27.6% in 2026. Over the same period Bentley's EBITDA margin was 27.7%, 24.2%, 28.3%, and 28.8% for 2022 through 2025, and PTC's rose from 28.6% in 2023 to 54.9% in 2026. In 2023 Autodesk's margin was below Bentley's 2022 level and PTC's 2023 level, and it remained below PTC in every year. The 2026 gap to PTC is wide, at 27.3 percentage points.

Return on invested capital also declined before recovering. ROIC fell from 25.8% in 2023 to 21.7% in 2024, then rose to 22.4% in 2025 and 22.7% in 2026. The 2023 level has not been regained. Autodesk's ROIC remains the highest in the group in every year it reports, but the 2024 drop is the largest year-over-year move in its own series.

Reported FCF conversion is volatile. It was 2.46 in 2023, 1.38 in 2024, 1.35 in 2025, and 2.11 in 2026. The table does not define the denominator. Illustrative check: dividing Autodesk's FCF by its EBITDA gives about 0.99 for 2024 (1,252 divided by 1,267) and about 0.97 for 2025 (1,505 divided by 1,549), which does not match the listed 1.38 and 1.35. The conversion figures are therefore probably computed against a different base, most likely net income, and the table does not say so. Readers should use the reported ratios only for direction, not as a ratio of FCF to EBITDA.

Gross margin is high and stable but not rising steadily. It was 90.4% in 2023, 90.7% in 2024, 90.6% in 2025, and 91.0% in 2026. The 2025 dip of 0.1 points is immaterial, but it means the margin has not expanded in every period.

The most significant valuation change is the fall in EV/EBITDA. The multiple was 40.26 in 2023, 43.43 in 2024, and 43.63 in 2025, then dropped to 27.2 in 2026. The fall is about 38% in one year, while EBITDA grew about 28% over the same period (1,989 against 1,549). The resources do not include share prices, enterprise values, or any explanation for the change, so the source of the decline cannot be determined from this data.

## Successes: revenue, cash flow, and margin progress

Revenue grew every year, from 5,005 in 2023 to 5,497 in 2024 (up 9.8%), 6,131 in 2025 (up 11.5%), and 7,206 in 2026 (up 17.5%). The 2026 acceleration is the largest in the series. Its growth rate exceeded Bentley's 10.1% and 11.0% in 2024 and 2025, and PTC's 19.2% in 2025 was higher only in the single year when PTC grew faster. In 2026 PTC's revenue was essentially flat at negative 0.2%, so Autodesk's growth rate was markedly higher than PTC's in that year. The resources do not identify what drove the 2026 acceleration, so no cause is assigned here.

Free cash flow rose from 1,252 in 2024 to 1,505 in 2025 and 2,376 in 2026, an increase of about 58% in the latest year (illustrative: 2,376 divided by 1,505). Over the same period, Bentley's FCF went from 421 in 2024 to 520 in 2025, and PTC's went from 736 in 2024 to 873 in 2026, so Autodesk's cash generation grew faster than either in dollar terms over the comparable window.

EBITDA rose from 1,167 in 2023 to 1,989 in 2026, and its margin moved up in the last two years.

## Year-by-year comparison against competitors

For 2023, Autodesk's gross margin of 90.4% was far above PTC's 79.0%, Bentley's 78.3% (2023), and Procore's 81.6%. Its EBITDA margin of 23.3% was the lowest of the three with available data, below PTC's 28.6% and Bentley's 24.2%, and Procore was deeply negative at -12.1%. Its ROIC of 25.8% was the highest, against PTC at 7.8%, Bentley at 6.9%, and Procore at -12.0%. Autodesk's EV/EBITDA of 40.26 sat close to Bentley's 57.16 and above PTC's 30.78. Procore's multiple was not meaningful. Autodesk's FCF conversion of 2.46 was higher than PTC's 2.39 and Bentley's 1.2 in that year.

For 2024, Autodesk's gross margin of 90.7% was again the highest in the group. Its EBITDA margin of 23.0% remained below PTC's 31.8% and Bentley's 28.3%, and Procore's -1.1% was still negative. Autodesk's ROIC of 21.7% was well above the others, with Bentley at 10.5%, PTC at 9.5%, and Procore at -5.9%. Its EV/EBITDA of 43.43 was above Bentley's 40.44 and PTC's 32.02. Its FCF conversion of 1.38 was below Bentley's 1.79 and PTC's 1.95. Revenue growth of 9.8% trailed Bentley's 10.1% and PTC's 9.6% only marginally, and Procore's 21.2% was higher.

For 2025, which is the only year all four companies report, Autodesk's revenue of 6,131 was the largest in the group, well above Bentley's 1,502, PTC's 2,739, and Procore's 1,323. Its revenue growth of 11.5% was above Bentley's 11.0%, PTC's 19.2% was higher, and Procore's 14.8% was higher as well. Autodesk's gross margin of 90.6% was highest. Its EBITDA margin of 25.3% was below PTC's 41.3%, Bentley's 28.8%, and Procore's 1.3% was far lower. Its ROIC of 22.4% was well above Bentley's 11.9%, PTC's 15.9%, and Procore's -5.5%. Its EV/EBITDA of 43.63 was above Bentley's 40.44 (2024) and 29.4 (2025), PTC's 22.47, and Procore's 598.77, which reflects an EBITDA of only 18 and should be treated as not meaningful. Autodesk's FCF conversion of 1.35 was below Bentley's 1.87 and PTC's 1.17 was lower.

For 2026, only Autodesk and PTC report. Autodesk's revenue growth of 17.5% far exceeded PTC's negative 0.2%. Autodesk's EBITDA margin of 27.6% trailed PTC's 54.9% by a wide margin. Its ROIC of 22.7% exceeded PTC's 21.8% by a small margin. Its EV/EBITDA of 27.2 was well above PTC's 11.2. Its FCF conversion of 2.11 was above PTC's 0.84.

## Metric-specific readings

On profitability, Autodesk is consistently the least profitable of the three reporting companies that have positive EBITDA in the latest year, measured by EBITDA margin, although its gross margin is the highest. The gap to PTC explains most of the difference. PTC's EBITDA margin expanded from 28.6% in 2023 to 54.9% in 2026 while its revenue growth slowed to negative 0.2% in 2026. The table does not split PTC's costs, so the source of that margin change cannot be determined from the resources. Bentley's margin is close to Autodesk's in 2025 (28.8% against 25.3%). Procore's margin, which turned from -30.7% in 2022 to 1.3% in 2025, is improving but remains close to break-even.

On valuation, Autodesk's EV/EBITDA is high relative to PTC in every year and above Bentley in 2024 and 2025. Its 2026 multiple of 27.2 is near Bentley's last reported multiple of 29.4 in 2025, but the years differ. Because the resources contain no price or enterprise value data, these multiples cannot be decomposed. The EV/EBITDA figures alone cannot establish whether Autodesk is over- or undervalued. They show that the market paid roughly 40 to 44 times EBITDA in 2023 through 2025 and roughly 27 times in 2026, which is still above PTC's 11.2 for 2026. The multiples also reflect the fact that Autodesk's EBITDA margin is lower than PTC's, so on a revenue basis the EV/Sales figures tell a different story: Autodesk's EV/Sales was 7.51 in 2026, against PTC's 6.15.

On cash flow efficiency, Autodesk's reported FCF conversion of 2.11 in 2026 is the highest in its own series since 2023 and above PTC's 0.84 in the same year. Bentley's conversion has been above 1.2 in every year reported, rising to 1.87 in 2025. Autodesk's conversion was above 1.3 in every year, but it has been volatile, and the denominator is not defined in the table.

On gross margin, Autodesk's 90.4% to 91.0% range is the highest and most stable in the group. The other three companies have gross margins in the 78% to 84% range. This suggests a high-margin software mix, but the resources do not break down revenue by product or type, so the cause of the gap cannot be verified.

On ROIC, Autodesk is the strongest of the four companies in every year reported, at roughly 22% to 26%. Its 2024 decline is the notable change. PTC is the only other company whose ROIC has risen to near Autodesk's level, reaching 21.8% in 2026. Bentley's ROIC has risen from 6.9% in 2023 to 11.9% in 2025, but remains about half of Autodesk's. Procore's ROIC has been negative throughout but has narrowed from -18.9% in 2022 to -5.5% in 2025.

## Conclusion

Autodesk's financial profile over these years is one of scale, high gross margin, consistent growth, and high ROIC. Revenue grew from 5,005 in 2023 to 7,206 in 2026, the 2026 growth rate of 17.5% was the strongest in the series, free cash flow rose to 2,376 in 2026, and EBITDA margin improved to 27.6% after a dip in 2024. Those results are the strongest parts of the record. The weaker points are a lower EBITDA margin than PTC in every year, a 2024 ROIC decline, volatile reported FCF conversion that cannot be reconciled to EBITDA with the data provided, and a 2026 fall in EV/EBITDA from 43.63 to 27.2 whose cause the resources do not explain.

These results partly justify the market's premium to PTC on EV/EBITDA. Autodesk's ROIC is about double PTC's 2024 level and roughly equal to its 2026 level, and its growth outpaced PTC's in 2026. They do not fully justify the premium on profitability, since PTC earns a much higher EBITDA margin. The current 27.2 multiple is below the 40 to 44 range of 2023 through 2025 and close to Bentley's 2025 multiple, so the valuation has come down toward the peer set even though the resources cannot show whether that reflects a lower share price, a higher EBITDA, or both.

Several results would prove this view wrong. If Autodesk's revenue growth falls back toward the 9.8% to 11.5% range of 2024 and 2025 in the next period, the 2026 acceleration would look like a one-time effect rather than a trend. If the EBITDA margin does not hold above 25%, or ROIC falls below the 21.7% low of 2024, the case for a quality premium weakens. If FCF conversion falls back toward 1.35 or below while revenue continues to grow, the cash generation case would be weaker than the 2026 figure suggests. Finally, if the EV/EBITDA multiple continues to fall without a matching rise in EBITDA, the market would be signaling doubts that the reported metrics do not yet resolve.
