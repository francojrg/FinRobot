# Autodesk, Inc.: Top Three Risks and Risk Assessment

The filing supports a clear picture of three concentrated exposures. The first is revenue concentration in AutoCAD-based products and in a single distributor, with the AutoCAD and AutoCAD LT family at 25% of fiscal 2026 net revenue and 26% in fiscal 2025. The second is international exposure, with 64% of net revenue from outside the United States in both years, combined with a tariff regime that changed several times during fiscal 2026. The third is execution risk in a multi-year transition to subscriptions, named-user plans, new transaction models, and AI, which the company is pursuing alongside two restructuring plans. Legal and governance matters, including the Audit Committee investigation, and the expanding AI and privacy rules are important but are treated here as secondary watch items.

Two data limits shape this analysis. The excerpt contains no peer or sector data, so the comparisons with other industry verticals below are qualitative and rest on what the filing says about the software industry. The excerpt also gives no total net revenue or operating income in dollars, so any dollar sizing is illustrative and uses only the percentages disclosed.

## Risk 1: Concentration in AutoCAD products and in a single distributor

**Industry vertical risk.** The filing states that the software industry has limited barriers to entry, that computing devices at progressively lower prices ease market entry, and that the shift to subscriptions and cloud technology "poses a disruptive challenge to established software companies." Competition is described as intensifying. Against that backdrop, a franchise that carries a quarter of revenue is exposed to share loss in a way a more diversified portfolio is not. The filing does not compare this exposure with other verticals, so the claim that it is higher than average cannot be verified from the excerpt.

**Cyclicality.** The filing names architecture, engineering, and construction, manufacturing, and digital media and entertainment as the industries served and lists "weak or negative growth" in those industries among the drivers of quarterly results. It also notes that customers "may delay or reduce technology purchases" when economic growth slows. The excerpt does not quantify how sensitive AutoCAD seat demand is to these cycles.

**Risk quantification.** The AutoCAD and AutoCAD LT family, excluding collections that include them, was 25% of fiscal 2026 net revenue and 26% of fiscal 2025 net revenue. Illustratively, each $1 billion of net revenue would carry about $250 million from this family. The filing adds that sales of these products have "at times" declined without a matching increase in Industry Collections or cloud revenue, and that the company is "taking steps to accelerate this migration." The distributor concentration is sharper. TD Synnex accounted for 14% of total net revenue in fiscal 2026, down from 33% in fiscal 2025. Indirect channel revenue fell to 37% from 58%. The filing expects direct channel revenue to keep rising as a share of the total. The filing does not explain the reason for the change in TD Synnex's share, and the reader should not assume a cause.

**Downside protections.** Subscriptions run for terms of 1 to 3 years and revenue is recognized over those terms, so "most of the revenue we report in each quarter" comes from contracts signed earlier. This slows the transmission of a sudden drop in demand into reported revenue, which is a real cushion, but the same mechanism means that a decline in renewals reaches revenue with a lag. The company also reports that it assesses distributor creditworthiness before sales and has taken steps in the past to support channel partners. The shift to direct sales reduces dependence on any single intermediary, though the filing states that the indirect channel will still handle "a considerable portion" of customers, particularly in emerging regions.

## Risk 2: International exposure and trade policy volatility

**Industry vertical risk.** The filing describes international revenue as subject to "economic volatility," tariffs, currency fluctuations, longer collection cycles, and "greater difficulty in protecting intellectual property," including "software piracy." It states that the company's dependency on international revenue "makes us much more exposed to global economic and political trends." Software companies with a large non-U.S. share generally face these risks, but the excerpt provides no benchmark, so the company's relative position cannot be measured here.

**Cyclicality.** The filing describes cyclical downturns in the United States and other economies, and says that international results are also affected by seasonality, with Europe's third quarter affected by a slower summer and APAC typically slowing in the fourth quarter. These patterns are seasonal, not cyclical, but they compound any economic downturn in a particular region.

**Risk quantification.** International net revenue was 64% of total net revenue in both fiscal 2026 and fiscal 2025. Illustratively, each $1 billion of net revenue implies about $640 million from outside the United States. The trade-policy disclosures are specific and recent. The filing states that additional IEEPA-based tariffs were rescinded on February 24, 2026, following a Supreme Court decision, but that "the availability, timing, and amount of any related refunds ... remain uncertain." From February 24, 2026, a global 10% "temporary import surcharge" under Section 122 of the Trade Act of 1974 applies to many of the same products, and additional trade investigations are in progress. The filing also cites the continuing sanctions and export restrictions tied to Russia, China, and the Middle East. Currency risk is compounded by the fact that the company says it "does not attempt to completely mitigate this risk" and that hedges carry transaction fees and maturities of one to 18 months.

**Downside protections.** The company uses foreign currency contracts and a cash flow hedge program that extends beyond the current quarter. The geographic spread of 64% of revenue across many countries limits the effect of any one country's downturn, though the filing warns that a U.S.-driven slowdown would affect international results too. Export compliance processes include screening against government lists and inclusion of sanctions clauses in channel agreements. These protections address the operational and currency exposure but not the basic demand exposure to global economic conditions.

## Risk 3: Execution of the subscription transition, restructuring, and AI investment

**Industry vertical risk.** The filing says the industry has "undergone a transition from developing and selling perpetual licenses and on-premises products to subscriptions and cloud-enabled technologies," and that new and existing customers "are reconsidering how they purchase software." It describes AI as capable of altering the market "in unpredictable ways." A company that is changing its pricing model while competing against entrants with lower barriers is carrying more execution risk than a company operating a stable model. The excerpt does not establish how this compares with other verticals.

**Cyclicality.** The filing ties renewal rates to "reductions in customer spending levels, customer activity, or number of users due to economic downturns." It also notes that "the ability of governments around the world to adopt fiscal policies" affects results, and that "a number of our customers rely, directly and indirectly, on government spending." A downturn therefore affects both renewals and new business, and the excerpt gives no quantitative estimate of the sensitivity.

**Risk quantification.** Several specific metrics are disclosed. Billing timing has been affected by "the shift to named-user plans and annual billing of multi-year contracts" in fiscal 2024, 2025, and 2026, and the filing expects this to continue into fiscal 2027. The company initiated the 2026 Plan in the first quarter of fiscal 2026 and the January 2026 Plan in the fourth quarter, and the latter "marks the final phase of our sales and marketing optimization program." The filing warns that expected costs may be higher and savings lower than forecast, and that personnel attrition beyond planned headcount reductions could occur. It also says operating expenses "are relatively fixed in the short term," so a revenue shortfall "has had, and in the future could have, an immediate and significant adverse effect on our profitability." On the capital side, $2.50 billion of principal debt is outstanding, with notes due from June 2027 to June 2035, and the 2025 Credit Agreement provides a $1.5 billion unsecured revolver that can be increased to $2 billion.

**Downside protections.** The restructuring is explicitly tied to reallocating resources toward cloud, platform, and AI investment, and the company has a staggered debt maturity profile that starts in 2027. The revolver offers liquidity, subject to covenants, and the filing notes that the company has had a valuation allowance against some deferred tax assets, which is a conservative accounting position rather than a protection in itself. The filing also states that the company has disaster recovery plans and backup systems, which is relevant to operational continuity during a transition.

## Overall assessment

The three risks are linked. A large share of revenue comes from AutoCAD products, which the company is trying to move customers beyond. The move is being financed through a restructuring whose savings are uncertain, while billing timing is distorted by the shift to annual billing of multi-year contracts. International exposure amplifies all of this because tariffs, sanctions, and currency changes can hit the same accounts. The subscription model's ratable recognition provides real protection against a sudden collapse in reported revenue, but it also means that weak renewals in fiscal 2026 would appear in later periods, which limits how quickly the company or investors can see a problem.

Two items deserve more weight than their placement implies. The first is the Audit Committee investigation into free cash flow and non-GAAP operating margin practices. The SEC closed its matter on August 19, 2025, and the USAO closed its matter on August 21, 2025, but the filing says shareholder litigation remains pending and that "we cannot guarantee" that further inquiries will not follow. Because the company guides investors on exactly these metrics, the credibility of the reporting framework is part of the risk. The second is AI. The filing names AI as both a growth driver and a source of competitive, legal, and reputational exposure, and it cites the European Union's AI Act and state-level U.S. laws as potential cost increases.

The filing does not disclose dollar revenue, operating margins, or peer benchmarks in the excerpt, so the overall risk rating cannot be tied to valuation or to a sector comparison. Based on the text alone, the company is most exposed to execution risk during a transition, with concentration and trade risk as the main amplifiers.

## What would prove this view wrong

The concentration view would weaken if AutoCAD and AutoCAD LT family revenue holds at roughly 25% of total while collections, Industry Collections, and cloud-based revenue grow fast enough to lower the share in subsequent years. The view on execution would weaken if the company reports that savings from the 2026 Plan and January 2026 Plan arrive at or above forecast, that billing timing normalizes earlier than fiscal 2027, and that renewal rates stay stable through a period of named-user migration. The trade view would weaken if the refund process for IEEPA-era duties is resolved and the Section 122 surcharge is not extended or expanded. A further inquiry from the SEC or another regulator tied to the investigation, or a restatement of any non-GAAP metric, would strengthen the view that governance is a more serious risk than the filing's own description suggests.
