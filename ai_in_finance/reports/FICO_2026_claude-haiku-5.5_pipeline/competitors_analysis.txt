# FICO Financial Metrics Against TRU, EFX and VRSK

FICO's metrics are the strongest in this peer group on margins and returns, but its valuation is the most demanding, and the 2026 data contain a large unexplained shift in that valuation. Across fiscal years 2023 to 2026, FICO's gross margin rose from 79.4% to 86.1%, its EBITDA margin rose from 43.9% to 52.9%, and its ROIC rose from 42.9% to 72.5%. Revenue growth accelerated from 13.5% in 2024 to 27.3% in 2026. Its EV/EBITDA multiple was 35.07x in 2023, 64.99x in 2024 and 40.48x in 2025, then fell to 13.23x in 2026. Peer multiples in 2025 ranged from 14.31x (TRU) to 20.18x (VRSK).

A note on the data before the analysis. FICO's fiscal labels run 2023 to 2026, while the peers' run 2022 to 2025, and the fiscal year ends differ. Comparisons below match by fiscal label, so they are approximate. FICO has no peer data for 2026, so the last peer comparison is 2025. The table does not state the units for revenue, EBITDA or FCF, so those figures are shown as given. The table also does not define FCF Conversion. FICO's reported conversion of 1.08 in 2023 does not match FCF divided by EBITDA (465 / 664, or about 0.70, illustrative), so the denominator is probably net income or another base. I have used the reported figures without re-deriving them.

## Revenue and Revenue Growth

FICO's revenue rose from 1,514 in 2023 to 1,718 in 2024, 1,991 in 2025 and 2,535 in 2026. Growth was 13.5% in 2024, 15.9% in 2025 and 27.3% in 2026. The growth rate has accelerated in each year, and the 2026 step-up is the largest change in the series. The table does not state what drove the 2026 increase, so the cause cannot be identified from these resources. Anyone relying on the 27.3% figure should check whether it reflects organic growth or a change in the reporting base, which the 10-K would need to confirm.

FICO is the smallest company in the group in every year where all four are shown. In 2023 its revenue of 1,514 compared with 3,831 for TRU, 5,265 for EFX and 2,681 for VRSK. In 2025 it was 1,991 against 4,576, 6,074 and 3,073. Its growth in 2023 to 2025 was faster than each peer in 2024 and 2025. In 2024, FICO's 13.5% compared with 9.2% for TRU, 7.9% for EFX and 7.5% for VRSK. In 2025 its 15.9% compared with 9.4%, 6.9% and 6.6%. The peers' growth rates were steady or slightly declining, and none accelerated as FICO did.

TRU's growth was the weakest in 2023 at 3.3% and rose to 9.2% and 9.4% in the next two years. EFX slowed from 7.9% in 2024 to 6.9% in 2025. VRSK was the most stable, with growth of 7.4%, 7.5% and 6.6%. FICO's growth profile is therefore the outlier in the group, and it is the main reason the valuation premium is hard to assess from the financial metrics alone.

## Gross Margin

FICO's gross margin rose each year, from 79.4% in 2023 to 79.7% in 2024, 82.2% in 2025 and 86.1% in 2026. The 2025 and 2026 increases were the largest. FICO's gross margin is the highest of the four companies in every overlapping year.

The peers differ sharply. TRU's gross margin fell from 62.7% in 2022 to 60.4% in 2023, 60.0% in 2024 and 59.1% in 2025. That is a steady decline of about 3.6 percentage points over the period. EFX moved from 57.5% in 2022 to 55.7% in 2023 and 2024, then recovered to 56.4% in 2025. VRSK's gross margin rose from 67.0% in 2022 to 67.3%, 68.7% and 69.9% in 2025. In 2025, FICO's 82.2% was about 12 points above VRSK, 23 points above TRU and 26 points above EFX.

The table does not split costs, so it cannot show whether FICO's margin gains came from pricing, mix, or lower direct costs. A margin this high is consistent with a business whose incremental costs are low relative to its revenue, but the resources do not establish that. The clearest test is whether 86.1% holds in later years. If it falls back toward the low 80s, the 2026 gain will look like a one-year effect rather than a trend.

## EBITDA Margin

FICO's EBITDA margin improved from 43.9% in 2023 to 44.3% in 2024, 47.8% in 2025 and 52.9% in 2026. The 2026 result is the first time the margin has passed 50%. EBITDA itself rose from 664 to 1,340 over the period, which is more than double. Part of that growth came from higher revenue, and part came from margin expansion, but the table does not separate the two.

FICO's margin trails VRSK in every overlapping year, and VRSK is the most profitable peer. VRSK's EBITDA margin was 53.1% in 2023, 57.6% in 2024 and 54.3% in 2025. FICO's 2025 margin of 47.8% was about 6.5 points below VRSK, and its 2023 and 2024 margins were about 9 and 13 points below. FICO was ahead of TRU and EFX in each year. In 2025, TRU's margin was 31.8% and EFX's was 30.2%. FICO was therefore a middle-to-upper performer in the group, but it did not lead on EBITDA margin in any year shown.

TRU's margin had a sharp disruption. It fell from 30.5% in 2022 to 17.4% in 2023, then recovered to 28.8% in 2024 and 31.8% in 2025. The table does not give a cause, so the 2023 dip should not be attributed to a specific event without the 10-K. VRSK also declined from 69.7% in 2022 to 53.1% in 2023, and the table does not explain that drop either. EFX's margin was stable, between 30.0% and 30.3% for 2023 to 2025.

## FCF Conversion

FICO's FCF Conversion was 1.08 in 2023, 1.18 in 2024, 1.13 in 2025 and 1.24 in 2026. The figure stayed above 1.0 in every year, which means FCF exceeded the base used in the calculation. The 2026 reading was the highest in the FICO series.

Against peers, the picture is less favorable. In 2023, FICO's 1.08 compared with EFX at 0.95 and VRSK at 1.35, while TRU's figure was not meaningful. In 2024, FICO's 1.18 compared with TRU at 1.82, EFX at 1.35 and VRSK at 0.96. In 2025, FICO's 1.13 was the lowest of the four, behind TRU at 1.45, EFX at 1.72 and VRSK at 1.31. FICO's conversion was stable, but peers improved faster. EFX rose from 0.19 in 2022 to 0.95, 1.35 and 1.72 over 2023 to 2025, and TRU's figure moved from -0.0 in 2022 to a positive 1.82 in 2024 and 1.45 in 2025. VRSK's figure is volatile, moving from 1.35 to 0.96 and then 1.31.

The denominator matters here. FICO's cash generation, measured as FCF over EBITDA, is about 0.78 in 2025 (739 / 951, illustrative). That is well below its reported conversion of 1.13, which confirms the table uses a different base. The comparison among companies is only as reliable as that base is consistent, and the resources do not confirm that it is.

## ROIC

FICO's ROIC rose in each year, from 42.9% in 2023 to 47.9% in 2024, 58.1% in 2025 and 72.5% in 2026. A rise of this size over three years would normally be tied to some combination of higher operating profit and a smaller invested capital base. The table does not show the components, so the cause cannot be determined from these resources.

The peers' ROIC is far lower in most years. TRU's ROIC was 4.3% in 2022, 1.1% in 2023, 5.4% in 2024 and 6.7% in 2025. EFX's was 8.6% in 2022, 7.2% in 2023, 7.9% in 2024 and 8.5% in 2025. VRSK was the closest to FICO, at 21.1% in 2022, 27.2% in 2023 and 33.6% in 2024, but it fell to 20.7% in 2025. In 2025, FICO's 58.1% was nearly three times VRSK's 20.7% and about nine times EFX's 8.5%.

VRSK's 2025 decline, from 33.6% to 20.7%, is the largest single-year drop in the group, and the table does not explain it. It is also the reason VRSK's comparison against FICO narrowed in the latest year. FICO's ROIC kept rising while VRSK's fell, so the gap widened.

## Valuation: EV/EBITDA

FICO's EV/EBITDA was 35.07x in 2023, 64.99x in 2024 and 40.48x in 2025. Peers were 22.79x to 27.41x in 2023, 18.77x to 25.09x in 2024, and 14.31x to 20.18x in 2025. In every overlapping year FICO's multiple was the highest in the group. In 2025 it was about twice VRSK's 20.18x and about 2.8 times TRU's 14.31x.

The 2026 figure of 13.23x is below every peer's 2025 multiple, but the comparison crosses years. The change is large. Using the reported EV/EBITDA and EBITDA figures, implied enterprise value fell from about 38,500 in 2025 (40.48 × 951) to about 17,700 in 2026 (13.23 × 1,340). That is an illustrative reduction of about 54%, while EBITDA rose about 41% over the same period. The table does not explain this. The EV/Sales multiple moves consistently with it (19.34x to 6.99x), so the change is not an arithmetic artifact of one column. Whether it reflects a market price move, a change in the measurement date, or a different enterprise value definition cannot be determined from the resources.

On this metric alone, FICO was over-valued relative to its peers in 2023, 2024 and 2025 and would have been fully valued on its earnings multiple. Its fundamentals exceed peers on gross margin, ROIC and revenue growth, but not on EBITDA margin relative to VRSK. A multiple of about 40x in 2025 implies that the market expected growth and returns to persist well beyond the current period. An illustrative check makes the point: if FICO's 2025 EBITDA were valued at VRSK's 2025 multiple of 20.18x, the implied enterprise value would be about 19,200, roughly half the reported figure. That gap is the premium the market was paying for FICO's growth and returns over that period.

## Conclusion

FICO's financial profile over 2023 to 2026 is strong. Its gross margin, ROIC and revenue growth each improved, its EBITDA margin rose above 50% in 2026, and its FCF conversion stayed above 1.0. Measured against peers, it leads on gross margin and ROIC and grows faster than all three. Its weaknesses are a smaller revenue base than every peer, an EBITDA margin that remained below VRSK in every overlapping year, and FCF conversion that was the lowest of the four in 2025. Its ROIC gains are also not yet matched by a clear explanation in these resources of what drove them.

These metrics support a premium valuation in part. In 2025, FICO's multiple of 40.48x was about double VRSK's, and its ROIC of 58.1% was nearly three times VRSK's, so the premium is at least directionally consistent with the returns gap. The premium is not supported on EBITDA margin, where FICO trails VRSK, and the 2026 multiple of 13.23x is far below its own 2025 level. The resources show that the market's valuation moved sharply, but they do not show why, so the current multiple cannot be fully tied to the fundamentals shown here.

The view that FICO's valuation is justified would be proven wrong if revenue growth fell back to the mid-teens or lower after 2026, if gross margin reversed from 86.1% toward the low 80s, if the EBITDA margin failed to hold above 50%, or if ROIC declined from its 2026 level. Conversely, a clearer account of the 2026 revenue acceleration and the multiple decline in the 10-K would be needed to judge whether the 2026 valuation reflects a lower price for the same business or a different underlying business.
