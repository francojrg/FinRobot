**Company profile**

**Overview.** Founded in 1956, Fair Isaac Corporation (NYSE: FICO) is a global analytics software leader that provides the FICO Score, the standard U.S. measure of consumer credit risk, and decisioning software used in more than 70 countries. Fiscal 2026 revenue was $2.53 billion, up 27%: Scores $1.65 billion (65%) and Software $882.7 million (35%).

**Strengths and products.** The FICO Score, on a 300–850 scale, is used in most U.S. credit decisions. The Scores segment earned a 90% segment operating margin in fiscal 2026. In Software, FICO Platform ARR reached $452.6 million, 53% of total software ARR of $850.9 million as of September 30, 2026. Dollar-based net retention was 112%. The company holds 206 U.S. and 37 foreign patents.

**Markets, customers and share.** The Americas generated 89% of fiscal 2026 revenue and financial services 92%. Customers include more than three-quarters of the 100 largest U.S. financial institutions, nearly all of the 100 largest banks worldwide, more than 600 insurers and more than 200 government agencies. Experian, TransUnion and Equifax provided 60% of revenue, up from 51% in fiscal 2025. The filing gives no overall market share figure.

**Trends, opportunities and challenges.** FICO Score 10T was approved in April 2026 for future use in the conforming mortgage market. Challenges include VantageScore, a joint venture of the three agencies, which has gained regulatory acceptance in U.S. mortgage lending. The Mortgage Direct License Program lets distributors bypass the agencies, which is designed to reduce lender costs. AI rules in the EU, California and Colorado (the latter two effective January 2027) add compliance requirements. Large language model tools are also enabling do-it-yourself alternatives in Software.

**Strategic initiatives and response.** The company launched the Mortgage Direct License Program in October 2025 and extended FICO Score 10T to it in April 2026. It also introduced UltraFICO with Plaid and expanded partnerships with systems integrators and payment processors. In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026, it eliminated about 15% of positions, a $27.3 million charge. Total debt rose to $5.3 billion from $3.1 billion, funding $3.1 billion of share repurchases.

---

**Summary**

Fiscal 2026 grew at the top line but weakened in Software. Revenue rose 27% to $2.53 billion and operating income rose 37% to $1.27 billion, driven by Scores revenue up 41% on mortgage origination pricing and volume. Software revenue grew only 7%, non-platform ARR fell 18% year over year to $398.3 million, and Software segment margin slipped to 28% from 30% on third-party hosting costs. Interest expense rose 67% to $223.3 million as debt reached $5.3 billion. The thesis is wrong if non-platform ARR decline does not slow, or if Scores pricing weakens as mortgage distribution moves away from the consumer reporting agencies.
