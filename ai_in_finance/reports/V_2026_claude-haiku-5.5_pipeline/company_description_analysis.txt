**1. Company overview and industry**

Visa traces its early days to 1958. Visa Inc. is a global payments technology company that provides authorization, clearing and settlement among consumers, financial institutions and sellers through its VisaNet network in more than 200 countries and territories. Visa is not a financial institution and does not issue cards or extend credit. Fiscal 2026 net revenue rose 15% to $45.9 billion, and net income rose 17% to $23.5 billion.

**2. Core strengths and key products**

Visa reports three business lines: consumer payments (credit, debit and prepaid), Commercial and Money Movement Solutions (CMS), and value-added services (VAS). VAS revenue reached $14.2 billion, up 31% from $10.9 billion. In fiscal 2026 Visa processed 281 billion transactions and reports over 5 billion payment credentials and over 400 million seller locations. Over 60% of ecommerce transactions were tokenized as of September 30, 2026.

**3. End markets, customers and market share**

International markets produced $28.5 billion, or 62% of fiscal 2026 net revenue; the U.S. produced $17.4 billion (38%). Visa reports over 14,000 financial institution clients, but the filing does not name major clients, so client concentration cannot be assessed. In calendar 2025, Visa's total volume was $17.1 trillion, compared with $10.6 trillion for Mastercard, $1.9 trillion for American Express, $0.27 trillion for Diners Club/Discover and $0.35 trillion for JCB. Illustratively, Visa holds about 57% of volume across these five networks.

**4. Industry trends, opportunities and challenges**

Ecommerce is growing faster than face-to-face spend, stablecoin-linked card programs exceed 225, and real-time payment networks operate in over 100 countries. Management cites a $520 billion VAS opportunity, based on third-party studies. Challenges include real-time networks competing for consumer and cross-border flows, Brazil's central bank requiring Visa to absorb certain settlement losses, and about $15.9 billion of interchange at issue in unresolved U.S. covered litigation.

**5. Strategic initiatives and response**

Visa completed the $1.5 billion acquisition of Prisma and Newpay in Argentina in February 2026. The $2.4 billion BioCatch acquisition, announced in August 2026, awaits regulatory approval. Product launches include the Visa Stablecoin Platform and Visa Intelligent Commerce. Visa returned $20.5 billion through share repurchases and raised its quarterly dividend 16% to $0.78. Operating expenses rose 11% to $17.8 billion, including $563 million of severance tied to efficiency actions and reinvestment.

---

**Summary**

Visa's fiscal 2026 net revenue rose 15% to $45.9 billion, and non-GAAP diluted EPS rose 16% to $13.27. Growth came from cross-border volume (up 15% excluding Europe), processed transactions (up 9%) and VAS revenue (up 31%). Costs grew faster than the revenue growth in one respect: non-GAAP operating expenses rose 16%. Litigation, Brazilian settlement rules and real-time payment competition are the main risks. The thesis is challenged if VAS and cross-border growth slow while litigation and regulatory costs keep rising.
