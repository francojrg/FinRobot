## Visa Inc. (V): Financial Metrics Compared with Mastercard Incorporated (MA), American Express Company (AXP) and PayPal Holdings, Inc. (PYPL)

Visa grew revenue steadily from 2023 to 2025, reaching 40,000 in 2025, and its gross margin held at 80.4% from 2024 onward. Its EBITDA margin, however, fell from 71.2% in 2024 to 65.0% in 2025, and its FCF conversion fell below 1.0 in 2026. Visa's profitability remains well above PayPal's in every year, and it trails Mastercard on ROIC in every year. The resources do not explain any of these movements, so the causes discussed below are inferred from the figures and are labelled as such.

Two data limits apply throughout. The resources do not say whether 2025 and 2026 figures are reported results or estimates, so I treat all listed years alike. The FCF Conversion definition is not provided, and Visa's figures do not reconcile to FCF divided by EBITDA (for example, 2024 gives 0.73 on that basis against 0.95 listed), so I compare the listed ratios as given. American Express has no gross profit, gross margin, EBITDA, ROIC or EV/EBITDA values (EV/EBITDA is shown as "n/m"), so it is compared only on revenue, revenue growth and FCF conversion. Fiscal year ends differ across the four companies, so the comparisons below pair the year labels as they appear in the data.

### Year-over-Year Trends for Visa

Revenue rose from 32,653 in 2023 to 35,926 in 2024 (growth of 10.0%), then to 40,000 in 2025 (11.3%) and 45,903 in 2026 (14.8%). Growth accelerated in each year shown, which is the clearest positive in the data. The 2026 acceleration is the largest single step in the series, and the figure should be checked against the company's reported results when available, because the resource does not say whether it is an estimate.

Gross margin rose from 79.9% in 2023 to 80.4% in 2024 and then stayed at 80.4% in 2025 and 2026. Gross profit grew in every year, from 26,086 to 36,903, so the margin gain was stable rather than volatile.

EBITDA margin moved the other way after 2024. It rose from 69.3% in 2023 to 71.2% in 2024, then dropped to 65.0% in 2025 and recovered only slightly to 65.9% in 2026. The 2025 decline is the most significant deterioration in Visa's series. Gross margin was unchanged that year, so the squeeze came below gross profit. Illustratively, gross profit less EBITDA rose from 3,293 in 2024 (9.2% of revenue) to 6,142 in 2025 (15.4% of revenue). The resource does not identify what drove the increase in those costs.

FCF Conversion was 1.14 in 2023, 0.95 in 2024, 1.08 in 2025 and 0.94 in 2026. It swings around 1.0 and ended the series below it. Free cash flow itself was 19,696 in 2023, 18,693 in 2024, 21,577 in 2025 and 22,057 in 2026. Free cash flow in 2026 grew only about 2.2% (illustrative) while revenue grew 14.8%, which is why conversion fell.

ROIC was 31.0% in 2023, 34.4% in 2024 and 33.0% in 2025, then jumped to 41.9% in 2026. The 2025 dip is small, and the 2026 figure is the highest Visa value in the data.

### Competitor Comparison by Year

Revenue: Visa ranked second of the four companies in each year from 2023 to 2025, behind American Express (60,515, 65,949 and 72,230). Visa was ahead of Mastercard (25,098, 28,167 and 32,791) and PayPal (29,771, 31,797 and 33,172) in each year, though PayPal's 2025 revenue (33,172) was only slightly below Visa's 2023 level of 32,653 plus growth, so the gap to PayPal narrowed from 2,882 in 2023 to 6,828 in 2025. Visa's gap to PayPal in fact widened over the period (2,882 in 2023, 4,129 in 2024, 6,828 in 2025), since Visa grew faster.

Revenue growth: Visa's 10.0% in 2024 trailed Mastercard (12.2%) and was ahead of American Express (9.0%) and PayPal (6.8%). In 2025 Visa's 11.3% trailed Mastercard's 16.4% and was ahead of American Express (9.5%) and PayPal (4.3%). Visa's 2023 growth is not available.

Gross margin: Visa was above Mastercard in every year (79.9% against 76.0% in 2023, 80.4% against 76.3% in 2024, and 80.4% against 77.9% in 2025). PayPal's gross margin was around 46% in 2023 to 2025, far below Visa's. American Express has no gross margin in the data.

EBITDA margin: Visa led Mastercard in 2023 (69.3% against 59.8%), 2024 (71.2% against 59.6%) and 2025 (65.0% against 62.3%). The 2025 lead narrowed sharply, from about 11.6 points in 2024 to 2.7 points in 2025. PayPal was at 22.9%, 21.2% and 23.2%, so Visa's margin lead over PayPal was large in every year. American Express has no EBITDA margin in the data.

ROIC: Visa trailed Mastercard in each year (31.0% against 51.6% in 2023, 34.4% against 54.3% in 2024, 33.0% against 58.2% in 2025). It was well ahead of PayPal (14.7%, 14.7% and 18.5%). There are no American Express ROIC values.

FCF Conversion: Visa was 1.14 in 2023, ahead of Mastercard (0.97), PayPal (0.99) and American Express (2.03 was higher, so Visa trailed American Express). In 2024, Visa's 0.95 trailed Mastercard (1.06), PayPal (1.63) and American Express (1.20). In 2025, Visa's 1.08 was close to Mastercard (1.10) and PayPal (1.06) and behind American Express (1.48). Visa's conversion was the lowest or near-lowest of the group in 2024 and 2026 (2026 has no peer values).

### Metric-Specific Insights

EBITDA Margin: Visa is the most profitable of the four on this measure in 2023 through 2025 when Mastercard, PayPal and American Express are considered, with the exception that American Express has no EBITDA margin in the data. The most recent year is where the lead narrowed most sharply. Visa's 65.0% in 2025 was only 2.7 points above Mastercard's 62.3%, and Visa's 2026 figure of 65.9% is not compared with any peer value in the data.

EV/EBITDA: Visa traded at 20.76 times EBITDA in 2023, 21.54 in 2024 and 25.58 in 2025, then 22.5 in 2026. Mastercard was higher in 2023 (27.01) and 2024 (29.22) and roughly equal in 2025 (25.38), so Visa was cheaper than Mastercard in the first two years and at about the same multiple in 2025. PayPal was much lower in every year (9.73, 13.05 and 7.23), and American Express has no meaningful EV/EBITDA (n/m). Relative to PayPal, Visa carries a premium throughout. Whether Visa is over- or undervalued cannot be judged from these ratios alone. Illustratively, the multiple combined with EBITDA implies an enterprise value that rose about 21% from 2024 to 2025 and only about 2% from 2025 to 2026, while EBITDA rose about 16% in 2026. The 2026 multiple compression therefore reflects EBITDA growing faster than the implied value, which depends on the multiple being measured on a consistent enterprise value basis that the resource does not confirm.

FCF Conversion: Visa's conversion is not the strongest in the group. American Express runs at 1.2 to 2.56 across the years, and PayPal reached 1.63 in 2024. Mastercard has been steadier, at 0.97 to 1.10, and it was above Visa in 2024 and 2025. Visa's ratio is not weak in absolute terms, but it has shown no upward trend.

Gross Margin: Visa's gross margin rose from 79.9% to 80.4% and then held, which indicates that its cost of revenue has been stable relative to revenue. Visa has the highest gross margin of the companies with data. PayPal's margin fell from 50.1% in 2022 to 46.0% in 2023 and has since risen only slightly, to 46.6% in 2025, and the resource does not explain that 2023 drop.

ROIC: Visa's ROIC rose from 31.0% to 34.4% and then eased to 33.0% in 2025 before the 2026 value of 41.9%. This suggests Visa earns a high return on the capital it deploys, though Mastercard earns a higher one in each shared year and has risen faster (50.8% to 58.2%). The 2026 jump is the most important recent change, and it is also the value most in need of confirmation because the resource does not say whether it is an estimate. If ROIC remains near 33% to 34% rather than 41.9%, the improvement claimed by the 2026 figure would not have occurred.

Revenue and Revenue Growth: Visa's revenue grew from 32,653 in 2023 to 45,903 in 2026, which is about 41% over three years. Growth went from 10.0% in 2024 to 11.3% in 2025 and 14.8% in 2026. Visa is growing at the second-fastest pace among the four companies in 2025 after Mastercard and well ahead of American Express and PayPal. Growth at PayPal decelerated each year (8.2%, 6.8%, 4.3%), and American Express slowed from 14.5% to 9.0% before recovering slightly to 9.5%. Visa's acceleration is the reverse of the trend at most peers.

### Conclusion

Visa's financial health, as shown by these metrics, is strong. It has the highest gross margin among the companies with data, EBITDA margins far above PayPal's, a ROIC well above PayPal's, and revenue growth that accelerated over the period. Its weaknesses are concentrated in 2025 and 2026: EBITDA margin fell 6.2 points in 2025, FCF conversion fell below 1.0 in 2026, and free cash flow growth lagged revenue growth. Visa also trails Mastercard on ROIC and EBITDA margin in 2025 and has no clear advantage on FCF conversion.

Whether these metrics justify the current valuation depends on the 2026 figures. An EV/EBITDA of 22.5 is well above PayPal's 7.23 and somewhat below Mastercard's 25.38 in 2025, so the market is pricing Visa at a premium to PayPal and close to Mastercard. The combination of accelerating revenue, a stable 80% gross margin and a high ROIC supports a premium multiple. The weaker 2025 EBITDA margin and the sub-1.0 FCF conversion in 2026 are the points that do not support it on their own.

The view that Visa's valuation is justified would be proven wrong if reported 2026 revenue growth falls back toward the 2024 level of 10.0%, if the EBITDA margin does not recover above 65.9%, or if FCF conversion stays below 1.0 for a further year. The 41.9% ROIC for 2026 would also need to be confirmed as reported, since a fall back toward the 33% to 34% range seen in 2024 and 2025 would weaken the case that Visa's return on capital is improving.
