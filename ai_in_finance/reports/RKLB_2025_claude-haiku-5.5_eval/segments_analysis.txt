**Business Segments Identified:** Space Systems and Launch Services. The MD&A reports revenue and cost of revenues for these two lines. Segment-level operating profit is not disclosed, so gross profit is derived as segment revenue minus segment cost of revenues.

### Space Systems

Revenue grew $91.9 million (30%) to $402.8 million, driven by spacecraft manufacturing, partly offset by a net $7.9 million downward catch-up adjustment. Cost of revenues rose 21% to $276.8 million, lifting implied gross profit from $81.5 million to $126.0 million. Bookings including the Space Development Agency Tranche 3 deal (up to $816 million) and the GEOST acquisition lifted the $1,371.7 million space systems backlog.

### Launch Services

Revenue grew $73.7 million (59%) to $199.0 million, driven by 21 missions versus 16 and higher revenue per launch ($8.5 million vs. $7.8 million). Cost of revenues rose 30% to $117.8 million, but cost per launch fell to $4.8 million from $5.7 million, lifting implied gross profit from $34.6 million to $81.2 million. Neutron's first launch is now targeted for Q4 2026.

---

**Note on gaps in the source material:** The MD&A does not report segment net profit. Consolidated net loss widened to $198.2 million from $190.2 million, while R&D rose 55% to $270.7 million, mainly for Neutron. The MD&A gives no quantified market share and does not describe specific national policy changes. It states only that Electron was the second most frequently launched orbital rocket in 2025 and that government expenditures are an important growth factor.
