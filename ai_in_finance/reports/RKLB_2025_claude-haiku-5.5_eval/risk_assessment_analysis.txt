# Top Three Key Risks for Rocket Lab Corporation

Based on the risk factors in the 10-K, the three most significant risks are (1) heavy dependence on U.S. government funding and a small number of large customers, (2) execution and launch-reliability risk tied to Neutron and Electron, and (3) continued losses combined with debt and capital-access constraints. These risks interact. A delay in Neutron, for example, could slow revenue from government and commercial customers while the company continues to carry losses and debt service obligations.

## Risk One: Concentration in U.S. Government Funding and Major Customers

**Industry Vertical Risk.** The filing describes the launch, mission services, spacecraft, and component markets as highly regulated and shaped by government policy. It notes licensing requirements from the Department of Transportation, the FAA, the FCC, the Department of State, and the Department of Commerce, along with FAR compliance, facility security clearances, and export controls. The filing does not provide data comparing these risks with other industry verticals, so any comparison must be qualitative. On the basis of the disclosures alone, this vertical carries a regulatory burden and a competitive environment (the filing notes that many competitors are larger and better resourced, and that some foreign competitors receive government support) that add risk beyond ordinary commercial exposure.

**Cyclicality.** The filing states that the rocket launch, mission services, spacecraft, and component markets are cyclical and that this cyclicality can make customer demand difficult to forecast. It also notes that order rates fluctuate annually as the number of contracts awarded varies. The filing does not quantify a cycle length or its historical amplitude. The more immediate economic exposure, however, comes from policy and funding decisions rather than from a generic business cycle. The filing cites the October 1, 2025 government shutdown as having delayed contract awards and payments, and it describes reduced workforce at critical sites such as Wallops.

**Risk Quantification.** The figures are specific. Revenue from U.S. government, U.S. prime contractors, and their subcontracts rose to approximately 47% of total 2025 revenue, up from 33% in 2024 and 31% in 2023. The top five customers accounted for about 49% of 2025 revenue, and the top five backlog customers accounted for about 77% of backlog at year-end 2025. Government contracts are fixed-price, so the company bears the risk of cost increases. The filing also notes that governments can terminate contracts at any time, that remedies may fall short of the benefit of full performance, and that the company may not replace lost backlog. Some customers have defaulted in the past.

**Downside Protections.** The protections here are limited and partly structural. Government contracts are subject to a regulatory framework that requires compliance and can offer predictability once awarded, and the filing notes that the U.S. government may pay third-party launch liability claims above insurance limits, subject to appropriation and a statutory cap. Revenue is diversified across more than five customers in one sense, but the top-five concentration figures show that this is not a substantial offset. The filing does not describe contract durations or renewal terms in enough detail to assess how long backlog is protected, so the protection afforded by long-term contracts cannot be verified from these resources.

## Risk Two: Execution and Launch Reliability

**Industry Vertical Risk.** Development and launch activities carry technical and operational risks that differ from those in more mature sectors. The filing identifies the Neutron launch vehicle as a new medium-lift rocket intended for constellation deployment, interplanetary missions, and potentially human spaceflight. It also states that the company's launch services business currently depends on Electron. This concentration in a small number of launch platforms heightens the consequences of any single technical failure.

**Cyclicality.** Execution risk is less cyclical than demand risk. It is driven by engineering outcomes and schedules rather than the economic cycle. However, the filing links the two: when launches or spacecraft are delayed, anticipated revenue is delayed, and during any period in which a vehicle type is not operational, the company may lose most or all of the revenue it would otherwise derive from it.

**Risk Quantification.** The filing reports 75 successful missions and three failed customer launches as of December 31, 2025, occurring in July 2020, May 2021, and September 2023. The causes differed: a battery-related power-supply issue, a second-stage engine computer malfunction, and a high-voltage power-supply failure. Each failed mission resulted in the loss of all payloads and halted launches until the FAA authorized resumption. On January 21, 2026, a qualification test of the Neutron Stage 1 tank ruptured during a hydrostatic pressure trial, which affected Neutron's launch schedule. The filing also notes that fixed-price development contracts carry more cost estimate variability than non-development work, and that delays can trigger penalties or liquidated damages.

**Downside Protections.** The record of 75 successful missions provides some evidence of operational capability. The filing also describes a qualification and performance surveillance process for suppliers and states that the company maintains insurance, though insurance does not cover the company's own losses to its launch vehicle, launch complex, or spacecraft. The filing explicitly acknowledges that the company does not maintain back-up manufacturing facilities and that its disaster recovery plans are limited, which weakens this category of protection.

## Risk Three: Continued Losses and Capital Access

**Industry Vertical Risk.** Capital-intensive development in space technology is a structural feature of this vertical, and the filing emphasizes that the company expects operating expenses and capital expenditures to increase significantly. The filing does not compare these capital needs with other industries, but the scale of required investment relative to current revenue is clearly a defining characteristic of the company's risk profile.

**Cyclicality.** Capital access is affected by market and credit conditions. The filing notes that instability in global credit markets and changing central bank policy could affect financing, and that the company may need additional capital on terms that may be unfavorable or unavailable.

**Risk Quantification.** Net losses were $198.2 million in 2025, $190.2 million in 2024, and $182.6 million in 2023. Relative to revenue of $601.8 million, $436.2 million, and $244.6 million, respectively, the net loss equaled approximately 33%, 44%, and 75% of revenue. The margin of loss relative to revenue has therefore narrowed considerably, even though absolute losses have remained near $200 million. The company expects losses to continue for at least the next 12 months. Aggregate indebtedness was $157.4 million at year-end 2025, including variable-rate equipment financing. The convertible senior notes may require cash repurchase upon a fundamental change and partial cash settlement on conversion. The Direct Funding Agreement with the Department of Commerce restricts share buybacks and dividends without prior approval. The filing also notes dilution risk from future equity issuance, convertible settlement in shares, and acquisitions.

**Downside Protections.** The filing states that management currently anticipates that existing cash and cash equivalents and operating cash flow will be sufficient to meet cash needs for the foreseeable future. The capped call transactions are intended to reduce or offset dilution upon conversion of the notes, subject to a cap and to counterparty credit risk. The company funds operations through a mix of equity, debt, and internal cash generation, which provides some flexibility, though the filing notes this flexibility is not assured.

## Overall Assessment

The company's risk profile is concentrated and interdependent rather than diversified. The most important observation is that the three key risks reinforce one another. Government customers now represent close to half of revenue, and those contracts are fixed-price, meaning that any cost overrun or technical delay in development programs such as Neutron falls directly on the company's margins. At the same time, the filing acknowledges that funding decisions and shutdowns can delay awards and payments, which could strain cash flow while the company continues to incur losses and carry debt.

The improvement in the loss-to-revenue ratio over the three-year period is a meaningful positive signal, as is the evidence of a substantial operating history through 75 successful missions. However, absolute losses have not declined materially, and the company's expansion of headcount from approximately 1,400 employees in December 2022 to over 2,600 by December 31, 2025 illustrates the scale of ongoing investment. The filing also raises the risk that growth itself strains management and controls.

The resources do not provide comparative industry data, market-based valuation metrics, or details on contract duration, so several conclusions must remain qualitative. A reader should weigh the disclosed concentration figures and the Neutron schedule impact most heavily, as these are the disclosures most likely to affect near-term results. The overall picture is one of a company with demonstrated operational progress but significant exposure to government policy, customer concentration, development execution, and continued funding requirements.
