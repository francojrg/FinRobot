**Productivity and Business Processes** (segment revenue $139,996 million, up 16%; operating income $83,879 million, up 20%)

- **Microsoft 365 Commercial:** Cloud revenue grew 17%, and products and cloud services revenue rose $14.2 billion (16%) to the segment's largest contributor. Growth came from revenue per user, driven by Microsoft 365 Copilot and Microsoft 365 E5, and from seats, which grew 6% on small and medium business and frontline worker offerings.
- **Microsoft 365 Consumer:** Cloud revenue grew 28%, and products and cloud services revenue rose $1.8 billion (24%). Revenue per user growth and subscriber growth of 7% drove the increase, although the consumer subscriber count was removed as a reported metric in fiscal 2026.
- **LinkedIn:** Revenue increased $2.0 billion (11%) to the segment's total, with growth across all lines of business. The 10-K reports growth across all four lines but does not attribute it to a single driver.
- **Dynamics:** Products and cloud services revenue rose $1.2 billion (15%), led by Dynamics 365 revenue growth of 18% with growth across all workloads.

**Intelligent Cloud** (segment revenue $137,791 million, up 30%; operating income $56,972 million, up 28%)

- **Azure and other cloud services:** Revenue grew 41%, the fastest growth of any line discussed in the filing, with growth across all workloads. The cost of this growth was visible in the segment's cost of revenue, which rose 44% to $57,876 million on investments in AI infrastructure, and in a lower segment gross margin percentage, which the company attributed to those investments and to the sales mix shift toward Azure, partly offset by efficiency gains in Azure.
- **Server products (on-premises and cloud):** Server products and cloud services revenue rose $31.0 billion (31%), driven by Azure, while on-premises Server products revenue grew only 1% because higher purchases of licenses in multi-cloud environments were offset by the continued customer shift to cloud.
- **Enterprise and partner services:** Revenue increased $500 million (6%), driven by growth in Enterprise Support Services.

**More Personal Computing** (segment revenue $54,052 million, down 1%; operating income $14,386 million, up 2%)

- **XBOX:** Revenue fell $1.7 billion (7%), with content and services down 5% and hardware down 29%. Content and services declined against a prior-year period that benefited from strong first-party content performance, which growth in XBOX Game Pass only partly offset, and hardware fell because of lower console volume.
- **Windows OEM and Devices:** Combined revenue decreased slightly, with Windows and Devices revenue down $230 million (1%). Windows OEM revenue grew 5% while inventory levels in the OEM channel remained elevated, but the decline in Devices more than offset that growth.
- **Search advertising:** Revenue rose $1.3 billion (9%), and revenue excluding traffic acquisition costs grew 12%. The filing attributes the increase to higher search volume, higher revenue per search, and benefit from third-party partnerships.
