# Microsoft Corporation (MSFT) Against Alphabet, Amazon and Oracle

Microsoft's margins and returns are the strongest in this four-company set for most of the years shown, and its 2026 EBITDA margin of 62.5% is the highest in the table. The weaker results are in cash generation. Free cash flow fell in 2025 and again in 2026, and FCF conversion dropped from 0.84 in 2024 to 0.50 in 2026. The 2026 valuation multiples are also far below the earlier years, with EV/EBITDA at 13.53 against 23.98 in 2025. The table does not show whether that drop came from a lower enterprise value, a higher EBITDA base, or both.

Several limits apply to the comparison. The peer series do not line up with Microsoft's. Alphabet and Amazon.com are shown for 2022 through 2025, Oracle for 2023 through 2026, and Microsoft for 2023 through 2026. Each company's fiscal year ends on a different date, so the same label does not mean the same calendar period. The resource does not state units, so revenue and cash flow figures are used as presented. FCF conversion does not reconcile to the EBITDA and FCF rows in the table (for example, Microsoft's 2023 FCF of 59,475 divided by EBITDA of 105,140 is 0.57, not the 0.82 shown), so the metric's denominator is not known. The valuation date is also not given. The comparisons below use the table's values as reported.

## Revenue and Revenue Growth

The main weakness is that Microsoft's growth did not rise steadily. Revenue grew 15.7% in 2024 and 14.9% in 2025, the lowest of the three reported rates, before rising to 17.8% in 2026. Revenue went from 211,915 in 2023 to 245,122 in 2024, 281,724 in 2025 and 331,839 in 2026. The 2025 slowdown is small, but it left Microsoft slightly behind Alphabet, whose growth was 15.1% that year.

Against peers on scale, Microsoft ranked second of the four companies in each year shared with the others. In 2023 its revenue of 211,915 was well below Alphabet's 307,394 and Amazon.com's 574,785. In 2024 it was 245,122 against Alphabet's 350,018, Amazon.com's 637,959 and Oracle's 52,961. In 2025 it was 281,724 against Alphabet's 402,836, Amazon.com's 716,924 and Oracle's 57,399.

On growth, the 2023 comparison is incomplete because Microsoft's 2023 growth is shown as n/a. Alphabet grew 8.7% and Amazon.com 11.8% that year. In 2024, Microsoft's 15.7% led Alphabet's 13.9%, Amazon.com's 11.0% and Oracle's 6.0%. In 2025, Microsoft's 14.9% sat just below Alphabet's 15.1%, ahead of Amazon.com's 12.4% and Oracle's 8.4%. In 2026, Microsoft's 17.8% was above Oracle's 17.3%, which had accelerated sharply from 8.4% the year before. The table does not explain Oracle's acceleration, so its cause cannot be attributed here.

## EBITDA Margin

Microsoft's EBITDA margin rose in every year, from 49.6% in 2023 to 53.7% in 2024, 55.2% in 2025 and 62.5% in 2026. The problem sits inside that trend. Over the same period Microsoft's gross margin fell from 69.8% in 2024 to 67.9% in 2026. The 2026 EBITDA margin rose 7.3 points while the gross margin fell 0.9 points, so the gain came from costs below gross profit. The table does not break those costs down, so the driver cannot be identified from these resources. Illustratively, EBITDA rose about 33.5% in 2026 (207,519 against 155,445) while revenue rose 17.8%, which is the scale of the gap that needs explaining.

Against peers, Microsoft had the highest EBITDA margin in each year shared with the others. In 2023 it was 49.6% against Oracle's 37.5%, Alphabet's 31.9% and Amazon.com's 15.6%. In 2024 it was 53.7% against Oracle's 40.4%, Alphabet's 38.7% and Amazon.com's 19.4%. In 2025 it was 55.2% against Oracle's 41.7%, Alphabet's 44.9% and Amazon.com's 23.1%. In 2026 it was 62.5% against Oracle's 49.7%. The gap to Oracle held at roughly 12 to 13 points. Amazon.com improved the most over its series, from 7.5% in 2022 to 23.1% in 2025, but remained far below Microsoft.

## EV/EBITDA

Microsoft's EV/EBITDA was the highest of the four companies in each year from 2023 to 2025. The values were 24.31 in 2023, 25.6 in 2024 and 23.98 in 2025. Peers were lower in 2023 and 2024: Alphabet at 17.8 and 17.07, Amazon.com at 18.34 and 19.19, and Oracle at 19.65 and 18.96. In 2025 the gap narrowed. Oracle was at 23.33, only 0.65 turns below Microsoft, while Alphabet was at 21.1 and Amazon.com at 15.38. The premium over Alphabet was 2.9 turns.

The 2026 figure is the largest change in the table. EV/EBITDA fell to 13.53, a 44% decline from 23.98, and sits well below Oracle's 23.18 for the same year. Illustratively, if enterprise value had stayed at its 2025 level, the 2026 multiple would be about 17.96 (23.98 divided by the 1.335 EBITDA growth factor). The reported 13.53 therefore implies enterprise value about 25% lower than in 2025. That is a derived figure that depends on the rounding of the multiples. The fall in EV/Sales (13.23 to 8.46), P/E (36.47 to 20.78) and P/B (10.77 to 6.26) over the same year points the same way. This is consistent with a lower market value for the equity, though the table does not confirm the price movement or date.

The earlier premium is not fully explained by growth. Microsoft's 2025 growth of 14.9% was essentially level with Alphabet's 15.1%, which carried a multiple 2.9 turns lower. The premium is better explained by margin and return differences, since Microsoft's EBITDA margin and ROIC were higher than Alphabet's in 2025 on the EBITDA margin measure, and its ROIC was only slightly below Alphabet's that year (26.8% against 28.7%).

## FCF Conversion

The cash flow trend is Microsoft's most significant problem. Free cash flow was 59,475 in 2023, rose to 74,071 in 2024, then fell to 71,611 in 2025 and 66,987 in 2026. FCF conversion fell from 0.84 in 2024 to 0.70 in 2025 and 0.50 in 2026. Illustratively, dividing FCF by the stated conversion ratio implies a denominator of about 102,000 in 2025 and about 134,000 in 2026. The conversion drop therefore reflects a base rising about 31% while FCF fell about 6.5%. The resource does not show what drove the base higher or why cash flow declined, so the cause cannot be named.

Against peers, the ranking depends on whether conversion or absolute cash flow is used. In 2023 Microsoft's 0.82 was the lowest of the four, below Alphabet's 0.94, Oracle's 1.0 and Amazon.com's 1.06. In 2024 its 0.84 was second to Oracle's 1.13 and ahead of Alphabet's 0.73 and Amazon.com's 0.55. In 2025 its 0.70 was the highest of the four, ahead of Alphabet's 0.55, Amazon.com's 0.10 and Oracle's negative -0.03. On absolute FCF in 2025, Microsoft's 71,611 was second only to Alphabet's 73,266, far ahead of Amazon.com's 7,695 and Oracle's negative 394. Conversion is falling across the group. Alphabet's conversion fell from 1.0 in 2022 to 0.55 in 2025 even as its FCF rose from 60,010 to 73,266, so the decline is not unique to Microsoft. Oracle's FCF turned negative in 2025 and 2026, giving a 2026 conversion of -1.39.

What would prove this concern wrong is FCF growing again in the next fiscal year while EBITDA keeps rising, which would show the 2026 decline to be a timing effect in the base rather than a sustained drop in cash.

## Gross Margin

Microsoft's gross margin stayed above 67% in all four years but has declined since its peak. It was 68.9% in 2023, 69.8% in 2024, 68.8% in 2025 and 67.9% in 2026, a drop of 1.9 points from the 2024 peak. The table does not give the cost components, so the reason for the decline cannot be stated.

Against peers, Microsoft's margin was the highest of the four in 2023 and 2024 and the second highest in 2025 and 2026, behind Oracle in 2025 at 70.5% and then ahead of Oracle in 2026 at 67.9% against 65.8%. Oracle's margin fell from 72.8% in 2023 to 65.8% in 2026, a 7.0-point decline. Alphabet and Amazon.com improved in every year shown, with Alphabet rising from 55.4% to 59.7% and Amazon.com from 43.8% to 50.3%. The gap between Microsoft and Alphabet narrowed from 12.3 points in 2023 (68.9% against 56.6%) to 11.6 points in 2024 and 9.1 points in 2025 (68.8% against 59.7%). Microsoft's gross margin is high, but it is no longer widening its lead over the fastest-improving peer.

## ROIC

Microsoft's ROIC was 29.2% in 2023, 28.3% in 2024, 26.8% in 2025 and 28.2% in 2026. The range is only 2.4 points, so returns have been stable but have not improved since 2023. The 2025 low is the point to watch. The ROIC definition is not stated in the resource.

Against peers, Alphabet was ahead of Microsoft in two of the three shared years after 2023. In 2023 Microsoft's 29.2% was above Alphabet's 25.1%. In 2024 Alphabet's 29.9% exceeded Microsoft's 28.3%, and in 2025 Alphabet's 28.7% exceeded Microsoft's 26.8%. Amazon.com's ROIC rose from negative 1.2% in 2022 to 18.1% in 2024 and then eased to 16.8% in 2025. Oracle held between 12.6% and 14.2% across its series, and Microsoft's 2026 ROIC of 28.2% was 15.6 points higher. Microsoft's returns stayed in the upper range of this group, but Alphabet has been the stronger performer on this metric since 2024.

If Microsoft's ROIC falls below the 2025 level of 26.8% in the next year, that would indicate that returns are declining as the capital base grows, which would contradict the stability shown so far.

## Conclusion

Microsoft's financial profile across these metrics combines high profitability with a weaker cash trend. Its EBITDA margin rose each year to 62.5% in 2026, its ROIC has stayed between 26.8% and 29.2%, and its revenue grew between 14.9% and 17.8% in each reported year. Against this, free cash flow fell in 2025 and 2026, FCF conversion dropped to 0.50, gross margin declined from its 2024 peak, and Alphabet has matched or exceeded Microsoft's ROIC since 2024.

On valuation, Microsoft carried the highest EV/EBITDA in the group from 2023 through 2025, at a premium that the margin and return gap supports better than the growth rate, which was similar to Alphabet's. The 2026 multiple of 13.53 is well below both its own history and Oracle's 23.18. Taken at face value, the 2026 EBITDA multiple suggests a lower valuation, but the table does not show whether the stock is undervalued. The EBITDA rise of about 33.5% lowers the multiple mechanically, and the falling FCF suggests that the rise in EBITDA is not yet turning into cash. An argument for undervaluation based on this multiple would be contradicted if free cash flow continues to decline while EBITDA rises, since the EBITDA multiple would then overstate the cash earnings behind the stock. It would also be contradicted by further gross margin erosion or an ROIC below 26.8%. Confirming the view requires the valuation date, the cost detail behind the 2026 margin, and the cash flow drivers, none of which are in the resources.
