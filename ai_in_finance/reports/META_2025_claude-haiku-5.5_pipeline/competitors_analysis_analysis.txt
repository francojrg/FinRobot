# Meta Platforms (META) Against Alphabet, Snap and Pinterest, Fiscal 2022 to 2025

Meta grew revenue fastest of the four scaled companies in the most recent year, kept the highest gross margin among the large platforms, and earned the highest EBITDA margin in 2025. Its returns and cash conversion softened in 2025, however, and its EV/EBITDA multiple of 16.25 in 2025 sits below Alphabet's 21.1. The figures below are as reported in the resource. The resource does not label units, so revenue and profit are read as millions of US dollars. The resource also does not state each company's fiscal year end, so the comparisons assume the year labels line up.

## Revenue and Revenue Growth

Meta's revenue rose from 116,609 in 2022 to 134,902 in 2023, 164,501 in 2024 and 200,966 in 2025. Growth was 15.7% in 2023, 21.9% in 2024 and 22.2% in 2025. The growth rate has risen in each of the last two years, and 2025 is the strongest year in the series. Over three years the revenue base grew about 72% (illustrative: 200,966 divided by 116,609).

Against the peers, Meta's 2023 growth of 15.7% was ahead of Alphabet's 8.7%, Pinterest's 9.0% and Snap's 0.1%. In 2024 Meta's 21.9% led Pinterest's 19.3%, Alphabet's 13.9% and Snap's 16.4%. In 2025 Meta's 22.2% remained the highest, ahead of Pinterest's 15.8%, Alphabet's 15.1% and Snap's 10.6%. Alphabet's growth accelerated steadily from 8.7% to 15.1%, so the gap narrowed over the period but did not close. Snap's revenue was almost flat in 2023 and its growth slowed again in 2025. Snap is the only company in the group whose growth has not held a consistent upward path.

In absolute terms Alphabet remains the largest company by revenue (402,836 in 2025 against Meta's 200,966). Snap and Pinterest are a small fraction of either, at 5,931 and 4,222 in 2025.

## Gross Margin

Meta's gross margin rose from 78.3% in 2022 to 80.8% in 2023, 81.7% in 2024 and 82.0% in 2025. The gains were largest in the first year and slowed to 0.3 points in 2025, so the margin is near a plateau. Meta has the highest gross margin of the four in every year.

Pinterest's margin moved from 75.8% to 80.1%, rising every year, and it closed to within 1.9 points of Meta in 2025. Snap's margin fell from 60.6% in 2022 to 54.1% in 2023, held near 54% in 2024 and reached 55.0% in 2025. Its gross profit was 2,787 in 2022 and 3,262 in 2025, so Snap's gross profit grew more slowly than its revenue would suggest. Alphabet's margin rose from 55.4% to 59.7% over the four years, the most consistent improvement among the larger companies, but it remains about 22 points below Meta.

The resource does not break down cost of revenue, so it does not show why Snap's margin fell in 2023. The margin shift itself is in the data.

## EBITDA Margin

Meta's EBITDA margin rose from 32.3% in 2022 to 43.8% in 2023 and 52.8% in 2024, then edged down to 52.6% in 2025. EBITDA grew from 37,690 to 105,713 over the period. The 2025 result is the first year in which margin did not improve, and the decline is small, at 0.2 points.

Alphabet's margin rose each year, from 30.1% to 44.9%. In 2025 Meta's margin of 52.6% is about 7.7 points above Alphabet's. Pinterest moved from negative EBITDA in 2022 and 2023 (margins of -2.0% and -3.4%) to a positive 5.5% in 2024 and 8.2% in 2025. Snap's margin improved from -25.6% to -2.8% but remained negative in every year. For Pinterest and Snap the EBITDA figures are small (345 and -166 in 2025), so their margins move a long way on small changes in profit.

Meta is the most profitable of the four on this measure in 2025, and the gap to Alphabet widened from 2023 onward, when the two were closer together.

## EV/EBITDA

Meta's EV/EBITDA was 8.66 in 2022, rose to 15.27 in 2023 and 17.14 in 2024, then fell to 16.25 in 2025. Alphabet's multiple went from 13.4 to 17.8, then 17.07, then 21.1. In 2022 Meta traded at a lower multiple than Alphabet (8.66 against 13.4). In 2023 and 2024 the two were close (Alphabet 17.8 and 17.07, Meta 15.27 and 17.14). In 2025 Alphabet's multiple is about 4.9 turns higher than Meta's.

Pinterest's multiple was not meaningful in 2022 and 2023 because its EBITDA was negative. It was 92.75 in 2024 and 47.81 in 2025, which reflects a small EBITDA base. Snap's multiple is not meaningful in any year because EBITDA is negative throughout.

The resource gives no forward estimates, so it cannot show whether any of these multiples is high or low relative to expected earnings. On the reported figures, Meta's 2025 multiple is the lower of the two companies with meaningful multiples, and it has fallen in the most recent year while its EBITDA rose. Meta's EV/Sales multiple also declined from 9.05 in 2024 to 8.55 in 2025, while Alphabet's rose from 6.6 to 9.46. The resource does not explain the price movements behind these changes.

## FCF Conversion

Meta's FCF conversion was 0.83 in 2022, 1.13 in 2023, 0.87 in 2024 and 0.76 in 2025. Free cash flow grew from 19,289 in 2022 to 54,072 in 2024, then fell to 46,109 in 2025, while EBITDA rose. The result is that conversion fell in each of the last two years.

Alphabet's conversion declined steadily, from 1.0 in 2022 to 0.94, 0.73 and 0.55 in 2025. Alphabet's free cash flow rose modestly, from 60,010 to 73,266, so the falling ratio reflects EBITDA growing faster than cash flow. In 2025 Meta's 0.76 is above Alphabet's 0.55.

Snap's conversion is not meaningful in any year, because EBITDA is negative. Its free cash flow was small but positive, rising from 55 in 2022 to 437 in 2025. Pinterest's ratio was not meaningful in 2022 and 2023, 0.5 in 2024 and 3.0 in 2025. Its free cash flow rose from 440 to 1,252 over the period.

A note on method: the resource does not define FCF conversion. Simple ratios of the reported free cash flow to the reported EBITDA do not match the conversion figures. For example, Pinterest's 2024 free cash flow of 940 divided by EBITDA of 201 is about 4.7 (illustrative), not 0.5. This analysis uses the reported conversion figures as given and does not reconcile them.

## Return on Invested Capital

Meta's ROIC was 17.2% in 2022, 23.0% in 2023, 29.8% in 2024 and 22.2% in 2025. The 2025 decline of 7.6 points is the largest single-year move in the series. The resource does not split the change between invested capital and profit, so it cannot say which side moved.

Alphabet's ROIC rose from 22.4% to 25.1% to 29.9% in 2024, then edged down to 28.7% in 2025. In 2022 Meta's return was below Alphabet's (17.2% against 22.4%). Meta was ahead of Alphabet in 2024 and behind in 2025 (22.2% against 28.7%). Pinterest's ROIC moved from -2.3% to 6.3% over the period and Snap's from -16.2% to -4.2%. Both remained low or negative.

The 2025 result is the most important recent change for Meta. Its EBITDA margin held near its peak and its revenue growth accelerated, yet its return on invested capital fell. The resource does not explain this, and the analysis should not assume a cause.

## Conclusion

Meta's financial position on these metrics is strong. It has the fastest revenue growth of the four in 2025, the highest gross margin in every year, and the highest EBITDA margin in 2025. Its free cash flow is positive and large in absolute terms, at 46,109 in 2025. Its ROIC is well above zero and its EV/EBITDA multiple of 16.25 is below Alphabet's 21.1. Meta is not the same kind of company as Snap and Pinterest, whose margins and returns are much lower.

Three metrics weakened in the most recent year and are the points to watch. FCF conversion fell for a second year, from 0.87 to 0.76. ROIC fell from 29.8% to 22.2%. EBITDA margin was flat, with a decline of 0.2 points. None of these changes is large enough by itself to change the picture, but together they show that the return on each dollar of investment and the cash it produces have slipped even as revenue accelerated.

Taken together, the performance supports the view that Meta's 2025 multiple is not stretched relative to Alphabet, since Meta's growth and margins are both higher. The multiple alone does not establish that the stock is undervalued, because the resource has no forward estimates and does not show what the market expects. The valuation is consistent with a company that is still growing revenue quickly and expanding margins, but the 2025 slide in ROIC and cash conversion is the reason to test that view further.

The view would be wrong if revenue growth fell back below Alphabet's 15.1% level, if the ROIC decline continued into a further year, or if FCF conversion dropped further toward Alphabet's 0.55. Any of those would suggest the 2025 margin and growth strength was not converting into returns for shareholders, and would weaken the case that the current multiple is justified by the reported financials.
